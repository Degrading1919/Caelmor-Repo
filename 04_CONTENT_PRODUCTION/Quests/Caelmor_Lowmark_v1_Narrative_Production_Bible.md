## 1. Lowmark v1 in one page

The player comes down the Brookhollow road as an ordinary wanderer and is put to work at once: a crossing marker has been moved and the river is rising. Over twelve main quests the player discovers why the water of Lowmark has started going wrong all at once.

**The answer has three parts, and the player learns them in this order:**

1. **Someone is doing it on purpose.** A grain-trading house is running unlit barges at night down a sealed wartime channel, the **Relief Cut**, by releasing stored fen water in pulses ("flushes") from a sluice-house called the **Hollow Lock**. The flushes let them move stolen relief grain past Crestbridge without tolls or inspection. They moved crossing markers so the sudden rises would read "open" and nobody would close a crossing and walk the bank. (Act I)
2. **What they are using was already broken.** The Relief Cut was dug through an older Riverwarden flood-tuning network during the Great War. Each flush drags water through Stillwater Fen, where a wartime grain convoy sank, and stirs that grief-heavy water down the Vale. That is why the Wraiths ("drift") are spreading. Stopping the barges does not stop the drift. (Act II)
3. **Fixing it takes everyone.** Workers, keepers, crews, a farm ditch-turner, a frightened official, the hidden Last Field-Mage and the withdrawn Riverwardens each hold one piece. The player earns the trust that lets those pieces meet, exposes the house in public, and stands at the centre of the fen on the night it finally lets go. (Act III)

**Emotional line (Phase 1.1 §6):** Wonder (the water climbs the stone) → Competence (reading the river, delving the depot, catching the flush) → Attachment (Tovin believed, Joric's field, Nan's song) → Agency (choices that change crossings, fields, the fen) → Mastery (the player's earned standing opens Lowmark's master commissions).

**What makes it an RPG and not a hearing:** every quest has a place to explore, a physical problem to solve with the player's hands, danger that is readable, and a moment that is strange. Talk is short and always about something the player has done or is about to do. Records matter, but the player is the one who goes into the depot, the culvert, the lock and the fen to find them.

## 2. Author truth [PROPOSAL — DIRECTOR]

This section is the private truth behind the arc. Players learn it gradually and never all at once from one source. It keeps the three causes D05 requires: **a current human decision**, **older damaged infrastructure**, and **bounded resonance shaped by living grief**.

### 2.1 The old works

- **The Riverwarden Braids [NEW].** Long before the war, the Riverwardens tuned Lowmark's floods by splitting pressure across many small, carved channels and weirs ("the Braids"), calibrated by carved **Marking Stones** at crossings (the Flood Calendar Stones of dossier §XI). Crossing crews have seated posts on those stones for generations without knowing why. This is why the water climbed the stone in The True Line: where a Braid still works, restoring its mark lets water "hold a line" (dossier §XV language).
- **The Relief Cut and the Hollow Lock [NEW].** During the Great War, engineers working for the supply effort (dossier §II: dwarven engineers fortified mills and bridges) cut a straight channel from Stillwater Fen's deep pools to the river below Crestbridge, severing several Braids. A sluice-house, the **Hollow Lock**, held fen water back and released it in pulses so heavy grain barges could run fast to the fronts. After the armistice the Lock was chained and the Cut left to silt.
- **The Fourteenth Lot [NEW].** Late in the war a flush was released while a grain convoy was still in the fen. The convoy sank in Stillwater with its crews. The loss was hushed up. Bramblelock Weir's numbered convoy stones are missing that lot's entry (Part 3 proposal), and Corvin Tath's burned ledger (canon) is one of the records that would have shown it. No v1 content states who gave the order. Corvin's ledger stays unrecoverable.

### 2.2 The present wrong

- **House Marrick [NEW]** is a lesser grain-holding family with its own claim that it "kept Lowmark alive" during the war (dossier §II political tension). **Provisioner Edda Marrick** sits on the Crestbridge **Provision Board**, which can declare a relief emergency and order grain released from the Fenmere granaries at the fixed relief price.
- Marrick's scheme: delay deliveries until a far-bank shortage is real, declare an emergency, draw relief grain, send a little on by road (Wynn Tarrow's cart) and most of it down the reopened Relief Cut at night to an outside buyer who pays priority rates. The Board's books show it all as "delivered." The shortages and the spreading drift also make House Fenmere's stewardship look like it is failing, which strengthens Marrick's claim at the next stewardship hearing.
- **Gideon Sallow [NEW]** is Marrick's river factor. He runs the Hollow Lock crew, recruited partly through the Brookhollow smugglers' cellar (canon black-market hook). His crew moved the Whisperflow post.
- **Keeper-Captain Bram Hesketh [NEW]** is the frightened official. He signed blanket emergency orders because the Board told him the far-bank villages would riot without grain. He does not know about the Cut. He is the Main Arc's "frightened official", not a villain.

### 2.3 The resonance

- **How every player learns of the Fourteenth Lot before the boss,** through main quests only and never from a manifestation: Bramblelock's numbered convoy stones with the missing lot (a required inspect in MQ4), the flow-table's wreck scar in MQ9, and convoy lore spoken at the start of MQ11.
- Stillwater Fen holds the war's grief: wreckage, the Fourteenth Lot, decades of people mourning at its edge. Water there is "heavy" (Wraithwatcher jargon).
- Each flush drags heavy water out of the deep pools and down the Cut and the river, so drift gathers in new places: orchard pools, mill ponds, ditches, bends. Manifestations are imprints, not souls. They repeat, they react to conditions, and they never identify anyone (evidence rule; C08).
- **The backwater [PROPOSAL — DIRECTOR].** The flushes also emptied the fen's deep pools faster than the old works could refill them. Once MQ4 closes the Lock, the fen's pressure pushes back through the wartime works on its own: through the Lock's silted **slack gate** (the first visible sign, seen in MQ4), under the Rebirth Fields along the guide-line (MQ5), and into the orchard dikes (MQ7). Sallow's crew still runs barges after MQ4, but only through Drowsebank's mill race and a wartime spur to the Cut, which they had used for weeks as an overflow run (MQ6). In MQ10's plan the crews plank the Cut-side culverts, closing the backwater's last outlets.
- When the Hollow Lock and its culverts are finally sealed for good, the pressure has nowhere to go. The fen "holds its breath" until it releases on its own in the **Hollowed Current** (canon high-tier event). The grief around the sunken convoy has coalesced into a **Wraith Lord**, called **the Muster** by Watchers because of the lantern-lines and stroke-calls it repeats.

### 2.5 What defeating a Wraith means [PROPOSAL — DIRECTOR]

**Recommendation:** Combat **disperses** a manifestation. The water "lets go" and the drift sinks back. It re-forms while the conditions that made it remain (trapped water, disturbed flow, unattended grief). **Changing the conditions** (clearing a sluice, closing the Lock, tending a remembrance pool, reopening a Braid) **quiets** the site.

Why: it fits dossier §XVI.1 ("some grief cannot just be killed — it must be faced"), keeps combat protective rather than evidentiary, and gives the systems a clean rule: every drift site has a state (**active → quieted**, or **tended** after Act III) that quests change and that drives encounter spawning. Players feel their work change the map.

### 2.6 The Last Field-Mage [PROPOSAL — DIRECTOR]

**Recommendation:** The Field-Mage lives in plain sight as **Sedge**, an old reed-cutter who works the Path of Unburnt Reeds near Ashenstead. Players meet Sedge in Act II as an ordinary NPC (seeded in main quest 5 and two side quests) before learning who he is in main quest 8. "Sedge" is a working nickname; his given name is never spoken in v1. The dossier's rumor calls the mantle "his cloak," and this proposal follows it.

Characterization: precise, tired, kind in small ways and blunt in large ones. He has spent his life quieting water at night so nobody would have to know he exists. He refuses to train, refuses to fight Wraiths for anyone, and refuses to rule. He knows combat-scale water practice and shows the player once, in main quest 8, why force spreads heavy water instead of ending it.

### 2.7 Riverwarden contact [PROPOSAL — DIRECTOR]

**Recommendation:** v1 reaches only the **Listening Steps**, the outer carved channels behind Veilwater Falls, and one Riverwarden, **Oriel** [NEW]. The inner oasis and Warden's Hearth stay unseen and unmarked (dossier §V.5, §VI). Oriel offers the Braid gate knowledge to Lowmark's workers on one condition: the Vale must agree in public to tend the Braids together, with no one house or office owning them. That condition is why main quest 10 is a public plan. The Riverwardens do not return to rule (Main Arc movement 12).

### 6.1 Main quest briefs

These are binding briefs for the act packages. The act packages author the beats, steps, flags, encounters, dialogue and integration notes.

**MQ2 Short Measure.** Lead-in varies by the True Line evidence choice. Corvin's duplicate tallies say 120 sacks were drawn under the Board's emergency order; Wynn's cart carried 40. Corvin thinks the granary is being robbed. The player descends from the granary undercroft into the Hill Depot, where Field Goblins have broken through and are hauling sacks into their warren (the plausible wrong reading: goblins took it). The warren is Lowmark's first proper dungeon: low tunnels, goblin traps made of farm tools, an elite goblin "Sack-King" (Field Goblin elite). Goblin heaps hold about a dozen sacks. Past the warren, the depot's old river chute has fresh rope burns, new tallow on the pulley and a dropped requisition slip with a Board seal: humans lowered the rest to the river at night. The depot also holds wartime stencils reading RELIEF CUT and a salvaged Marking Stone mortared into a wall. Corvin and Aldric cannot accuse the Board on a slip. Hesketh is glimpsed signing for the next draw; Marrick is glimpsed thanking him `[ANTAGONIST]`. Choice for the remaining grain: send it by road under keeper escort to named households (slow, safe), hold it until the Board explains (honest, hungry far bank), or split it. The far-bank households in MQ12 remember which.

**MQ3 Heavy Water.** Ansel Corrie (from the True Line camp) asks for help on three sites the Watchers cannot cover. Any order. (a) **Orchards** with Bryn Ruddock: a blocked dike sluice traps flood water among roots; at dusk drift forms in the pool. The first Wraith fight. Ansel teaches that the drift will come back unless the water moves; the player clears the sluice (Woodcutting on a jammed driftroot, then a channelled lever) and the pool drains: the first quieted site. (b) **Ashen Flats ditch:** livestock refuse to drink. Ash Hounds denning in a burned culvert are the ordinary cause; but the ditch also runs backwards for a few minutes at the same hour each wrong night. (c) **Widow's Step** with Lessa Mirepath: a fen-cutter has lost a night and stands knee-deep in heavy water. Bring him out under drift attack using banner-thistle smoke (a carried Watcher tool, not magic); Elspeth Quill treats him. Synthesis at the Lantern Post: Ansel's book shows all three events on the same nights and hours, and they match the night the Whisperflow post was moved. Ordinary causes explain part of each; one shared pattern survives. That is the evidence rule taught by play.

**MQ4 The Night Flush.** Ansel's book predicts the next wrong night. The player keeps watch at Bramblelock Weir (a "keep watch" interaction that advances the hour). The river sound drops, then the surge: a pulse of water lifts the weir pool and an unlit barge rides it past. The player follows on foot along the towpath and weir walls (agility triggers, a timed run that cannot be failed, only lost and re-found by tracking the wake and dropped grain). The barge turns into the Relief Cut. Up the Cut, the Hollow Lock: Sallow's crew, lanterns hooded. Assault in two parts (yard, then the winding floor). Inside: greased winding gear, a stock of new yellow marker posts with notches pre-cut a hand high (D-2), a bill of lading under Board seal to an outside buyer. The player closes the Lock under attack (channelled crank, waves). Sallow escapes by boat, named by a crewman. Choice: smash the winding gear (the flushes stop here for good, but the mechanism evidence is gone and Sallow must find another way) or chain it and leave it as evidence (the Keepers can see it; Sallow's crew later cuts the chain). Either way, at dawn Ansel reports Stillwater's fog thicker than ever: the last flush stirred the fen. Act I turn.

**MQ5 The Guide-Line.** In Ashenstead the Ember-Well runs strange and the Rebirth Fields flood from below on flush nights (the Cut's backwater is seeping through something under the fields). Joric knows where the old boundary stones are; Annelise knows the remembrance circle stands where the water rises first. The player traces dips and wet lines across the field, digs at three points, and opens a buried, stone-lined wartime channel: the land was used for emergency care and supply (Main Arc movement 5), and the channel carried water around a treatment boundary. Inside the culvert: a flooded crawl, River Skitters, a Reed Stalker ambush, wartime boundary markers, and a choked iron grate where the water backs up. No imprint identifies anyone; a few scratched marks are names the player cannot read. Choice of fix: clear the grate from beneath the circle (Annelise must agree to lift the circle stones), cut a new relief ditch through Joric's unplanted plot (Joric must agree; ties to *Unreplanted*), or build a slower dike around both (costs extra labour, delays the fields a season). Sedge is seen cutting reeds at the field edge and offers one sentence about where the water wants to go. Players may not notice him. That is fine.

**MQ6 Tovin's Floor.** Tovin's employer, miller Imber Cobb, is ready to let him go: tools moved, wet floors, "seeing things." The player fixes the demonstrable hazard first: the cracked mill-race gate (salvage timber, Smithing or crew repair at entry level, carry and seat). Night watch in the mill reveals the ordinary cause of the moved tools: Sallow's crew is using the mill race as a second route since the Lock (either outcome of MQ4). Tovin's sketches of the morning wet-lines, laid in order on the mill floor, form a calendar of flushes. It predicts the next one: tonight. Set piece: hold the race gate shut while the pulse hits and the crew tries to force it, with drift forming in the race as the water churns. What stays unexplained: the mill-pond reflections lag. Tovin never names it. Choice: credit Tovin's sketches in the Keepers' record by name (he becomes a witness, which scares him) or submit them unsigned (safer; less weight in MQ10).

**MQ7 What the Ditch Knows.** With the calendar, everyone knows when the next big pulse comes, and it will top the orchard dikes. Bryn Ruddock can hold a dike section using household water practice if she has a riverglass bowl (Ottilie Penner), fresh softmoss (gathered at shrine stones; it rots faster near drift, canon cue) and driftroot stakes (Woodcutting). A charter officer insists a dike this size falls under Fenmere charter oversight (the C09 layered model's threshold question). Set piece: the player defends Bryn through the pulse (drift, Mud Pipers mimicking her voice to draw her off, a flood-driven Bog-Touched Elk). If enemies reach her, the hold slips and one orchard section floods. No quest fail. The practice is shown plainly: water folds aside around the bowl like cloth smoothed by a hand. It saves the orchard; it cannot fix the fen. Choice: enrol Bryn on the commons roll under an experienced teacher (lawful, watched) or vouch to the council that this was household practice below the charter line (free, exposed).

**MQ8 The Mantle.** Ansel's book and the player's own visits show sites quieted overnight with no repair: fresh-pressed softmoss, water settled, mist lingering at dawn. The trail leads to the Mantle Shrine and Sedge. Sedge does not admit anything. He sets three tests of judgment: walk a panicked Bog-Touched Elk out of heavy water without killing it (positioning against its alert radius; killing it is possible and ends the test with a cooler Sedge, not a failure); read three Marking Stones on a fen channel and say which reads true (the True Line skill, extended); and answer what the player has seen (dialogue where interpretive claims are gently corrected and observations are accepted). Then he shows the player why force fails: the player disperses a drift with a weapon and it splits into two smaller drifts downstream; Sedge quiets the pool by hand, slowly, and is exhausted. He confirms nothing about himself out loud. He agrees to take the player to someone who knows how the water used to be kept.

**MQ9 Behind the Falls.** A pilgrimage, combat-light by design. Climb the falls path (agility triggers, slick stone, spray), pass behind the curtain, and enter the Listening Steps: carved channels, moss, a stillness that is not a dead spot. The wonder set piece is the **flow-table**, a carved basin where water runs the Vale's real channels in miniature. The player can find Whisperflow, the orchards, the fen, and see the Relief Cut as a raw straight scar through the braided lines. It shows pressure, not names. Oriel speaks with the player and Sedge. The Braids can spread the fen's pressure if their gates are opened in the right order during a pulse and the Cut is sealed for good. The Riverwardens withdrew because rulers kept asking for floods on command. Oriel's condition: the Vale must agree in public to tend the Braids together. The player's promise (personal; three phrasings) is quoted back in MQ12.

**MQ10 The Crest Hall Reckoning.** Aldric convenes a public plan at Crest Hall: routes, food, memorial ground, and the Braids. The night before, Sallow's crew tries to burn the Keepers' record room and sink the evidence barge at Crest Hall's water-gate. Night siege through the hall's undercroft and water-gate: fire-lines to cut, records to carry out, crew to fight, and the miniboss **Gideon Sallow** (boat-hook sweeps, lantern-rope cuts that drop the lights, a shove toward the water; readable and fair). He yields; he is not killed. The hearing is short and physical: the player lays evidence on the long table, each item a chip earned in play (True Line proof per choice, Corvin's tallies, the depot slip, the Hollow Lock bill and posts, Tovin's calendar, Rhosyn's forged papers from *Under the Lamb*, Sallow's own account if captured). Any three valid items expose Marrick `[ANTAGONIST]`; more items give better terms. Choices: who closes routes, who tends the fen's memorial ground, and whether the player speaks for Hesketh. The Braid plan passes with named roles for crews, keepers, Watchers, farm workers and Flow-Menders, and no new government (Main Arc movement 10).

**MQ11 Stillwater's Vigil.** With the Cut sealed, the fen holds its breath and lets go: the Hollowed Current. Reedmere at dusk; Maelin's crews at three Braid gates; Watchers with lanterns; Elspeth with the evacuees; Sedge at the centre. Phase 1: escort fen-cutters out through drift-choked paths; trance-struck villagers stop and turn toward the water and must be brought back with banner-thistle smoke. Phase 2: open the three Braid gates in Oriel's order; each gate is a held channel under attack (Reed Stalkers, drift, a Stillwater Serpent breaching the second gate). Phase 3: **the Muster**, the Wraith Lord coalesced over the Fourteenth Lot, rises as pressure peaks. Boss language: lantern-lines (lanes of cold water telegraphed by lantern light), stroke-calls (pulses on a readable count), the drag (a pull toward deep water broken by stepping onto a marked stone, the True Line skill one last time). Defeat disperses it; Sedge spreads the pressure through the Braids; every Marking Stone in the fen holds a line at once and the fen exhales. Choice for Stillwater (Main Arc movement 11; dossier §XVI.5): **Seal** (close the deep fen for a generation; safest, harshest), **Tend** (a guided remembrance channel at the fen edge, Watchers and Annelise tend it; occasional quiet drift remains), or **Shared** (seal the deep pools, tend the edge).

**MQ12 The River Remains.** A walk through the changed Vale: the far-bank households (their memory of MQ2's choice), Nan's own bed, Wynn paid, Tovin's sketch pinned in the Keepers' hall, Bryn's orchard, Joric's field, Ansel's new patrol book, Hesketh's fate, Marrick's house diminished. Sedge refuses to train the player and says why. Oriel's sign of trust is non-mechanical: the player's mark is cut beside the crews' marks on the Whisperflow Marking Stone (Main Arc movement 12: a sign, not power). The final beat: Maelin, who never crosses at night, takes the player across Whisperflow at night by lantern because the line now reads true. `LM_CIVIC_STANDING` is set, which is the narrative legitimacy the four aspirational commissions require (`Caelmor_Aspirational_Equipment_Foundation.md`, acquisition model step 1).

### 9.2 Drift-site state (D-6)

Each authored drift site has a site id and state. **Active:** drift pocket spawns on its conditions (dusk, after rain, flush nights before MQ4). **Quieted:** no spawn. **Tended** (only after MQ11 Tend/Shared): rare passive drift that does not engage unless the player attacks it; Watchers patrol. **Closed** (MQ11 Seal): the deep-fen area is blocked by a Keeper boundary; outside it, heavy water is gone. Sites: orchard pool (MQ3), ditch (MQ3), Widow's Step (MQ3, MQ11), Bramblelock pool (MQ4), Drowsebank race (MQ6), orchard dike (MQ7), Mantle channel (MQ8), Stillwater deep pools (MQ11), farmhouse cellar (WD), watergarden outlet (CN), Old Lantern's bend (OL). After MQ11, a small set of heavy-water sites remains active by design so Wraith combat stays available for progression. The canonical drift-site register (18 site ids, conditions, owners and post-MQ11 states per ending) is in `04_CONTENT_PRODUCTION/Enemies/Caelmor_Lowmark_Encounter_and_Enemy_Requirements.md`; completing *Old Lantern* quiets its bend permanently, and the Dunnock farm cellar (*The Whispering Door*) stays as the player left it.

## 11. Authoring conventions (binding for every package)

1. **Format.** Quest packages follow `Caelmor_Lowmark_Quest_The_True_Line.md`: summary card, places, beats with objective text, encounters, discovery spec where relevant, steps table, flags table, choices and consequences, rewards, failure/recovery/co-op, integration needs, mystery-ledger entries, open decisions. Dialogue follows the cast file's node format and voice rules.
2. **Ids.** Quest content ids: `lowmark.main.NN_slug` and `lowmark.side.slug` (suggested; the Schema & Content Engineer owns naming). Steps: `S00_…`. Flags: `<CODE>_…` for quest-local, `LM_…` for region flags from §9 only. Dialogue nodes: `<CODE>-D<n>.<nn>`.
3. **Evidence rule.** Supernatural effects never identify a person, prove guilt, settle a claim or name the dead. Combat never reveals evidence. Records, objects, places and testimony do.
4. **Never soft-lock.** Every required proof has a guaranteed path. No main quest can fail. Choices have costs, not wrong answers.
5. **Rewards.** Access, knowledge, tools, small payments, standing. No lump quest XP. No new items beyond quest items unless the item pass defines them; reward tools are named functionally ("crew hatchet").
6. **Quest-item budget.** Phase 1.5 allows 8–12 quest items for all of v1. The True Line uses 3. Each act package may use at most 2 new quest items; side quests use carry-state objects or none.
7. **Tone.** Grounded, mythic, dry humor, understatement. At least one moment of wonder or unease per quest. No exposition dumps. No cutscenes; the camera stays with the player.
8. **Addressing the player.** Origin-neutral and address-neutral (D-3).
9. **Antagonist tagging.** Any node naming Marrick or Sallow is tagged `[ANTAGONIST]` (§3.1).
10. **Map.** Relative placement only; mark **MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED**.
11. **Status labels.** [CANON], [NEW], [PROPOSAL — DIRECTOR] as in the True Line.
