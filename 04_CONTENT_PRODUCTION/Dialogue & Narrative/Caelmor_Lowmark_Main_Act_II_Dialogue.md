### 1.3 How Sedge and Oriel speak

- **Sedge `[D-7]`:** very few words, all exact. He answers questions with questions about what the player *saw*. He never names himself, his practice or the Field-Mage. He never says "I can't tell you", because that confirms there is something to tell. He talks about reeds, thatch, knots, weather and water levels. Humor bone-dry and rare.
- **Oriel `[D-8]`:** formal, complete sentences, unhurried, no contractions. Water is **work**: it carries, scours, rests, bears weight, is gated, is tended by season. Oriel never says water feels, wants, remembers or grieves. Oriel doesn't know Lowmark's present houses and says so plainly. Calls the player "you" and Sedge nothing at all.

## Part 2 — Cast notes

### Joric Fen — field laborer and net-mender [CANON ANCHOR]
- **Canon:** his land was burned in the war and never replanted; works neighbours' fields; mends nets at dusk; soft-spoken; never complains; avoids standing water; lost family in a river incident never recorded; says the water "gives things back slowly."
- **In Act II:** MQ5. Knows where the boundary stones stand; recognizes the guide-line; decides about his plot.
- **Want:** to be left to decide about his own ground. **Fear:** standing water (shown by his feet, never explained).
- **Knows:** the stones; that ploughs went round them; his plot. **Doesn't know:** what the ground was used for, beyond "something." Anything about the scratched marks.
- **Voice:** short, low, practical. Long silences, written as stage notes. Never explains why he won't step on the wet ground.
- **Guard:** no age, service, kin name, incident detail, travel or meeting is added. He never connects the field to his own loss aloud.
- **Change:** he chooses (or allows others to choose) what his ground does, and stays himself.

### Annelise Reed — watergarden and memorial caretaker [CANON ANCHOR]
- **Canon:** tends quiet memorials; removes offerings before they rot; keeps the water clear; speaks softly to the water while working; dislikes unattended candles; knows which names never appear on public memorials; believes remembrance should be "light enough to carry."
- **In Act II:** MQ5. Knows the water rises first inside the remembrance circle. Gatekeeper of the circle option.
- **Want:** remembrance that helps the living keep living. **Fear:** remembrance that drowns them.
- **Knows:** the circle; which names never come to it (she says she knows, never which). **Doesn't know:** what the culvert marks are; who was treated on the field.
- **Voice:** gentle and deliberate. Talks to the water mid-conversation. Precise about objects. Firm without being loud.
- **Guard:** no name is ever added or confirmed by her. She won't put unreadable marks on a memorial.

### Tovin Pell — mill runner [CANON ANCHOR]
- **Canon:** see the True Line cast file. Notices lagging reflections, moved tools, water sounds without current; sketches mill floors; assumes something is wrong with him; talks only when asked directly; does not understand, name or seek answers.
- **In Act II:** MQ6. Hands over the sheets when asked directly. Runs for the keepers. Sees the pond lag and doesn't name it.
- **Voice:** apologetic, short, concrete. Describes; never explains. Hesitates before every admission.
- **Guard:** never solves the puzzle, never names the phenomenon, never asks what it was.

### Imber Cobb — Drowsebank miller [NEW]
- **Want:** the wheel turning. **Fear:** Tovin being right, which would mean something wrong with the mill that Imber can't fix.
- **Knows:** every board and pin of the mill; that the gate has been "going funny"; that Tovin is the steadiest worker he has, which makes the rest worse.
- **Voice:** gruff, short sentences, complaints as affection. Swears mildly at objects. Kinder than he admits, and never says so.
- **Change:** rubs out his own notice.

### Bryn Ruddock — orchard ditch-turner [NEW]
- **Want:** her trees kept. **Fear:** being called a mage.
- **Knows:** her ditches, her boundary stones, the hold her mother taught her. **Doesn't know:** why the bowl helps; anything about the fen beyond "it's coming down."
- **Voice:** brisk, profane under her breath (written as "—" or mild oaths), proud. Refuses to name what she does.
- **Change:** her work gets a place in the Vale's rules, whichever place the player picks.

### Ottilie Penner — glasswright, Brookhollow [NEW]
- **Want:** her glass used for bowls, not schemes. **Fear:** her riverglass ending up in a cellar.
- **Voice:** talks to the glass more than to the customer, calling it "she." Exact about flaws. Sells only to people who say what it's for.

### Elspeth Quill — Flow-Mender, Ashenstead [NEW]
- **Want:** fewer people walking into water. **Voice:** gentle, practical, never wastes softmoss. Treats the hold as tiring work, not wonder. Uses riverglass bowls in her own work and finds nothing strange in it.
- **Pronouns:** not stated by the bible; this file uses none.

### The charter officer — Fenmere charter office [NEW, role-based]
- **Role:** a clerk-surveyor who checks irrigation works against the old Fenmere charters. Unnamed.
- **Want:** works measured before they fail, not after. **Fear:** four flooded holdings and no line in the ledger.
- **Voice:** ledger voice, polite and literal. Says "practice" and "works." Honest about a measure even when it goes against them.
- **Pronouns:** they.

### Sedge — reed-cutter (the Last Field-Mage, D-7) [NEW] `[D-7]`
- **Want:** no one to need him. **Fear:** what force does to water.
- **Knows:** heavy water, how force spreads it, how the Braids once worked. **Doesn't know:** the names of the dead; the grain scheme.
- **Voice:** see §1.3. Given name never spoken in Lowmark content.
- **Change in Act II:** from a man who cuts reeds at a field edge to a man who lets one person watch him work.

### Oriel — Riverwarden of the Listening Steps (D-8) [NEW] `[D-8]`
- **Want:** the Braids tended by many hands. **Fear:** being needed by rulers again.
- **Knows:** the Braids, their gates and seasons; why the Riverwardens withdrew. **Doesn't know:** present Lowmark houses or politics.
- **Voice:** see §1.3. Pronouns: they.
