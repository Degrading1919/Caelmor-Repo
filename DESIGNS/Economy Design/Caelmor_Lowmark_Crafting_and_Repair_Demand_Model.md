# Caelmor — Lowmark Crafting and Repair Demand Model

**Status:** Proposal-stage material demand model
**Scope:** Reusable construction quantities, Milestone 1–4 consumption, and repair/refurbishment sinks; not final item recipes or durability rules

## 1. Demand principles

- The same approved materials serve all four milestones.
- Later work consumes somewhat more material where selection, fit, sectional protection, test work, or service access physically requires it; most progression still comes from workmanship.
- Exact weapon form, handedness, guard slot, armor breakdown, and loadout compatibility remain unresolved.
- A range is a planning envelope, not a locked recipe.
- Optional inputs appear only when the final form uses the corresponding component function.
- Workshop-only parts never become ingredient IDs.
- Aspirational commissions use the upper end of existing patterns and additional labor/inspection. They require no prestige substance.

## 2. Reusable quantity patterns

### 2.1 Smith-led construction

| Pattern | Plain steel | Charcoal | Leather | Linen / tow | Tallow | Intended use |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Compact hard work | 2–3 | 1–2 | 0–1 | 0–1 | 0–1 | Edge-bearing or compact controlled structure |
| Medium defensive work | 4–7 | 2–4 | 1–3 | 0–2 | 0–1 | Hand-controlled impact work with justified flexible control/damping |
| Heavy protective work | 8–14 | 4–7 | 2–4 | 2–5 total | 1–2 | Integrated hard protection with real fastening, lining, and damping needs |
| Major commission uplift | Add 1–2 primary stock and 1–2 process/support units to the relevant Milestone 3 pattern | — | — | — | — | Selection allowance, matched sections, fit trials, inspection, and planned service access |

The commission uplift is not a fifth construction class. It moves the appropriate compact, medium, or heavy pattern toward its upper end.

### 2.2 Leather-led construction

| Pattern | Finished leather | Linen | Tow | Tallow | Optional steel | Intended use |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Light flexible work | 2–3 | 1 | 0–1 | 1 | 0 | Dependable flexible structure, closure, and body-contact work |
| Medium fitted work | 4–5 | 1–2 | 1 | 1 | 0–1 | Reinforced fit, movement zones, and replaceable wear |
| Extensive specialist work | 6–8 | 2–3 | 1–2 | 1–2 | 0–1 | Zoned protection, matched behavior, lining, and repair access |
| Major commission uplift | Use the upper specialist range; add at most 1 compatible leather/textile unit when matching and fitting consume it | — | — | — | — | Individual patterning and inspection without a new material tier |

## 4. Leather preparation demand

| Batch scale | Raw hide | Bark | Leather output | Typical purpose |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Small batch | 2 | 1 | 2 | One light construction input or several small repairs |
| Working batch | 4 | 2 | 4 | Medium flexible work or mixed Smith-led support |
| Workshop batch | 8 | 4 | 8 | Extensive specialist work, commission preparation, or repair reserve |

Batch scale changes convenience and planning, not conversion efficiency or material quality.

## 5. Repair and refurbishment consumption

Repair uses the represented materials directly. There are no repair kits, component durability bars, or disposable maintenance tokens.

| Repair pattern | Provisional input | Appropriate use |
|---|---|---|
| Routine flexible care | 1 tallow | Bounded conditioning or thread/surface care after meaningful use; never structural restoration |
| Minor flexible repair | 1 leather **or** 1 linen/tow; 0–1 tallow | Patch, seam, lining, padding, fastening, or small wear replacement |
| Major flexible repair | 2–4 leather; 1–2 linen/tow; 1 tallow | Torn/stretched structure, sectional replacement, relining, repadding, or major refit |
| Minor hard repair | 1 steel; 1 charcoal; optional 0–1 flexible input | Hard wear point, safe correction, small junction or control repair |
| Major hard refurbishment | 2–4 steel; 2–3 charcoal; optional 1–2 flexible inputs and 1 tallow | Lost alignment, distorted protection, failed junction, rebuilt section |
| Heavy sectional refurbishment | 3–5 steel; 3–4 charcoal; 1–3 leather/linen/tow; 1 tallow | Substantial Harness section while preserving sound fitted sections |
| Commission-preserving work | The applicable pattern at its upper end plus accountable workshop labor | Restores verified behavior and fit; provenance guides but is not consumed |

### Long-run repair planning envelope

This is an economy stress assumption, not a durability timer or mandatory schedule.

| Actively used equipment identity | Average represented-material demand per 10 engaged combat hours | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Keeper’s Blade | 0.5–1.5 steel; 0.5–2 charcoal; 0–1 flexible input; 0–1 tallow | Routine care plus occasional hard correction |
| Crestbridge Guard | 1–2 steel; 1–2 charcoal; 1–2 leather/linen/tow; 0–1 tallow | Control/damping wear plus occasional face/junction repair |
| Bridgewarden Harness | 2–4 steel; 2–4 charcoal; 1–3 flexible/textile inputs; 1 tallow | Higher sustained protection and sectional maintenance demand |
| Riverpath Leathers | 2–4 leather; 1–2 linen; 1–2 tow; 1–2 tallow | Patch, lining, padding, closure, conditioning, and refit demand |

Repair events should follow meaningful damage, wear, field conditions, and player choice. They must not fire on a clock simply to consume stock. Routine care extends service intervals but cannot erase structural failure.

## 6. Construction and repair sink strength

| Material | Construction demand | Repair demand | Sink finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steel stock | 2–16 per representative equipment path/milestone | 1–5 per repair event | Strong, persistent, import-dependent |
| Vegetable-tanned leather | 1–8 across flexible/mixed construction | 1–4 per repair | Strong across all paths where physically justified |
| Linen textile | 0–3 per construction | 1–2 per lining/joining repair | Broad recurring demand |
| Flax tow | 0–2 per construction | 1–2 per damping/padding repair | Narrower demand; surplus watch if final forms use little damping |
| Charcoal | 1–8 per Smith-led construction | 1–4 per hard refurbishment | Strong heat sink without entering finished equipment |
| Tallow | 0–2 per construction/finish | Usually 1 per bounded care/repair | Small but recurring cross-path sink |
| Bark | Consumed upstream at 1 per 2 leather | Only through new leather or justified retanning | Strong only while hide/leather demand exists |
| Raw hide | Consumed upstream one-for-one into leather | No direct repair use | High supply converts into stable leather rather than repair stock |

## 7. Self-supply and buying implications

- A Hunting-focused player can supply hide quickly but still needs bark, textile, tallow processing, or market access.
- A Woodcutting-focused player can supply wood/bark and indirect charcoal but cannot create imported steel or animal inputs.
- A Smithing-focused player deliberately buys steel and often charcoal; Woodcutting is optional.
- A Leatherworking-focused player may Hunt and Woodcut, buy one input, buy both, or purchase finished ordinary leather.
- A mostly self-supplying player still encounters imported steel and ordinary textile/renderer labor, preserving the lived economy.
- A market buyer pays spreads and service costs but saves gathering and processing time.

No core skill is forced into another merely to make the dependency graph look dense.

## 8. Repair annoyance controls

1. no per-component durability simulation;
2. no repair after every outing;
3. routine care delays rather than resets structural wear;
4. field care consumes little or no structural material;
5. major repair follows meaningful use or damage and restores an attached item rather than replacing it;
6. commissioned equipment uses ordinary materials but may need its accountable workshop or fitting reference;
7. a player may pay for service instead of training both primary crafts;
8. damaged primary structure cannot be fixed with tallow alone.

The result creates recurring demand without converting equipment ownership into a chore loop.
