# Caelmor — Major Equipment Milestones from Aspirational Destinations

**Status:** proposal-stage backward progression design based on the Creative Director-approved aspirational portfolio. This is not a level-by-level ladder and does not define materials, recipes, components, nodes, exact statistics, drop tables, schemas, or final item names.

## Purpose

The four aspirational destinations require a small number of earlier equipment experiences that teach the player what the final equipment means. A milestone exists only when it changes player understanding, equipment identity, crafting expectation, or combat behavior. A numerical replacement with the same purpose is not a major milestone.

## Progression constraints

- Approved progression identities are Novice 1–30, Competent 31–50, Advanced 51–70, Masterful 71–98, practical mastery around 80, and Cap 99.
- Exact unlock levels remain open and should be chosen only after combat and crafting requirements are defined.
- The aspirational commissions should become achievable around practical mastery rather than being held until level 99.
- Post-80 equipment progression should emphasize specialist options, alternate preparations, identity, and commemoration instead of mandatory replacement tiers.
- Lowmark remains the only full v1 region. V1 has no player spellcasting equipment.
- Ranged milestones and ranged BIS remain deferred under S01.

## Minimum milestone architecture

The minimum coherent structure is **three major teaching milestones followed by the aspirational destination**. Starter equipment exists before these beats, but it is tutorial context rather than a major authored milestone.

### Milestone 1 — Dependable field equipment

**Progression identity:** late Novice direction, with exact placement open.

**Purpose:** Move the player from “anything usable” into equipment that behaves predictably and can be maintained. The player learns that weapon rhythm, active defense, and protection choice matter more than nominal improvement alone.

**Required teaching outcomes:**

- A basic melee weapon teaches commitment and recovery.
- A basic guard-capable setup introduces deliberate defending and the fact that mitigation is not guaranteed.
- Basic heavy protection demonstrates direct protection and positional commitment.
- Basic light protection demonstrates spacing and flexibility with lower tolerance for repeated hits.
- Repair and upkeep become visible expectations, without yet creating a detailed durability economy.

**Crafting expectation:** The player encounters Smithing and Leatherworking as sources of dependable function, not factories for disposable level increments. Equipment may be obtained through ordinary making, repair, or local exchange; exact acquisition remains open.

**Why this milestone is necessary:** Without it, later equipment would ask the player to choose identities before the combat language is understood.

### Milestone 2 — Declared working identity

**Progression identity:** Competent direction, with exact placement open.

**Purpose:** Ask the player to choose how equipment solves danger. The choices become behaviorally different and stop being simple heavy-versus-light number comparisons.

| Aspirational path | Working identity taught at this milestone | Required tradeoff lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Keeper's Blade | Reliable, balanced melee rhythm for mixed encounters. | Consistency does not provide maximum reach, defense, or concentrated offense. |
| Crestbridge Guard | Active-defense setup built around telegraph recognition and defensive commitment. | Safety depends on timing and reduces offensive flexibility. |
| Bridgewarden Harness | Heavy protection intended to withstand direct pressure during longer engagements. | Protection does not solve positioning, reach, or environmental adaptation. |
| Riverpath Leathers | Light protection intended for spacing, movement freedom, and prepared expeditions. | Flexibility does not forgive repeated heavy impacts. |

**Crafting expectation:** The player chooses a primary craft relationship and learns that fitting, maintenance, and intended use matter. The milestone should not require mastery of every craft or introduce four parallel catalogs.

**Why this milestone is necessary:** It establishes that future equipment is chosen for function. Removing it would make the final commissions feel like arbitrary upgrades.

### Milestone 3 — Proven specialist equipment

**Progression identity:** Advanced direction, with exact placement open.

**Purpose:** Confirm the chosen identity under more demanding encounters and introduce the preparation expected by an aspirational commission.

**Required teaching outcomes:**

- Equipment strengths must be used deliberately rather than passively assumed.
- Weaknesses remain relevant and sometimes require a different setup.
- Maintenance, fitting, and provenance become part of trust in the equipment.
- More than one discipline or source relationship may contribute to a finished piece, but no recipe structure is selected yet.
- Field use, local service, or demonstrated judgment matters alongside skill progression.

| Aspirational path | Specialist proof before commissioning |
|---|---|
| Keeper's Blade | Demonstrate reliable attack and defense decisions across varied encounters without relying on a single burst pattern. |
| Crestbridge Guard | Demonstrate correct defensive commitment against readable pressure and recovery after the defended sequence. |
| Bridgewarden Harness | Demonstrate sustained survival while continuing to act, maintain position, and manage the limits of direct mitigation. |
| Riverpath Leathers | Demonstrate spacing, preparation, and route or encounter knowledge while accepting lower direct protection. |

**Crafting expectation:** Advanced equipment should require intentional coordination between primary craft, maintenance, fitting, and world access. “Higher tier” alone is insufficient. Exact disciplines, components, and sources remain later decisions.

**Why this milestone is necessary:** It proves that the player understands the equipment identity before story legitimacy and high mastery make the final commission available.

### Milestone 4 — Aspirational commission

**Progression identity:** Masterful direction near practical mastery, with exact placement open.

The player reaches one or more approved destinations through earned commission plus player mastery:

- Keeper's Blade;
- Crestbridge Guard;
- Bridgewarden Harness;
- Riverpath Leathers.

The commission combines civic or narrative legitimacy, demonstrated combat understanding, meaningful player craft contribution, and skilled fitting or witness. It does not require a random rare drop, a supernatural resource, another player, or level 99.

The endpoint rewards mastery of an already learned identity. It should deepen attachment and reliability without invalidating the other three destinations.

## Path-by-path backward map

| Destination | Milestone 1 | Milestone 2 | Milestone 3 | Aspirational payoff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Keeper's Blade | Learn readable melee commitment with dependable basic equipment. | Choose consistency and balanced control as the working identity. | Prove judgment across varied encounter patterns and maintain the weapon responsibly. | A trusted generalist weapon whose value comes from reliability, mastery, and service. |
| Crestbridge Guard | Learn that defending is an active commitment with bounded mitigation. | Choose telegraph reading and guard timing as the working identity. | Prove defensive judgment under sustained pressure without treating defense as invulnerability. | A defensive implement that makes composure and protection of passage the player's specialization. |
| Bridgewarden Harness | Learn the difference between direct protection and avoiding danger. | Choose sustained heavy protection and positional commitment. | Prove that mitigation can support continued action but cannot replace encounter knowledge. | Heavy protection associated with public duty, endurance, and repeated maintenance. |
| Riverpath Leathers | Learn spacing and flexibility while experiencing the cost of lower protection. | Choose light protection and prepared movement as the working identity. | Prove route, hazard, and encounter knowledge while maintaining vulnerable equipment. | Light protection that rewards preparation and the learned use of Lowmark's terrain. |

## Why three teaching milestones are sufficient

1. **Dependability** teaches the common combat language.
2. **Declared identity** creates a meaningful choice.
3. **Proven specialization** tests that choice and introduces interconnected preparation.
4. **Aspirational commission** rewards mastery and attachment.

Adding another universal equipment tier between these beats would repeat an existing lesson. Individual levels may still provide progress, access, efficiency, or minor improvements without receiving a new major equipment family.

## Acquisition and solo-scope implications

- A player may pursue the four commissions in an order shaped by preferred playstyle and skills; exact prerequisites remain open.
- Core completion must remain achievable solo.
- Commission progress may involve Lowmark story, civic work, combat proof, craft mastery, and preparation, but should reuse a shared framework instead of requiring four unrelated capstone quest structures.
- Earlier equipment remains useful for alternate identities, replacement, practice, or different encounter demands. Aspirational equipment should not erase the economy beneath it.
- Craft contribution must feel substantial without requiring every player to master every possible discipline before receiving any aspirational equipment.

## Deliberately unresolved

- Exact levels within the approved progression bands.
- Exact item names before the four provisional destination names are finalized.
- Exact weapon shape, armor-piece breakdown, shield or guard slot, and compatible loadouts.
- Exact attack profiles, defense profiles, abilities, cooldowns, mitigation, movement effects, statistics, and balance.
- Exact acquisition quests, civic actors, service proofs, crafting disciplines, and commission procedures.
- Durability, repair cost, binding, trading, loss, replacement, and duplicate-commission rules.
- Materials, components, recipes, stations, gathering actions, world nodes, drops, and resource volumes.
- All ranged milestones and ranged aspirational equipment under S01.

## Progression implications

- Major equipment beats align with changes in player understanding, not every numerical level.
- The same early combat language supports all four destinations, reducing tutorial and asset duplication.
- Competent progression creates identity; Advanced progression tests and interconnects it; Masterful progression rewards it.
- Practical mastery can deliver aspirational equipment while level 99 retains its approved role as a completion and identity landmark.
- Heavy and light protection remain parallel destinations, while active defense and balanced offense remain distinct combat identities.

## Exact next handoff

Work backward from these milestones into **recipe and component functions**: identify what each stage must assemble or prove, without naming materials yet. Only after those functions are coherent should the minimum material set be selected.
