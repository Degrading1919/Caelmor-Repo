# Caelmor — Lowmark Economy Stress-Test Report

**Status:** proposal-stage adversarial report. This document tests the Lowmark gathering and supply architecture qualitatively before node or content production. It introduces no quantities, prices, rates, XP, action times, schemas, map placements, final species, or new materials. Findings are provisional until Creative Director audit.

## 1. Test basis and health criteria

The tested economy uses:

- player Hunting for eligible game, edible-output relationships, conditional guaranteed raw hide, and routed rendering feedstock;
- player Woodcutting for ordinary wood relationships, conditional guaranteed bark co-output, and optional fuel-worker supply;
- ordinary household, textile, husbandry, tannery, fuel-worker, renderer, carrier, market, workshop, and storage relationships;
- imported conventional steel stock;
- the approved nine equipment-chain inventory states and four player-facing recipe families;
- increasing workmanship, fitting, integration, repairability, access, and provenance rather than material-tier replacement.

A scenario is healthy when it preserves:

1. a viable path for the tested play preference;
2. a reason for relevant player skills and ordinary workers to coexist;
3. a complete source-to-sink relationship;
4. understandable scarcity without a mandatory chore;
5. stewardship and lore credibility;
6. manageable solo-development scope; and
7. useful play beyond XP or first equipment completion.

Ratings:

- **STRONG:** architecture already contains a bounded answer.
- **PARTIAL:** architecture is sound but later content or tuning must enforce a stated boundary.
- **THIN:** a relationship lacks enough design to proceed to node/content work.
- **MISSING:** a required relationship has no answer.
- **CONTRADICTORY:** the answer conflicts with approved design or lore.

## 2. Scenario results

### Scenario A — The player never Hunts

**Supply behavior:** husbandry and ordinary animal processing continue to place some raw hide into markets and tanning flows. Ordinary renderers receive their stable animal-processing context. The player can buy raw hide or finished leather, use Leatherworking on purchased hide, commission or buy equipment through approved paths, and obtain food through later non-Hunting relationships.

**Pressure revealed:** ordinary hide access must be sufficient for a viable, non-Hunting equipment path without being so convenient that direct recovery has no value.

**Intervention levers:** market availability bands, workshop access, household supply interruptions, and the difference between purchasing convenience and direct control. No numerical choice is made here.

**Result:** **STRONG.** Hunting is useful and connected, not compulsory.

### Scenario B — The player never Woodcuts

**Supply behavior:** ordinary forestry and yard work provide eligible bark; fuel workers receive wood from ordinary, civic, and traded flows; charcoal can also enter by trade. The player can purchase bark and charcoal and continue Leatherworking and Smithing.

**Pressure revealed:** bark and fuel availability must visibly derive from responsible woodland work so the alternate supply does not feel like an infinite shop abstraction.

**Intervention levers:** bounded closures, public repair priority, ordinary stock condition, and market availability. No bark-only substitute action is permitted.

**Result:** **STRONG.** Woodcutting improves control and participation without becoming an equipment tax.

### Scenario C — The player gathers heavily

**Supply behavior:** repeated Hunting yields useful bundles only from eligible targets and successful recovery. Repeated Woodcutting yields ordinary wood, with bark only from qualified sources and actions. The player may sell, store, process, or route outputs into ordinary conversion.

**Pressure revealed:** unconstrained access could turn stewardship into decoration, oversupply bark or hides, and reward repeating the easiest opportunity rather than learning varied contexts.

**Intervention levers:** permissions, seasonal or condition-based availability, source closures, habitat response, public-work claims, carrying and storage constraints if later justified, and diverse sinks. These should express world conditions rather than low random chances.

**Revision applied:** the architecture requires opportunity eligibility and closure states to be legible before action. Bark remains deterministic when qualified, so scarcity must come from access and source condition rather than bad luck.

**Result:** **PARTIAL.** Node content must demonstrate that stewardship conditions and opportunity variation constrain heavy gathering without creating arbitrary lockouts.

### Scenario D — Trade supply is temporarily disrupted

**Supply behavior:** imported steel is most directly affected. New hard equipment and major hard refurbishment tighten, while held stock, existing equipment maintenance, flexible equipment work, player-supplied hides and bark, household textiles, and ordinary local conversions continue where their own inputs remain available.

**Pressure revealed:** a broad disruption must not halt all progression or prompt an emergency local ore source. Repair demand can also consume scarce charcoal and stock.

**Intervention levers:** existing-stock conservation, repair priority, alternate lawful consignments, bounded receiving assistance, substitution between new construction and refurbishment, and deferred nonessential commissions.

**Revision applied:** disruptions affect selected relationships rather than the entire economy at once. Great War remains never become a renewable replacement supply.

**Result:** **STRONG** at architecture level. Exact event cadence and access are later content decisions.

### Scenario E — One material becomes oversupplied

**Example pressures:** many raw hides can exceed tanning capacity; bark can exceed current hide flow; flax bundles can exceed textile labor; tow can accumulate if lining demand dominates padding demand; charcoal can accumulate when Smithing demand is low.

**Healthy behavior:** materials have multiple legitimate sinks or may be sold and stored, but not every surplus receives an invented recipe. Ordinary processors and markets need not accept unlimited volumes.

**Intervention levers:** processor acceptance, competing household and civic uses already supported by lore, repair demand, trade outflow, bounded storage rules if later justified, and slower ordinary restocking when local surplus exists. No disposal recipe or XP-only sink is added.

**Revision applied:** the proposal distinguishes **accepted supply** from unconditional vendor purchase. This permits capacity and stewardship to matter without a live commodity simulation.

**Result:** **PARTIAL.** Later balance must ensure linen and tow each have sufficient real use and that ordinary buyers do not function as limitless deletion sinks.

### Scenario F — Repair demand becomes high

**Supply behavior:** Smithing refurbishment consumes steel and charcoal where heat and structure require them. Leatherworking repair consumes leather, linen, tow, and tallow according to the actual failure. Serviceable wear, fastening, and lining work creates recurring sinks while structural failure remains workshop-level.

**Pressure revealed:** overly cheap restoration could erase new-production demand and scarcity; overly broad repair recipes could consume every material regardless of physical need.

**Intervention levers:** failure-specific inputs, safe-refurbishment limits, workshop access, civic repair priority, and eventual condition thresholds. No per-component durability simulation is required.

**Revision applied:** repair uses represented materials directly and cannot restore unsafe primary structures through tallow, generic kits, or arbitrary patching. New construction and repair therefore remain distinct sinks.

**Result:** **STRONG.** Later tuning must set severity and cost without changing the material logic.

### Scenario G — Aspirational equipment is reached

**Supply behavior:** the commission uses the same material foundation and recipe family as earlier equipment. Its aspiration comes from fitting, tolerances, matched construction, inspection, custody, repair access, and demonstrated mastery. Maintenance continues to consume appropriate ordinary goods.

**Pressure revealed:** once acquired, the item could collapse demand if it never needs care, or become a chore if maintenance is constant. A unique capstone ingredient would create artificial scarcity.

**Intervention levers:** meaningful but bounded maintenance, workshop refurbishment, alternate equipment identities, and continued participation in civic or trade supply for reasons beyond personal replacement.

**Result:** **STRONG.** No new material or fifth recipe family is needed.

### Scenario H — The player continues after practical mastery

**Supply behavior:** mastery supports greater knowledge of opportunities, reliable recovery, access to demanding contexts, high-quality construction, responsible refurbishment, and participation in supply disruptions. The player may continue gathering for trade, self-supply, discovery, repairs, or different equipment identities.

**Pressure revealed:** an economy based only on first-time equipment creation would exhaust its purpose. Endless superior material tiers would violate the approved progression direction.

**Intervention levers:** recurring maintenance, varied opportunity conditions, civic demand, market supply choices, commission and refurbishment work, and mastery of route and source knowledge. Exact long-term rewards remain later content design.

**Revision applied:** the node blueprint now requires every Advanced-or-later instance to provide at least one nonnumeric knowledge payoff: earlier diagnosis, invalid-work avoidance, safer recovery, combined delivery efficiency, or legitimate access to purpose-limited work. Post-mastery value uses recurring world relationships and recombined constraints rather than prestige-only materials or repeated reskins.

**Result:** **STRONG architecturally.** Later content must demonstrate the approved payoff contract; cadence and reward tuning remain production work rather than missing economic logic.

### Scenario I — Ordinary suppliers compete with player production

**Supply behavior:** ordinary tanners sell leather, households and processors supply hides, forestry supplies bark, and markets carry goods the player can also source or transform. This creates convenience and world credibility.

**Pressure revealed:** unrestricted, always-available, fully equivalent NPC conversion would invalidate Hunting, Woodcutting, or Leatherworking. Conversely, removing ordinary supply would make the world dependent on one adventurer.

**Intervention levers:** differing control, immediacy, access, stock condition, conversion availability, and disruption exposure. Player craft owns personal transformation and equipment mastery; ordinary supply owns continuity.

**Revision applied:** ordinary tanners sell a bounded supply of finished leather but do not automatically provide unrestricted input conversion. Ordinary suppliers do not satisfy aspirational qualifications or perform player mastery.

**Result:** **STRONG** if later stock and service rules preserve this division.

### Scenario J — The player prefers buying instead of gathering

**Supply behavior:** all nine represented equipment-chain states have ordinary or trade access at some stage. A buyer can obtain raw hide or leather, linen and tow, bark, charcoal, tallow, and imported steel without training Hunting or Woodcutting.

**Pressure revealed:** purchasing everything could reduce contact with world opportunities, while forced self-supply would violate player choice.

**Intervention levers:** convenience cost, disruption exposure, availability, workshop access, and the distinct discovery or control value of gathering. No gathered-only capstone input is added.

**Result:** **STRONG.** Buying is a legitimate strategy, with dependence on ordinary availability as its natural tradeoff.

### Scenario K — The player prefers self-supply instead of buying

**Supply behavior:** Hunting can provide eligible hides; Woodcutting can provide qualified bark and ordinary wood relationships; Leatherworking can convert hide to leather; Smithing and Leatherworking can construct and repair. Ordinary labor still converts flax, charcoal, and tallow, and steel remains imported.

**Pressure revealed:** complete isolation is neither physically grounded nor compatible with the approved economy. Requiring all ordinary goods to be personally producible would create hidden skills.

**Intervention levers:** let the player supply inputs and commission ordinary conversions, trade gathered surplus for unavailable goods, use accessible storage, and choose how much of the chain to control.

**Result:** **STRONG.** Self-supply means active participation and exchange, not solitary production of every input.

| Vegetable-tanned leather | Player Leatherworking plus ordinary tanners | Flexible construction, mixed work, repair | NPC competition; high repair demand | Player control and mastery versus bounded ordinary continuity |
| Flax bundle | Household cultivation | Ordinary textile conversion | Oversupply; buying-only play | Accepted workroom conversion into two useful outputs |
| Flax tow | Textile co-output | Damping, filling, padding repair | Uneven sibling-output demand | Multiple functional sinks; later balance must validate demand |
| Linen textile | Textile output | Lining, joining, wrapping, repair | Uneven sibling-output demand | Multiple construction and maintenance sinks |

No represented equipment state is source-less or sink-less. Tow and linen demand balance remains the clearest later tuning risk, not an architectural blocker.

## 4. Intervention hierarchy

If later prototyping exposes an unhealthy economy, interventions should be attempted in this order:

1. **Clarify eligibility and use:** ensure the good comes from the right source and reaches every already-approved sink.
2. **Adjust access and custody:** alter which ordinary supplier, workshop, or opportunity can accept or provide the good.
3. **Adjust competing claims:** use household, civic repair, stewardship, or trade demand already supported by the world.
4. **Adjust conversion and repair relationships:** refine when represented materials are physically required without adding WIP inventory.
5. **Tune quantities, prices, rates, and timing later.**
6. **Add a material or skill only after proving that the approved relationships cannot satisfy a real physical function.**

This order prevents balance problems from becoming excuses for resource bloat or hidden professions.

## 5. Adversarial findings and applied revisions

| Finding | Initial classification | Revision in the integrated architecture | Final classification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heavy gathering could ignore stewardship | **PARTIAL** | Require visible source/action eligibility, a maximum of one altered state per ordinary instance, bounded closures, and no low random bark chance as restraint | **STRONG architecturally; validate in content** |
| Ordinary buyers could become infinite deletion sinks | **PARTIAL** | Distinguish accepted supply from unconditional purchase; allow capacity and competing claims without continuous simulation | **STRONG architecturally** |
| NPC tanning could erase Leatherworking | **PARTIAL** | Keep market leather, reject assumed unrestricted conversion service, reserve player construction/mastery | **STRONG** |
| Ordinary conversion could become hidden skills | **PARTIAL** | Use simple custody handoffs with no XP or player production minigame | **STRONG** |
| Trade disruption could force local Mining or salvage farming | **CONTRADICTORY if adopted** | Preserve stock conservation, repair, alternate lawful trade, and flexible-path continuity | **STRONG** |
| Aspirational completion could exhaust demand | **PARTIAL** | Preserve maintenance, refurbishment, other equipment identities, trade, civic demand, and the explicit knowledge-payoff contract without superior material tiers | **STRONG architecturally; tune cadence later** |
| Linen and tow can accumulate unevenly | **PARTIAL** | Keep distinct multi-use sinks and combined production; exact proportions and demand cadence remain later balance variables | **STRONG structurally; validate in balance** |
| Self-supply could imply every profession becomes a skill | **CONTRADICTORY if adopted** | Define self-supply as gathering, primary craft, commission, and exchange | **STRONG** |

No **THIN**, **MISSING**, active **CONTRADICTORY**, or fixable **PARTIAL** source-to-sink relationship remains after revision. Content and balance must still validate stewardship readability, payoff cadence, and sibling-output demand, but those tests do not require missing economic logic.

## 6. Provisional decisions and Creative Director review

### Provisional decisions

1. Ordinary supply establishes viable baseline access; player gathering adds control, discovery, and useful output bundles.
2. Supply disruptions affect bounded relationships rather than running as a continuous region simulation.
3. Ordinary suppliers can decline or limit accepted inputs according to capacity, stewardship, and civic demand; markets are not infinite deletion sinks.
4. Player self-supply includes lawful ordinary conversion and exchange rather than personal ownership of every production step.
5. Repair remains a recurring sink but must reflect actual failure and safe refurbishment limits.
6. No new material, skill, generic repair kit, or capstone ingredient is required.

### Genuine morning review items

1. **Systemic disruption scope:** approve occasional bounded economy states outside authored stories, or keep shortages entirely story-triggered. Both preserve the architecture; the choice controls implementation and content burden.
2. **Post-mastery recurrence:** approve civic repair demand and changing opportunity conditions as recurring reasons to participate, subject to later bounded content design. The alternative is to keep recurrence mostly market and personal-maintenance driven, with less systemic world response.
3. **Ordinary-supplier acceptance:** approve finite/contextual acceptance of player surplus rather than universal vendor purchasing. This is recommended to prevent gathering from becoming consequence-free output dumping.

## 7. Stress-test conclusion

The Lowmark economy remains viable when the player skips either gathering skill, specializes heavily, buys inputs, or pursues substantial self-supply. Ordinary producers keep the world credible without erasing player mastery. Disruptions create conservation and sourcing choices without inventing local ore, renewable war salvage, hidden production skills, or mandatory chores. Repair creates durable sinks but does not replace new construction or demand generic repair items.

The architecture is ready for node and content blueprinting provided that the later opportunity design demonstrates stewardship constraints, varied mastery payoffs, and non-volume reasons to continue after practical mastery. Those are content-proof requirements rather than missing supply logic.
