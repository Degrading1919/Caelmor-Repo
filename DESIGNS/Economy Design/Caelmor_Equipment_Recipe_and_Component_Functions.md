# Caelmor — Equipment Recipe and Component Functions

**Status:** proposal-stage component architecture following Creative Director approval of the structural package. This document defines physical functions and broad craft responsibilities. It does not select materials, recipes, quantities, statistics, nodes, actions, prices, drops, schemas, equipment slots, or final forms.

## Authority and boundaries

This proposal works backward from the approved aspirational equipment foundation, the major equipment milestone proposal, and the approved Lore Foundation Lock.

- The four paths are the Keeper's Blade, Crestbridge Guard, Bridgewarden Harness, and Riverpath Leathers. All names remain provisional.
- Milestones 1–3 teach dependable function, declared identity, and proven specialization. Milestone 4 is the aspirational commission.
- Smithing and Leatherworking are the principal v1 craft responsibilities. Mixed-material assembly, fitting, joining, finishing, and commission integration are ordinary processes or specialist workshop labor, not separate required skills.
- Adornment does not gate functional specialization. Decorative or symbolic finishing may express identity without controlling equipment progression.
- The eight component families are a vocabulary, not a checklist. A piece uses only the families justified by its physical job.
- Documented custody belongs to acquisition, provenance, and commission legitimacy. It is neither a physical component nor a consumable inventory token.
- Exact weapon form, handedness, armor breakdown, guard slot, and loadout compatibility remain unresolved.
- S01 remains deferred. This proposal creates no ranged equipment path.

## 1. Shared component-function vocabulary

| Component family | Physical purpose | Required qualities | Likely responsibility | Repair and maintenance role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Load-bearing structure** | Carries the primary forces placed on the equipment and preserves its usable form. It may be rigid or flexible according to the item. | Stable under expected load; resistant to permanent distortion, tearing, or joint failure; workable into a controlled form. | Smithing for hard weapon and protection structures; Leatherworking for flexible protective structures. | Inspected for cracks, stretching, distortion, and failed joins. Serious damage requires workshop refurbishment rather than routine field replacement. |
| **Working surface** | Performs the equipment's primary physical job: holding an edge, receiving and spreading impact, or presenting a protective outer face. | Appropriate hardness or toughness, controlled surface geometry, resistance to abrasion and repeated contact, and compatibility with the supporting structure. | Usually formed and finished by the primary craft. | Maintained through sharpening, dressing, conditioning, reshaping, or surface repair as the form permits. It is not assumed to be replaceable. |
| **Junction reinforcement** | Prevents force from concentrating where structures, surfaces, fastenings, or control points meet. | High resistance to splitting, pull-through, shear, and repeated flex at local stress points. | Made by the primary craft; cross-craft joining may be ordinary workshop labor where two construction types meet. | Checked at seams, mounts, rims, corners, and other stress transitions; repaired before local damage reaches the primary structure. |
| **Control/fit interface** | Lets the wearer or wielder direct the item reliably and keeps force aligned with the body. | Secure without harmful pressure; stable when wet, worn, or under load; adjustable or shaped where the item requires it. | Smithing or Leatherworking according to the primary construction, with fitting by the responsible workshop. | Refit as use changes the interface; restore grip, shape, or contact before looseness compromises control. |
| **Flexible fastening** | Closes, retains, tensions, suspends, or articulates parts without making the whole assembly rigid. | Repeated-flex endurance, secure closure, controlled give, and practical replacement. | Usually Leatherworking when physically required; ordinary fitting and joining do not create a separate skill dependency. | A normal replaceable service point. Inspection focuses on stretch, tearing, loosened holes, and failed closures. |
| **Impact-damping layer** | Reduces shock transmitted from a strike or collision into the user's hand or body. | Compresses or yields in a controlled way, recovers sufficiently for repeated use, stays positioned, and does not destroy control. | Leatherworking where a separate flexible layer is used; the primary workshop integrates and fits it. | Replaced when packed down, shifted, hardened, or unable to recover. It does not substitute for the working surface or load-bearing structure. |
| **Protective lining** | Separates the primary assembly from the body, limits chafing and snagging, and manages ordinary sweat or incidental damp. | Comfortable under movement, resistant to abrasion at contact points, cleanable, and compatible with the outer construction. | Leatherworking or ordinary workshop finishing where the item needs a lining. | Cleaned, dried, patched, or replaced. A failed lining reduces usability before it destroys the main structure. |
| **Serviceable wear part** | Accepts predictable sacrifice so routine wear can be corrected without remaking the primary assembly. | Accessible, replaceable, deliberately less permanent than the structure it protects, and secure during use. | Made by the craft appropriate to its physical form and fitted by the maintaining workshop. | Replaced as planned maintenance. It remains distinct from the working surface, though a later design may justify one part serving both functions. |

### Vocabulary rules

1. A family appears only when the item has the corresponding physical need.
2. A physical part may satisfy more than one family only when its construction can meet both functions without hiding a tradeoff.
3. Working surfaces are presumed integral unless a later design proves that replacement is physically and economically justified.
4. Serviceable wear parts are intentionally sacrificial. Their purpose is to protect a more difficult repair, not to create arbitrary recipe count.
5. Better workmanship means more controlled geometry, alignment, fit, joining, preparation, finish, inspection, and repair access. It does not mean merely substituting a higher-ranked material.

## 2. Milestone-by-milestone component requirements

### Keeper's Blade path

The working name establishes an edge-bearing sidearm identity, while exact form and handedness remain open.

| Milestone | Required component functions | Physical and process demand | Maintenance lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Dependable field equipment** | Load-bearing structure; edge-bearing working surface; control/fit interface. Junction reinforcement only if the eventual form has a distinct high-stress connection. | Smithing establishes a straight, stable structure, usable edge geometry, and secure control. Ordinary fitting makes the weapon predictable rather than prestigious. | Keep the working surface usable, inspect alignment, and correct a loosening control point before continued use damages the structure. |
| **2 — Declared working identity** | The same core families, with a more deliberate junction and a serviceable wear part only where repeated hand or contact wear warrants one. | Smithing must balance edge support, recovery, and control instead of maximizing one isolated property. Fit and finishing establish the reliable generalist identity. | The player learns that surface care, alignment, and grip upkeep preserve combat rhythm. Routine replacement must not turn the edge itself into a disposable insert by default. |
| **3 — Proven specialist equipment** | Load-bearing structure, working surface, junction reinforcement, and control/fit interface must act as one force path. A justified wear part must be accessible without disturbing the primary structure. | Smithing uses tighter geometry and joining tolerances. Workshop fitting checks how forces move from working surface through structure to the user's control point. | Field care can restore ordinary readiness; edge damage, distortion, or junction failure requires skilled workshop correction. Repair history begins to matter as evidence of responsible use. |
| **4 — Aspirational commission** | No new family is added. The commission requires a matched structure and working surface, individually fitted control, verified junctions, and planned access to any wear part. | Smithing remains primary. The commission adds selective preparation, close tolerances, repeatable balance, staged inspection, and final fitting under an accountable workshop. | A repair standard is established at commissioning so future work preserves alignment and identity instead of gradually changing the weapon into a different tool. |

### Crestbridge Guard path

The Guard remains a hand-controlled defensive implement. Its precise silhouette, slot, and compatibility rules remain open.

| Milestone | Required component functions | Physical and process demand | Maintenance lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Dependable field equipment** | Load-bearing structure; impact-receiving working surface; control/fit interface. | Smithing forms a stable defensive body and a reliable means of control. The surface must receive ordinary impacts without uncontrolled collapse or dangerous force concentration. | Inspect the face, structure, and control point after repeated impacts. Cosmetic marks are distinguished from distortion that changes defensive behavior. |
| **2 — Declared working identity** | Add junction reinforcement where the control interface meets the structure. Add an impact-damping layer or flexible fastening only if the eventual form physically needs it. | Smithing establishes impact distribution; Leatherworking may supply a physically necessary flexible control, retention, or damping element without becoming a universal prerequisite. | The player learns that secure fit and damping condition affect defensive control even when the outer face appears intact. |
| **3 — Proven specialist equipment** | The structure, working surface, reinforced control junction, and any damping or retention system must sustain repeated defensive commitments. A serviceable wear part is added only at a predictable high-wear contact. | The primary Smithing workshop controls face geometry and load transfer. Any secondary Leatherworking contribution is integrated through ordinary fitting and must have a clear mechanical job. | Worn control and damping parts are serviced before they cause late movement, poor bracing, or structural damage. Severe deformation returns to the workshop. |
| **4 — Aspirational commission** | No new family is added. Required functions are matched for the bearer: impact face, load path, control position, damping where used, and access to planned wear points. | Smithing remains primary. Commission quality comes from consistent impact behavior, fitted control, tight junctions, inspection under expected loading, and a repair plan that preserves those relationships. | The commissioned fit is recorded as provenance, not carried as an item. Later refurbishment must reproduce the verified control and force path. |

### Bridgewarden Harness path

The Harness is an integrated heavy-protection identity. This document does not decide whether it is one item or several equipment pieces.

| Milestone | Required component functions | Physical and process demand | Maintenance lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Dependable field equipment** | Load-bearing structure; protective working surface; flexible fastening; protective lining. Junction reinforcement appears wherever closures or moving boundaries concentrate stress. | Smithing is primary for the protective structure and facing. Leatherworking supplies fastenings or lining only where the physical assembly requires them. | Dry and clean body-contact layers, keep closures secure, and distinguish a replaceable fastening failure from damage to the protective structure. |
| **2 — Declared working identity** | Strengthen junctions, control fit under movement, and introduce impact damping only where direct protection needs it. Serviceable wear parts protect predictable closure, edge, or contact locations. | Smithing shapes coverage and distributes direct force. Fitting prevents the heavy assembly from shifting or concentrating load on the wearer. | Heavy protection requires adjustment and repeated inspection; poor fit is treated as functional failure rather than discomfort alone. |
| **3 — Proven specialist equipment** | Protective surfaces, structural supports, reinforced junctions, fastenings, lining, and any damping are zoned according to load and movement rather than applied uniformly. | The Smithing workshop coordinates controlled overlap, clearance, and load transfer. Secondary Leatherworking work is required only for actual flexible elements. Workshop integration keeps mobility boundaries from becoming weak points. | High-wear parts can be replaced without dismantling sound structural sections. Distorted coverage or failed load-bearing joins require workshop refurbishment. |
| **4 — Aspirational commission** | No new family is added. The commission requires matched protective sections, bearer-specific fit, controlled movement at boundaries, accessible service points, and compatible lining and fastening behavior. | Smithing remains primary. Commission quality lies in consistent coverage, close fit, disciplined joining, staged inspection, and the ability to refurbish damaged sections without losing the established fit. | The workshop preserves fitting references and service history as provenance. They guide future work but never enter the physical recipe. |

### Riverpath Leathers path

The Leathers are flexible protective wear. Exact garment breakdown and equipment slots remain open.

| Milestone | Required component functions | Physical and process demand | Maintenance lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Dependable field equipment** | Flexible load-bearing structure; protective working surface; flexible fastening; protective lining only where direct body contact requires it. | Leatherworking creates a stable flexible form, usable closure, and an outer face that tolerates travel, incidental damp, and ordinary abrasion. | Clean, dry, condition, and patch before stiffness, stretch, or seam wear compromises movement. |
| **2 — Declared working identity** | Add control/fit interfaces and junction reinforcement at movement and load points. Serviceable wear parts protect predictable cuffs, closures, or contact zones only where justified. | Leatherworking patterns the assembly for freedom of movement without loose snagging or uncontrolled stretch. Protection is concentrated where it preserves mobility rather than spread uniformly. | Fit and closure condition are part of protection. Routine repairs should preserve movement instead of merely covering damage. |
| **3 — Proven specialist equipment** | Structure, protective face, reinforced junctions, fit, fastening, and lining are zoned for changing conditions. An impact-damping layer may appear only where it supports the light-protection identity without imitating heavy armor. | Leatherworking uses more exact patterning, orientation, seam placement, surface preparation, and finishing. Any minor Smithing contribution must answer a real closure or reinforcement need and does not require Smithing mastery from the player. | Replace sacrificial contact pieces and refit stretched areas before damage spreads into primary panels. Deep tearing or persistent loss of shape returns to a skilled workshop. |
| **4 — Aspirational commission** | No new family is added. The commission requires matched flexible sections, wearer-specific movement and closure fit, deliberately placed reinforcement, controlled surface finish, and accessible repair zones. | Leatherworking remains primary. Commission quality comes from selection for compatible behavior, precise patterning, matched stretch, careful joining, inspection through full movement, and repairability without progressive stiffening. | Fitting references and maintenance history allow later repairs to preserve mobility and weather behavior. They are records of custody, not components. |

## 3. Aspirational commission component profiles

| Commission | Primary craft | Required physical integration beyond Milestone 3 | What the commission specifically proves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Keeper's Blade** | Smithing | The edge-bearing surface, supporting structure, junctions, and control fit are matched as one predictable force path; planned wear access does not disturb alignment. | Repeated performance, controlled balance, individual fit, inspectable workmanship, and a repair standard that preserves the weapon's established behavior. |
| **Crestbridge Guard** | Smithing | The impact face, supporting structure, control point, and any justified damping or retention parts are fitted to receive repeated pressure without unpredictable shift. | Defensive consistency under expected loading, secure bearer control, safe force transfer, and refurbishment that restores rather than changes the defensive response. |
| **Bridgewarden Harness** | Smithing | Protective sections, load-bearing supports, movement boundaries, fastenings, lining, and any damping are fitted as one serviceable protection system. | Reliable coverage while acting under pressure, bearer-specific fit, controlled stress at boundaries, and sectional repair without sacrificing the established whole. |
| **Riverpath Leathers** | Leatherworking | Flexible protective sections, movement zones, closures, reinforcements, lining, and repair points are patterned and matched to the wearer. | Freedom of movement under changing conditions, stable fit when worn, deliberate protection without heavy construction, and repair that does not accumulate stiffness or distortion. |

Commission legitimacy also requires the already approved civic or narrative standing, demonstrated equipment understanding, meaningful player contribution, and skilled workshop participation. Those requirements govern acquisition. They do not add physical recipe components.

## 4. Craft and process dependency map

```text
Keeper's Blade
  Smithing: structure + edge-bearing working surface + hard junctions
  Ordinary workshop process: joining + alignment + fitting + finishing + inspection
  Leatherworking: only if the chosen control or wear design physically requires it

Crestbridge Guard
  Smithing: structure + impact-working surface + hard junctions
  Ordinary workshop process: joining + load testing + fitting + inspection
  Leatherworking: only for justified flexible control, fastening, damping, or wear parts

Bridgewarden Harness
  Smithing: protective structure + facing + hard reinforcement
  Leatherworking: physically required fastenings, lining, damping, or flexible interfaces
  Ordinary workshop process: integration + fitting + movement check + inspection

Riverpath Leathers
  Leatherworking: flexible structure + protective surface + fit + fastening + lining
  Ordinary workshop process: patterning + joining + fitting + finishing + inspection
  Smithing: only for a justified hard closure or local reinforcement
```

### Dependency rules

- Each path has one primary craft. Secondary contribution follows construction need rather than an obligation to train both crafts.
- An NPC specialist workshop may perform commission integration and fitting without creating a new player skill.
- A mixed assembly does not imply a Fabrication skill. Fabrication is not a v1 equipment dependency.
- Functional specialization comes from construction, fit, and use. Adornment is not a v1 equipment dependency.
- Symbolic marks, civic records, workshop witnesses, and custody histories can support identity and legitimacy without becoming recipe inputs.
- Field maintenance handles cleaning, surface care, adjustment, and planned wear replacement. Workshop refurbishment handles damaged primary structures, lost alignment, distorted coverage, and failed major junctions.
- The game may represent upkeep at item level. This architecture does not require separate durability meters for component families.

## Exact minimum material functions for the next pass

The next pass must derive the smallest coherent material set capable of satisfying these functions. One material may satisfy several functions where world logic and processing support it; this list is not a demand for one material per line.

1. **Rigid structural support:** retain controlled shape under weapon, guard, or heavy-protection loads and accept reliable forming and repair.
2. **Flexible structural support:** carry tension and repeated movement in light protection without uncontrolled stretch or tearing.
3. **Edge-bearing work:** take and retain a controlled cutting edge while remaining supportable and maintainable under repeated use.
4. **Impact-receiving work:** withstand and distribute repeated direct contact without brittle failure or dangerous collapse.
5. **Flexible protective facing:** resist abrasion, snagging, incidental damp, and limited impact while preserving movement.
6. **Local junction reinforcement:** resist shear, pull-through, splitting, and fatigue where components or stress zones meet.
7. **Secure control and fit:** provide stable hand or body contact, controllable friction, shape retention, and practical adjustment.
8. **Flexible fastening and articulation:** close, retain, tension, or permit controlled movement through repeated flex and service.
9. **Impact damping:** yield and recover enough to reduce transmitted shock without causing unstable control or excessive bulk.
10. **Body-contact lining:** limit chafing and snagging, tolerate sweat or incidental damp, and permit cleaning, patching, or replacement.
11. **Sacrificial wear service:** accept predictable abrasion, compression, or closure wear and be replaceable without remaking the primary assembly.
12. **Joining and finishing support:** enable durable seams, mounts, bonds, edge treatments, surface preparation, and protective finishing compatible with the joined parts.
13. **Workshop heat and process support:** provide the broadly required heating, forming, drying, curing, cleaning, and finishing conditions without assuming one universal fuel or treatment.
14. **Repair compatibility:** permit sharpening, dressing, conditioning, patching, refitting, part replacement, or workshop reshaping without forcing whole-item replacement after ordinary wear.

These are material functions, not material names, resource categories, recipe slots, or quantity requirements.

## Exact next handoff

Derive the **minimum coherent material set** from the approved functions above. Begin with the four equipment purposes and select only enough world-supported materials and processing relationships to satisfy the required structural, working, joining, fitting, protection, maintenance, and repair functions.
