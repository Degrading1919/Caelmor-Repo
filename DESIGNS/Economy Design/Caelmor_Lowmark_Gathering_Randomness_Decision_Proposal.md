# Caelmor Lowmark Gathering Randomness Decision Proposal

**Status:** Proposal for Creative Director approval

**Scope:** Hunting HM1–HM8 and Woodcutting WM1–WM8

**Authority:** Approved Lowmark gathering architecture, method definitions, cadence and throughput calibration, and source-conditional output rules

## 1. Decision

Recommend **Model A: fully deterministic outputs**.

In this proposal, *fully deterministic* means that a valid opportunity completed correctly produces its authored primary outputs. It does not mean that every interaction produces goods. Invalid opportunities, execution mistakes, deliberate refusals, readable world-condition losses, and correctly abandoned work can produce no material output because the player did not complete a recoverable source action.

No random roll should turn a valid, correctly executed Hunting recovery or Woodcutting harvest into failure. No random roll should add extra core equipment-chain goods to an otherwise identical completion.

## 2. Why Model A Fits the Approved Architecture

The approved progression already creates uncertainty and mastery through:

- finding and recognizing valid opportunities;
- reading behavior, habitat, source condition, damage, custody, and access;
- choosing an appropriate method;
- executing approach, recovery, diagnosis, or cutting correctly;
- refusing work that should not be taken;
- responding to closures, stale evidence, unsafe conditions, and public need;
- improving route choice and reducing nonproductive time through knowledge.

Those are visible causes that the player can learn. A success roll after correct execution would add variance without adding a new decision.

Model A also preserves the existing rule that raw hide and tannin-rich bark are **source-conditional guaranteed outputs**. Eligibility determines whether the output exists; chance does not.

## 3. Model Comparison

| Model | Primary-output rule | XP rule | Main benefit | Main cost | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Fully deterministic** | Authored outputs occur when the opportunity is valid and the relevant action succeeds | Existing fixed authored-action XP | Maximum readability; knowledge and execution explain results | Requires clear state communication | **Recommended** |
| **B. Deterministic primary + rare bounded bonuses** | Primary output remains guaranteed; a small chance adds more of an existing output | Bonus grants no extra XP | Brief surprise and marginal extra supply | Adds unneeded variance, raises late supply pressure, and weakens source-to-output legibility | Reject for HM1–HM8 and WM1–WM8 |
| **C. Random success/failure** | A valid completion can lose its primary output or fail after commitment | Either reduces realized XP or decouples XP from output | Familiar stochastic gathering cadence | Conflicts with source-conditional guarantees, mastery, calibration, and player trust | Reject |

Model B is less harmful than Model C, but it does not solve a demonstrated design gap. There are no approved bonus items, and extra copies of existing core goods increase pressure precisely where HM8 and WM8 already have the highest throughput.

## 4. Hunting Outcome Audit

### 4.1 Invalid Opportunity

An invalid opportunity produces no recovered edible output or raw hide. Invalidity must arise from an authored, readable state: stale evidence, an ineligible target, an unsuitable recovery condition, an inaccessible situation, or another method-defined mismatch.

The player may receive the fixed XP assigned to a completed diagnostic or investigative action. They do not receive the fixed completion award for an action they did not complete.

This is deterministic nonproduction, not random failure.

### 4.2 Player Execution Failure

A missed approach, mishandled pursuit, failed recovery, or damaged source can prevent some or all material recovery when the method defines that consequence. The result follows from the player's action and the readable situation.

Fixed authored-action XP remains attached to the action actually completed. The system does not secretly scale XP by level or roll a second success check after correct execution.

### 4.3 Deliberate Refusal

HM6 and HM7 may present situations where declining, redirecting, delaying, or leaving the opportunity is the correct decision. A deliberate refusal produces no material output from that opportunity.

Any XP belongs only to the authored assessment, inquiry, or stewardship action completed. Refusal must never be punished by converting a correct judgment into a random chance at the goods the player deliberately left.

### 4.4 World-Condition Loss

Closures, unsafe conditions, changed access, stale sign, or a lost recovery window may remove output. These conditions must be legible before or during commitment and must follow authored world rules.

World-condition loss is allowed because it creates routing and preparation decisions. Randomly declaring a valid opportunity lost after successful execution is not allowed.

### 4.5 Random Failure

**None.** Once an eligible opportunity has been correctly completed and recovery conditions are satisfied:

- its authored edible relationship occurs where appropriate;
- raw hide occurs when the animal and recovery conditions qualify;
- suitable fat may enter the ordinary rendering relationship without becoming a required equipment-chain inventory state.

Raw hide remains unavailable from ineligible animals or failed recovery. That conditional rule is deterministic.

## 5. Woodcutting Outcome Audit

### 5.1 Valid Harvest Success

A valid, stewardship-approved cutting, pruning, yard-recovery, repair-supply, or damage-recovery action completed correctly produces its authored ordinary-wood output relationship. No post-completion success roll applies.

### 5.2 Ordinary Wood Output

Ordinary wood remains the primary output of productive woodland work. The amount and form remain future balance and content decisions, but the existence of the approved output is deterministic for the authored action.

### 5.3 Bark Eligibility

Tannin-rich bark is a source-conditional co-output:

- an eligible source and action produce the authored bark output;
- an ineligible source or action produces none;
- bark is never a weighted random drop;
- no bark-only action or bark-only node is created.

### 5.4 Damage Recovery

WM5 outcomes depend on diagnosed damage, usable material, safety, stewardship, and the scope of the accepted work. Some cleanup or stabilization work may correctly produce little or no usable supply. That outcome must be established by the authored condition and the player's decision, not a hidden yield roll.

### 5.5 Constrained and Public Work

Authorized public, reserve, bank, route, or repair work produces its authored outputs when completed correctly. A closure, custody restriction, unsafe condition, correct refusal, or nonproductive stabilization task may prevent supply.

Those constraints create method identity through obligation and judgment. They must not become random throttles.

### 5.6 Random Failure

**None.** A valid productive action that the player completes correctly yields its authored ordinary wood and, when eligible, tannin-rich bark.

## 7. XP and Output Contract

The decision establishes the following contract across HM1–HM8 and WM1–WM8:

1. The same authored action under the same conditions grants the same XP.
2. Player level does not multiply success, XP, or material output invisibly.
3. Method cadence and calibrated throughput remain unchanged.
4. Knowledge improves realized efficiency by reducing mistakes, bad choices, and nonproductive routing.
5. Valid primary outputs and source-conditional co-outputs are deterministic.
6. No bonus-output roll grants extra XP or extra core goods.
7. Authored variation may change which opportunity exists; it may not secretly change the result of an identical valid completion.

## 8. Readability Requirements

Model A depends on communicating causes clearly. Later content and UX work must distinguish:

- eligible from ineligible sources;
- recoverable from unrecoverable conditions;
- productive work from cleanup or stabilization;
- correct refusal from execution failure;
- custody or closure restrictions from mechanical failure;
- bark-eligible woodland work from ordinary wood-only work.

The interface does not need to reveal every result in advance, but post-action feedback must let an attentive player understand why goods were or were not produced.

## 9. Rejected Uses of Randomness

The following are outside the approved model:

- accuracy-style success rolls for gathering;
- level-based hidden success chances;
- rare extra raw hide, bark, wood, food, steel, leather, textile, charcoal, or tallow rolls;
- junk consolation drops;
- critical harvests that multiply core outputs;
- random closures used to suppress throughput;
- XP bonuses attached to lucky material rolls.

This decision does not prohibit authored ecological variation, changing world conditions, or variation among distinct opportunities. It prohibits chance from changing the material result of the same valid authored action under the same conditions.

## 10. Recommendation

Approve **Model A: fully deterministic outputs** for Lowmark Hunting HM1–HM8 and Woodcutting WM1–WM8.

Primary outputs should be deterministic. Random failure should not exist. Random bonus outputs should not exist in this gathering scope. Mastery should improve results through recognition, judgment, execution, recovery, stewardship, and route knowledge.
