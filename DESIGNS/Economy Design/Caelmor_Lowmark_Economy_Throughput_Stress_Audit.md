## 5. Player-path stress tests

### 5.1 Hunting-focused player

**Result: HEALTHY WITH FOOD/HIDE SURPLUS PRESSURE.** HM2/HM5 generate useful food, hide, and renderer relationships. The player can sell raw, bank hide, tan later, or route eligible animal value. Low floor values prevent bulk goods from becoming automatic wealth. Final food consumption and hide/equipment demand remain critical.

### 5.2 Woodcutting-focused player

**Result: HEALTHY WITH HIGH WM8 PRESSURE.** Wood can be sold, stored, routed to repair/public demand, or converted 3:1 into charcoal. Bark supports tanning. WM8 can still flood ordinary wood and possibly bark if final per-unit values or local intake are generous.

### 5.3 Smithing-focused player

**Result: HEALTHY, IMPORT DEPENDENT BY DESIGN.** The player buys conventional steel and charcoal, then chooses construction or refurbishment. Woodcutting can reduce charcoal cost indirectly but is optional. Multiple ordinary steel suppliers must prevent one interruption from becoming a permanent hard lock.

### 5.4 Leatherworking-focused player

**Result: HEALTHY AND INTERCONNECTED.** The player needs hide and bark in a 2:1 ratio, can gather either side, buy either side, buy finished ordinary leather, or combine self-supply and trade. Linen, tow, and tallow broaden construction/repair without forcing new skills.

### 5.5 Mostly self-supplying player

**Result: VIABLE BUT NOT AUTARKIC.** Hunting and Woodcutting cover hide, bark, wood, and renderer flow. Ordinary workers still perform textile, charcoal, and rendering conversions; steel remains imported. This preserves useful settlements and markets.

### 5.6 Market buyer

**Result: VIABLE AT A TIME PREMIUM.** Retail spreads and service fees make buying more expensive than efficient self-supply, but the buyer saves gathering time and can specialize in Smithing, Leatherworking, combat, or quests. No material requires player-exclusive gathering.

### 5.7 Post-80 gatherer

**Result: VIABLE WITH BULK-SALE CONTROLS REQUIRED.** HM8 produces about 52–55 edible units/h sustainably; WM8 produces about 46 wood/h sustainably and up to 55 continuously. Modest buyer floors, bounded premium demand, yield tuning, and legitimate processing prevent automatic money dominance.

### 5.8 Mixed bank/process/craft loop

**Result: STRONG.** A player can Hunt for hide/food, Woodcut for bark/wood, bank batches, tan leather, commission ordinary conversions, craft or repair, sell surplus, and buy missing steel/textile. Each stage changes the player’s decision rather than adding an XP-only intermediate.

## 6. Self-supply versus buying

| Choice | Advantage | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Gather raw inputs | Avoids retail spread; earns gathering XP; supports route knowledge | Time, bank pressure, source/access conditions |
| Buy raw inputs | Saves gathering time; enables craft specialization | Retail spread and local availability |
| Process own gathered inputs | Adds useful form and may save future retail cost | Skill/service time, paired inputs, fee where ordinary labor owns conversion |
| Buy processed goods | Immediate construction/repair access | Highest unit price; ordinary producer margin |
| Sell raw now | Fast currency and storage relief | Gives up later self-supply and processing value |
| Bank raw/processed goods | Future repair and commission readiness | Delayed currency and storage use |

No choice dominates every player goal.

## 8. Currency architecture audit

### Faucet classes

- baseline sale of lawful ordinary goods;
- processor/workshop purchases within real demand;
- bounded work, repair, and civic commissions;
- ordinary local trade margin;
- already supported quest or reward payments when later specified.

### Sink classes

- material purchases, especially imported steel and missing cross-skill inputs;
- legitimate processing services;
- equipment purchases and workshop labor;
- repair/refurbishment;
- commission integration and fitting;
- useful transport or storage services where they change a real choice.

No tax, license, arbitrary listing fee, rent timer, or nuisance service has been invented solely to remove currency.

## 9. Vendor-arbitrage and money-method audit

All tested buy-process-sell loops lose value when inputs are purchased at NPC retail and outputs are dumped to baseline buyers:

- tanning: 59 in, 22 out;
- charcoal: 18 in, 6 out;
- textiles: 32 in, 12 out;
- tallow resale: 14 in, 4 out;
- steel resale: 42 in, 18 out.

**Best XP versus best money:**

- HM8 leads Hunting XP; HM5 retains the stronger focused hide/renderer money identity.
- WM8 leads Woodcutting XP and bulk floor pressure; WM2 tannery demand and WM6 repair commissions can outperform it while their real demand lasts.
- WM8 is not approved as a guaranteed best-money method. Exact intake, prices, bark ecology, and item yield must verify that its bulk sale remains below demand-led alternatives.

## 11. HM8/WM8 dominance result

| Test | HM8 | WM8 |
|---|---|---|
| Best XP | Yes, intended | Yes, intended |
| Best focused self-supply | No; HM5 hide and HM3 simple food remain clearer | No; WM2 bark and WM6 repair purpose remain clearer |
| Lowest attention / safest / most reliable | No | No |

No universal dominant method is accepted. WM8 remains the first method to fail if later price/stock tuning makes continuous bulk sale outperform every demand-led alternative.

## 12. Unresolved quantities requiring playtesting

- final edible item catalog, recovery yield presentation, Cooking ratios, and consumption;
- final ordinary-wood inventory representation and carrying behavior;
- bark-eligible source mix along WM2/WM4/WM8 routes;
- renderer eligibility and tallow demand frequency;
- exact repair incidence and material consumption during normal combat;
- final equipment material quantities after forms/slots are resolved;
- ordinary NPC stock, intake, local demand capacity, and recovery;
- actual prices, commission payouts, transport/storage costs, and workshop labor;
- whether the proposed value floors keep bulk gathering useful without making it dominant;
- whether imported steel remains available enough for normal progression while retaining scarcity meaning.

## 13. Creative Director decisions

No Creative Director decision is required before concrete price, stock, and recipe prototyping. The following findings require review but have bounded tuning controls:

1. WM8 ordinary-wood/bark bulk pressure is the largest oversupply risk.
2. Imported plain carbon steel is the largest scarcity risk.
3. HM8 food and focused HM2/HM5 hide supply require final Cooking, tanning, equipment, and repair quantities.

## 14. Final readiness

Gathered relationships now map to plausible inventory quantities; conversions create coherent physical loss and sinks; construction and repair consume the same materials across all milestones; raw sale, processing, banking, buying, self-supply, and crafting remain legitimate choices; vendor arbitrage fails; and no filler item or junk recipe is required.
