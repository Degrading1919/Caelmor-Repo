# Caelmor — Lowmark Gathering Route and Hub Requirements

**Status:** Proposal-stage placement and traversal requirements
**Scope:** Broad route relationships and multifunction hub requirements for Lowmark v1; no exact geometry or site counts

## Purpose

This document explains how the approved gathering opportunities, ordinary suppliers, processors, storage, markets, and workshops should relate in the world. It protects physical and civic plausibility without inventing roads, river courses, travel times, settlement layouts, or coordinates.

Every exact connection named below is a requirement to test against the existing map, not a map fact. Where placement depends on unresolved geometry, it is marked **MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED**.

## Placement principles

1. **Sources belong where their ecology or labor makes sense.** Convenience cannot move wet-ground game into a workshop yard or charcoal work into dense housing.
2. **A few memorable hubs should serve several related functions.** Lowmark does not need one station or vendor per item.
3. **Routes should express purpose.** A return from a hunt, a repair commission, and an imported-steel delivery may share a receiving area while beginning in different landscapes.
4. **Early sources remain connected to later needs.** Mastery adds judgment and route integration instead of abandoning starting landscapes.
5. **Dirty, smoky, or hazardous work needs credible separation.** Tanning, rendering, charcoal work, food handling, textile work, storage, and smithing cannot be collapsed into one implausible service counter.
6. **Stable geography is the default.** Occasional authored disruptions can alter one route, opportunity, or interface when clearly signaled; the economy is not a continuous simulation.
7. **Map uncertainty remains explicit.** No proposal here fixes a bridge, ferry, road, river direction, or settlement assignment.

## Multifunction hub requirements

### Hub class A — Market, receiving, and storage

**Purpose:** provide a legible circulation center for imported conventional steel, household goods, player-gathered goods, ordinary processed supply, banking/storage, and contextual demand.

Required functions:

- dependable baseline market outlet for normal lawful transferable goods;
- imported plain carbon steel receipt and inspection;
- purchase and sale of ordinary supply without making the player the sole producer;
- access to represented storage;
- visible redirection toward workshops, households, or processors;
- occasional authored shortage or delayed-supply presentation.

Ordinary wood is one broad tangible state here. It can be kept, banked, traded, supplied to repair demand, or offered to fuel workers where suitable. The hub does not divide it into grades, logs, fuelwood, offcuts, or repair stock.

The baseline outlet protects gathered goods from becoming stranded. Contextual demand remains meaningful through purpose, access, convenience, or better terms; it must not become an unlimited high-value deletion mechanism.

**MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED:** which established settlement or receiving context can plausibly connect regional trade, covered storage, workshops, and player access.

### Hub class B — Craft, repair, and public-work receiving

**Purpose:** connect Smithing and Leatherworking construction, repair, refurbishment, commissions, and crafting from storage.

Required functions:

- correct workshop context for storage crafting;
- direct use of represented materials rather than generic repair kits or component bundles;
- intake of imported steel, charcoal, leather, linen, tow, tallow, and other approved recipe inputs;
- visible repair demand and custody for purpose-bound work;
- workshop refurbishment for damaged primary structures;
- separation of smithing heat and leather/cloth work where physically necessary.

Ordinary wood may support broader household or public repair relationships, but it does not enter the nine-state equipment chain unless a later approved physical design demonstrates the need. Purpose-bound wood remains a tangible player-owned state whose disclosed destination is preserved.

**MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED:** proximity among storage, safe forge heat/cooling, leatherwork space, public repair staging, and the market/receiving hub.

### Hub class C — Settlement-edge process services

**Purpose:** make ordinary tanning and rendering visible and reachable without creating player skills or management simulation.

Required functions:

- ordinary tanners can sell leather and accept appropriate ordinary inputs under bounded interfaces;
- player Leatherworking retains the player-facing raw-hide-to-leather transformation;
- renderers accept legitimate animal-processing flow and sell rendered tallow;
- dirty water, odor, heat, spoilage, and waste remain separated from household and irrigation use;
- outputs can return to market, storage, or workshops.

This hub class may share a broad district or route with other services, but tanning and rendering remain distinct labor relationships. There is no carcass or fat inventory state required by this equipment chain.

**MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED:** drainage, water direction, flood reach, habitation distance, market access, and safe return route.

### Hub class D — Fuel-work interface

**Purpose:** connect suitable wood supply to ordinary charcoal production and return charcoal to smithing and trade.

Required functions:

- ordinary wood intake where the source is physically suitable and not reserved for another purpose;
- fuel-worker conversion as ordinary labor, not a player recipe or skill;
- firebreak, dry staging, cooling, smoke separation, and covered charcoal storage;
- sale or delivery of charcoal to market and smithing demand;
- clear closure during dangerous fire or wet-feed conditions.

**MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED:** safe distance, practical wood movement, prevailing smoke relationship if represented, and connection to market/workshop demand.

### Hub class E — Household production and clean conversion

**Purpose:** show flax, husbandry, household storage, textile labor, and food relationships as ordinary Lowmark life rather than abstract vendor generation.

Required functions:

- flax bundle production through household cultivation, with no Farming skill;
- linen and tow emerging together from ordinary textile labor;
- husbandry and animal-processing flow supporting alternate raw-hide supply and renderer feedstock;
- household purchase, sale, commission, or shortage response;
- clean drying and storage separate from dirty processing.

These relationships can be distributed visually across households while a small number of legible exchange interfaces handle player transactions.

**MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED:** suitable agricultural context, flood exposure, drying space, clean water if used, and practical exchange access.

## Gathering route families

A route family is a reusable relationship among source, recovery, and destination. It is not a prescribed road or optimized loop.

### R1 — Household-margin learning route

```text
PC1 household margin
→ HN-01 / HN-02 / WN-01
→ household intake or PC9 market/storage
→ optional PC7 processor or PC10 workshop
```

Purpose:

- teaches that gathering begins with ordinary need and evidence;
- connects early Hunting and Woodcutting to food, raw hide, ordinary wood, trade, and repair;
- keeps early opportunities useful through convenient self-supply and baseline trade.

**MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED:** whether plausible household margins can reach an exchange/storage function without inventing adjacency.

### R2 — Managed-woodland supply route

```text
PC2 managed woodland
→ WN-02 / WN-04 / WN-06
→ ordinary wood + eligible bark
→ PC9 storage/market, PC7 tannery, PC8 fuel workers, or PC10 repair receiving
```

Purpose:

- makes one harvest support several legitimate choices;
- protects bark as a co-output of valid work;
- creates repeat value without wood tiers;
- lets destination choice respond to current self-supply, market, tannery, fuel, or repair need.

**MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED:** extraction feasibility, access, flood/fire exposure, and relationships to receiving points.

### R3 — Wet-margin mixed route

```text
PC3 worked-water transition
→ HN-03 / HN-04 and/or WN-03
→ safe recovery
→ household, PC9 market/storage, PC7 processor, or PC10 public-work receiving
```

Purpose:

- shows that water-edge ecology and bank work create different actions in the same broad landscape;
- allows Hunting and Woodcutting knowledge to intersect without merging skills;
- makes wet-ground route planning and recovery meaningful.

**MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED:** all water, bank, crossing, and safe-return relationships.

### R4 — Movement and recovery route

```text
PC4 habitat transition
→ HN-04 / HN-05 / HN-06
→ planned field recovery
→ PC9 baseline market/storage or a contextual household/tannery/renderer destination
```

Purpose:

- makes route knowledge part of Hunting progression;
- distinguishes target contact from successful recovery;
- permits normal combat to resolve an existing danger without converting combat loot into Hunting output.

**MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED:** viable habitat transition, ordinary danger support, and recovery destination access.

### R5 — Public-repair custody route

```text
verified repair demand at PC10 or civic staging
→ PC5 stewarded source
→ WN-05 / WN-07
→ tangible ordinary wood under disclosed custody
→ PC10 repair receiving
```

Purpose:

- gives mastery a civic and cultural role;
- preserves a visible source-to-use relationship;
- avoids commission tokens and superior reserve wood;
- teaches that access is bounded by purpose.

**MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED:** whether the source and repair destination have a plausible movement relationship. Exact road, water, or crossing use remains open.

### R6 — Ordinary-supply alternative route

```text
PC9 market or relevant ordinary supplier
→ buy raw hide / leather / flax goods / charcoal / tallow / steel / ordinary wood where stocked
→ storage
→ PC10 correct workshop context
```

Purpose:

- supports players who prefer buying or who skip one gathering skill;
- keeps ordinary society economically credible;
- preserves gathering value through self-supply, trade, knowledge, disruption response, and contextual demand.

This route must remain viable without making every good permanently abundant or making contextual player delivery pointless.

## Route readability requirements

Later world content should communicate routes through ordinary evidence:

- worked margins, tracks, reeds, crossings, retained growth, cut cycles, reserve marks, damaged banks, repair staging, smoke, drying cloth, covered stock, and arriving trade;
- clear access states before commitment;
- visible destination roles rather than a separate icon for every item;
- storage and workshop relationships that support crafting from storage;
- disclosed destination for purpose-bound work;
- readable alternatives when an occasional authored disruption closes one source or service.

A route should not depend on hidden timers, external guides, arbitrary click order, or memorizing unsupported map facts.

## Authored disruption placement

Lowmark remains stable and learnable. A later content set may apply an occasional authored disruption to one opportunity, route, or interface when all of the following hold:

1. the local cause is visible or explained;
2. the affected scope is bounded;
3. the player can understand whether to wait, redirect, buy, sell elsewhere, or choose another opportunity;
4. the disruption reveals stewardship, labor, trade, weather, public work, or repair pressure;
5. normal play does not become a schedule of rotating chores.

Suitable proposal classes include unsafe flood condition, recovery closure, fire danger, delayed trade, damaged storage, or purpose-limited public need. Exact cadence, duration, and triggers remain future content and balance work.

## Density and solo-development controls

The world does not require all placement classes to become separate named sites. A feasible v1 arrangement can use:

- distributed source instances for the seven Hunting and seven Woodcutting patterns;
- a few memorable multifunction hubs combining only physically compatible roles;
- shared household, market, storage, workshop, and processor interfaces;
- a small authored condition vocabulary reused across opportunities;
- environmental evidence and dialogue variants instead of bespoke systems for every instance.

Content production should reject any proposed site that exists only to expose one item, one conversion arrow, one progression band, or one short-lived task.

## Verification register

Before exact placement, map and content review must verify:

1. where agricultural household margins plausibly support gathering and ordinary exchange;
2. where managed working woodland can exist without contradicting settlements, agriculture, or bank protection;
3. where wet-margin Hunting and bank-protection work are ecologically and physically plausible;
4. whether habitat transitions support movement-based Hunting without inventing exact corridors;
5. where stewarded repair sources can connect plausibly to public-work demand;
6. where imported steel can arrive and be inspected;
7. where dirty processing can separate waste from domestic and irrigation water;
8. where fuel work can maintain firebreaks and smoke separation;
9. where storage is credible against flood, damp, and fire;
10. where the correct workshop contexts can connect to storage, receiving, and repair demand.

Failure to verify one exact relationship blocks only that placement. It does not require rewriting the economy architecture.

## Route and hub readiness

The approved opportunities can be placed through ten broad classes and six route families without assuming exact map geometry. Five multifunction hub classes cover trade, storage, crafts, repair, ordinary processing, fuel work, and household production while keeping incompatible labor physically distinct.
