# Caelmor — Lowmark Woodcutting Progression Architecture

**Status:** proposal-stage progression architecture for Creative Director audit. This document defines bounded Woodcutting opportunity patterns and progression identities before node, species, tool, yield, timing, XP, level-placement, or map production. It does not amend lore or approve final content.

## Authority and fixed boundaries

This proposal follows the approved Lowmark gathering and supply chain:

```text
stewardship-approved selective harvest
→ ordinary wood as the primary relationship
+ tannin-rich bark when the source and action qualify
→ optional delivery of suitable wood into ordinary fuel-worker supply
→ wood charcoal through ordinary labor
```

- Woodcutting is selective work in an inhabited, repaired landscape. It is not clear-cutting.
- Ordinary wood is one broad provisional tangible inventory state outside the nine equipment-chain states. It is transferable, bankable, tradeable, and reusable across approved destinations. No species tier, wood grade, separate log family, tool tier, or Fletching demand is selected here.
- Tannin-rich bark is a source-conditional guaranteed co-output of legitimate wood work. There is no bark-only action, route, or node.
- Fuel workers produce charcoal. Woodcutting may supply acceptable feedstock but does not gain a charcoal recipe or a new skill.
- A player is not Lowmark's sole cutter. Households, public crews, yard workers, and traders remain active suppliers.
- Exact stewardship offices and powers remain unresolved. This proposal uses neutral terms such as custodian, work brief, public reserve, and closure.
- Exact geography and route relationships remain subject to later map verification.
- S01 remains deferred. No ranged demand is used to justify a wood output.

## Progression identity

Woodcutting mastery is the ability to recognize **which cut serves the landscape and community, when it should happen, what can be recovered, and where the output is most valuable**.

The familiar physical core remains: inspect, prepare, cut, recover, sort, and deliver or retain. Progression changes the constraints around that core:

- source condition becomes less obvious;
- public and household claims compete;
- terrain and recovery become harder;
- the acceptable output mix becomes more specific;
- poor choices carry visible bank, fire, access, or repair consequences;
- route and destination knowledge become valuable;
- experienced players can combine multiple needs in one responsible work plan.

Higher bands do not replace an early tree with a higher-tier tree. They ask the player to read more of the situation and make a better cut.

## Opportunity vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Source** | A living, damaged, fallen, pruned, or yard-held woodland subject eligible for a defined work purpose. This is not yet a final node type. |
| **Work brief** | A readable reason the work is permitted: renewal, hazard removal, bank care, public repair, household maintenance, or responsible recovery. It need not become an inventory permit. |
| **Custodian** | The household, public crew, landholder, crossing keeper, or other neutral authority responsible for access and stewardship. Exact office is deferred. |
| **Primary wood relationship** | Useful ordinary wood produced by valid work. Final wood states and uses are outside this pass. |
| **Eligible bark relationship** | Guaranteed tannin-rich bark recovered when the selected source and action physically provide it. Ineligible work produces none and never rolls for it. |
| **Destination claim** | A broad downstream need such as public repair, household use, market sale, or fuel-worker intake. It influences choices without creating a management simulation. |
| **Closure** | A visible temporary or standing restriction caused by source health, bank instability, fire risk, regeneration, public reserve, unsafe conditions, or civic need. |

## Bounded managed-woodland opportunity set

Six archetypes provide enough distinct relationships for long progression. They are reusable content patterns rather than six species or six tiers.

### W1 — Tended-edge maintenance

**Purpose:** teach valid selection and recovery through ordinary pruning, clearance, and small-scale household or settlement-edge work.

- **Core judgment:** distinguish marked or visibly eligible work from healthy stock that should remain.
- **Primary relationship:** ordinary wood suitable for modest household, yard, or repair flows.
- **Bark:** available only from an eligible source and cut; never the purpose of the opportunity.
- **Stewardship:** simple, readable instruction from the custodian; protected growth and unmarked stock remain untouched.
- **Logistics:** short recovery and obvious destinations make this the clearest learning pattern.
- **Failure pressure:** wasteful cuts, obstructed shared space, or taking protected stock.
- **Progression use:** Novice foundation; it can remain useful later when local repair or convenience matters.

### W2 — Renewal-stand harvest

**Purpose:** represent planned harvest in a managed stand where present output must preserve future growth.

- **Core judgment:** select eligible growth, respect the renewal pattern, and avoid removing too much from one part of the stand.
- **Primary relationship:** a predictable useful-wood flow for households, markets, and ordinary work.
- **Bark:** source-conditionally guaranteed where eligible; recovery stays integrated with the main cut.
- **Stewardship:** rotation, regrowth, weather condition, and temporary rest periods can alter access.
- **Logistics:** players benefit from knowing which collection and delivery points serve the active work.
- **Failure pressure:** local overcutting, damage to renewal, and poor recovery that turns useful stock into waste.
- **Progression use:** begins at Competent and supports dependable long-term practice without becoming a static tier node.

### W3 — Bank-protection intervention

**Purpose:** connect Woodcutting to Lowmark's maintained water landscape without turning water practice into a player spell system.

- **Core judgment:** remove dangerous, compromised, or obstructive growth while retaining roots and living cover needed for bank stability.
- **Primary relationship:** useful recovered wood; public safety and water access are the first reasons for the work.
- **Bark:** only where the authorized source and recovery condition qualify.
- **Stewardship:** shared-water and bank concerns can close, redirect, or narrow the work. Ordinary stewards identify the need; the player performs Woodcutting.
- **Logistics:** wet ground, awkward recovery, and the need to keep channels or paths clear distinguish the pattern.
- **Failure pressure:** erosion, debris obstruction, damage to protective growth, or fouling shared water.
- **Progression use:** Competent into Advanced; knowledge of site condition matters more than raw cutting power.

### W4 — Damaged-woodland recovery

**Purpose:** recover value after storms, floods, fire exposure, rot, or other ordinary damage while recognizing unsafe or unusable stock.

- **Core judgment:** assess what is safe to approach, what remains useful, what should be left for recovery, and what must be removed as a hazard.
- **Primary relationship:** mixed-condition ordinary wood routed according to suitability; final grades and items remain deferred.
- **Bark:** produced only when the surviving surface and source remain physically usable. Damage never becomes a bark lottery.
- **Stewardship:** closures may persist until conditions are safe; public access and ecological recovery outweigh immediate extraction.
- **Logistics:** unstable ground, obstructed approach, and awkward loads make planning and recovery meaningful.
- **Failure pressure:** injury, wasted haulage, spreading fire or rot risk, and removing material that should remain in the woodland.
- **Progression use:** Advanced; adds assessment and output-routing decisions without creating salvage resources.

### W5 — Public-repair reserve selection

**Purpose:** supply demanding bridge, dike, ferry, mill, building, or other public repair work from scarce managed stock.

- **Core judgment:** match a work brief's broad structural need while leaving unsuitable or strategically reserved growth intact.
- **Primary relationship:** ordinary wood selected and delivered for a defined civic sink. No final construction item is created here.
- **Bark:** retained as a co-output when eligible and routed separately to tanning supply.
- **Stewardship:** public demand may reserve sound stock, suspend market cutting, or redirect deliveries. Exact issuing authority remains open.
- **Logistics:** selection, safe extraction, load handling, and destination choice matter together.
- **Failure pressure:** consuming rare repair stock for lesser uses, delivering unsuitable material, or damaging the reserve that future repairs depend on.
- **Progression use:** Advanced into Masterful; connects skill knowledge directly to Lowmark's repair culture.

### W6 — Constrained selective extraction

**Purpose:** provide the deepest repeatable opportunity by combining difficult access, narrow stewardship tolerances, source assessment, and destination planning.

- **Core judgment:** plan a low-impact extraction where terrain, nearby infrastructure, protected growth, weather, fire risk, or recovery distance sharply limits acceptable methods.
- **Primary relationship:** useful ordinary wood whose value depends on reaching the right downstream use without excessive loss.
- **Bark:** guaranteed only on an eligible source/action and still subordinate to the main work.
- **Stewardship:** access can require demonstrated competence or an active need. This is earned trust, not a magical lock or consumable permit.
- **Logistics:** route knowledge, preparation, and recovery planning are central. Exact routes remain unselected.
- **Failure pressure:** damage to the source area, loss of recoverable stock, blocked passage, or violation of an active closure.
- **Progression use:** Masterful and Practical Mastery; its difficulty comes from combined constraints rather than a superior species.

## Progression bands

| Band | Player relationship to the work | Primary opportunity emphasis | New knowledge or choice | Economic role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Novice** | Follows clear markings and a local work brief. Learns to inspect before cutting, recover usable wood, and recognize that some growth must remain. | W1; sheltered examples of W2 | Eligible versus protected source; complete recovery; basic destination distinction; bark appears only when plainly eligible. | Supplies modest household and repair flows. Learns that wood has uses beyond sale. |
| **Competent** | Selects among several valid sources and adjusts work to renewal or bank condition. | W2 and W3; W1 remains useful | Rotation, regrowth, bank stability, changing access, co-output eligibility, and basic delivery choice. | Reliably supplies ordinary wood and some bark without creating ecological pressure for bark. |
| **Advanced** | Diagnoses damaged sources and meets more specific public needs. Preparation and route choice begin to affect whether work is worthwhile. | W3, W4, and W5 | Safe recovery, source-condition assessment, structural-stock priority, destination matching, and closure interpretation. | Connects woodland work to public repair, tannery supply, and appropriate fuel-worker feed without treating all wood as interchangeable. |
| **Masterful** | Balances competing claims across difficult work: future stock, public repair, bank safety, household need, fuel demand, and recovery cost. | W5 and W6; harder states of W2–W4 | Multi-constraint planning, low-impact extraction, route logistics, judgment under shortage, and responsible refusal of unsuitable work. | Stabilizes shortages and high-demand repairs through knowledge rather than dramatically larger output. |
| **Practical Mastery** | Can read an entire work situation and choose the smallest responsible intervention that satisfies the important need. | Integrated variants of W3–W6 | Combines stewardship, source condition, extraction, sorting, destination, and repair urgency. Practical Mastery is a functional culmination around the approved mastery point, not a unique species, tool, or output. Progress toward level 99 can emphasize refinement, unusual constraint combinations, trusted civic work, efficient recovery, and identity without withholding a required material.

## Action architecture

Every archetype uses a subset of one compact sequence:

1. **Read the work context** — visible condition, work purpose, custodian instruction, and active closures.
2. **Select an eligible source** — determine what should be cut and what must remain.
3. **Prepare the work** — choose a safe approach and protect nearby growth, bank, path, or structure as appropriate.
4. **Perform the cut** — the familiar Woodcutting core.
5. **Recover and sort** — preserve usable wood and eligible bark instead of leaving needless waste.
6. **Choose a destination** — retain, sell, deliver to public repair, or offer acceptable feedstock to fuel workers where the opportunity permits.

Not every opportunity needs six separate clicks. These are design responsibilities, and several may be expressed through readable setup, contextual selection, or the completion result. Later interaction design should preserve judgment without turning work into paperwork.

### Bark anti-grind enforcement

Later content and tuning must preserve all of the following:

1. The main work remains worthwhile for its ordinary wood and world purpose without bark.
2. Bark eligibility is a visible property of the source and authorized work, not a hidden chance.
3. Bark shares the opportunity's access, closure, and recovery conditions.
4. A player cannot repeatedly strip bark while leaving the underlying work unresolved.
5. Ordinary forestry and yard recovery also supply bark, preventing player Woodcutting from becoming the sole tannery bottleneck.
6. Scarcity may come from eligible source mix, responsible closures, and competing demand, not low drop odds.

## Stewardship without management simulation

Stewardship should be legible at the opportunity:

- visible marks or a concise work brief identify why work is permitted;
- nearby condition communicates flood, bank, fire, damage, or renewal concerns;
- a closure removes or changes an opportunity rather than asking the player to administer a forest;
- one destination choice may express a real tradeoff, but the player does not schedule crews, set quotas, or maintain spreadsheets;
- completed public work can alter visible repair activity or local dialogue without requiring permanent world simulation.

The design should reuse a small set of states: **open**, **purpose-limited**, **reserved**, **recovery closure**, and **unsafe closure**. Exact terminology and implementation remain later decisions.

## Public repair and shortage relationships

Lowmark's crossings, dikes, ferries, mills, boats, buildings, yards, and waterworks create credible wood demand. This proposal does not establish final items or site counts.

- Ordinary demand gives early and mid-band wood a persistent sink.
- Active repair need can temporarily favor W1, W4, or W5 work without changing their material identity.
- A public reserve can make destination choice meaningful: immediate market sale may compete with a visible repair request.
- Fuel workers accept suitable flow after structural and civic claims are considered; they do not consume every harvested source indiscriminately.
- Imported wood and ordinary crews can relieve shortage, so the economy never requires the player to solve every closure.

## Logistics and knowledge-based efficiency

Efficiency can grow through:

- knowing which opportunity currently serves the needed output;
- combining delivery of ordinary wood and bark without a second route;
- selecting a suitable destination before beginning difficult recovery;
- avoiding closed or unsuitable sources;
- recognizing when damaged wood is not worth hauling;
- aligning public-repair and fuel-worker deliveries with ordinary market trips;
- preparing for wet ground, difficult access, or safe load recovery.

These are future content dimensions. They do not authorize exact routes, carry capacities, inventory friction, or travel times.

## Provisional decisions and alternatives

### P-W01 — Six reusable opportunity archetypes

**Provisional recommendation:** use W1–W6 as the bounded production vocabulary.

- **Alternative:** merge bank intervention and damaged recovery. This lowers content count but loses the difference between protecting living infrastructure and assessing already damaged stock.
- **Alternative:** split public repair by bridge, dike, ferry, mill, and boat. This adds reskinned tasks and exceeds the needed content burden.

**Reason:** six patterns allow different judgments and placements while sharing actions, assets, outputs, and interfaces.

### P-W02 — Work-context progression rather than species tiers

**Provisional recommendation:** unlock harder combinations of stewardship, condition, logistics, and destination need. Species may later create physical variation but cannot carry progression alone.

- **Alternative:** conventional species ladder. It is readable but fails the anti-reskin rule and pressures the material catalog.
- **Alternative:** fully dynamic woodland simulation. It may create depth but is unsuitable for a solo developer and hides authored progression.

### P-W03 — Brief-and-closure access language

**Provisional recommendation:** communicate access through local work briefs, visible markings, and a small closure-state vocabulary. Do not create permit inventory items.

- **Alternative:** universal free harvest. It weakens Lowmark stewardship and removes decision structure.
- **Alternative:** detailed legal reputation and licensing. It prematurely resolves governance and adds administration without a proven play benefit.

### P-W04 — Destination choice as a bounded mastery layer

**Provisional recommendation:** where meaningful, let harvested wood flow to a general market, an active public repair claim, or fuel-worker intake. Use a maximum of one clear decision at completion or delivery.

- **Alternative:** automatic routing. Simpler, but loses economic understanding and useful knowledge-based efficiency.
- **Alternative:** granular grade sorting and contracts. It creates inventory and management bloat before wood states exist.

## Readiness result

Woodcutting has a coherent long-form identity based on stewardship, source judgment, recovery, civic demand, and logistics. Six opportunity archetypes are sufficient to demonstrate progression without final species, resource tiers, or bark-only content.

Later node production must define which archetypes receive concrete representations, readable eligibility, and placement classes. It must continue to defer exact species, levels, yields, XP, timings, tools, routes, and site counts.
