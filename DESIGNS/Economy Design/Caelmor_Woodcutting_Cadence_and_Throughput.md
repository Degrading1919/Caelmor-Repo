# Caelmor — Woodcutting Cadence and Throughput

**Status:** Proposal-stage analytical balance following approval of WM1–WM8, their XP calibration, and their unlock map
**Scope:** Complete-loop rhythm, attention, sustainable availability, output-event pressure, and session behavior; not runtime timing or final item yield

## 1. Cadence policy

This pass keeps the approved fixed action XP and calibrated loop centers. The timing ranges are prototype tolerances around those centers. They do not define animations, tool speeds, server ticks, exact distances, or source respawn.

Knowledge saves time by rejecting invalid growth, reading rotation and bank condition sooner, choosing the smallest responsible intervention, ordering a route, preparing the right destination, and avoiding duplicate handling. It never grants more XP for the same action and never turns stewardship into indiscriminate fast cutting.

An **ordinary-wood event** is a completed work relationship, not a final stack quantity. A **bark event** exists only when the selected source and legitimate action physically produce usable tannin-rich bark. The bark ranges below describe the mix of qualifying source contexts across sustained play; they are not drop chances.

## 3. Sustainable availability

Sustainable share is a plausible share of engaged time in the relevant band when a player prefers that method. It is not a cooldown, scheduled closure, or rotating task. Invalid conditions redirect the player to another legitimate method.

| Method | Availability class | Sustainable engaged-time share when preferred | World basis |
|---|---|---:|---|
| WM1 | Durable / routinely repeatable | 80–100% | Household maintenance and ordinary receiving are common, though active use or unsafe fall zones may close individual work |
| WM2 | Durable managed supply | 70–100% | Renewal sections rotate through legitimate work; retained growth and fire/wet conditions still matter |
| WM3 | Route- and source-condition-limited | 50–80% | Bank condition and minimum intervention must justify work; not every visible obstruction should be cut |
| WM4 | Route-limited, durable advanced supply | 65–80% | Several valid renewal sections must align along a credible route |
| WM5 | Authored-condition-limited | 10–20% | Flood, wind, fire, rot, obstruction, or collapse must create bounded damage; nonproductive cleanup remains valid |
| WM6 | Demand- and trust/access-limited | 20–30% before 80; 5–10% in broad post-80 play | A disclosed public repair demand and matching reserve source must coexist |
| WM7 | Compound opportunity | 10–20% | Several real constraints must coincide; familiarity cannot manufacture them |
| WM8 | Compound route opportunity | 65–75% post-80 | Household, renewal, and bank work plus shared receiving must all be valid; the approved 80% XP share is about 73% of engaged time |

These ranges match the approved sustainable knowledgeable progression without relying on waiting, random closures, respawn throttles, or fake travel.

## 8. Downstream pressure

| Flow | Current sinks | Pressure | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| WM1/WM2 ordinary wood | Household/public repair, market, storage, accepted fuel-worker flow, later approved uses | **UNRESOLVED UNTIL DOWNSTREAM QUANTITIES** | Multiple legitimate sinks exist, but event yield and consumption ratios do not |
| WM4 ordinary wood | Same sinks with consolidated route throughput | **HIGH** | Roughly 31–34 events/h can oversupply if every event yields generously |
| WM8 ordinary wood | Same sinks | **HIGH** | About 46 sustainable weighted events/h and 52–55 during continuous circuit play are the clearest economy risk |
| Tannin-rich bark | Player Leatherworking, ordinary tanners, trade | **HIGH / UNRESOLVED UNTIL DOWNSTREAM QUANTITIES** | Bark eligibility mix, bark-per-event, tanning consumption, and tannery competition are unset |
| Public repair / reserve supply | Disclosed repair demand and custody | **MANAGEABLE** | Demand-limited work has a real sink and cannot absorb unlimited bulk wood |
| Charcoal feedstock | Ordinary fuel workers, then Smithing construction/refurbishment | **UNRESOLVED UNTIL DOWNSTREAM QUANTITIES** | Conversion ratio, accepted feedstock share, workshop fuel demand, and ordinary forestry competition remain open |
| Baseline market outlet | Lawful ordinary purchase/trade | **MANAGEABLE** | Prevents stranding if baseline value is modest; it must not make WM8 the universal money method |

If wood or bark overwhelms plausible demand, the control order is: reduce item quantity per event, verify sustainable source density and route composition, set processing ratios, establish legitimate repair/fuel demand, then tune market value. Do not add junk sinks, superior wood tiers, bark-only work, or artificial closures.

## 9. Verification requirements

### Map verification required

- WM1 household-to-receiving proximity and safe work spacing.
- WM2 rotation density and whether wood/bark destinations create understandable choices.
- WM3 bank geography, protected recovery, and public destination burden.
- WM4 three-section route: 335–365 baseline seconds must remain credible without compressed world scale.
- WM6 reserve-to-repair delivery and custody readability.
- WM8 household, renewal, bank, and shared receiving relationship: 198–218 baseline seconds is acceptable only if exact placement supports it.

### Playtesting required

- Whether WM1/WM2 stable cutting windows feel comfortable rather than automatic.
- Whether source eligibility can be read without repeated menu checking.
- Whether WM3/WM5/WM7 diagnosis is clear enough to reward knowledge.
- Whether WM4 and WM8 decision frequency feels deliberate rather than click-dense.
- Whether destination visits create banking choices instead of inventory chores.
- Whether continuous WM8 play is economically sustainable after actual item yields and sink quantities exist.

No Creative Director decision is required to carry this cadence foundation into prototype and downstream quantity work. WM8 throughput is a high-priority tuning and map-verification issue with bounded remedies; it does not require a new material, method, or artificial throttle.
