# Caelmor — Lowmark Node-Content Blueprint

**Status:** Proposal only

**Scope:** Lowmark v1 gathering and supply economy
**Purpose:** Define the minimum authored opportunity patterns required before schemas, nodes, balance, species, and exact placement are designed.

## Fixed boundaries

This blueprint carries forward the approved material, inventory-state, processing, Hunting, Woodcutting, and ordinary-supply architecture. It introduces no material, skill, species, exact level, yield, rate, tool tier, route, site count, or map fact. "Node" here means a later content representation of a relationship, not necessarily a stationary object.

The content set must support five progression bands: **Novice, Competent, Advanced, Masterful, and Practical Mastery**. Progression comes from changing relationships among source condition, behavior, access, stewardship, preparation, recovery, logistics, civic demand, and output decisions. It does not require five visual tiers of the same interaction.

## Minimum pattern count

The minimum coherent proposal contains:

- **6 Hunting opportunity archetypes**;
- **6 managed Woodcutting opportunity archetypes**;
- **6 ordinary supply and custody interface patterns**;
- **5 cross-cutting authored condition patterns** reused across those opportunities.

This is a vocabulary of **18 world opportunity/interface patterns plus 5 reusable condition patterns**, not 23 unique pieces of content. H5 modifies recovery with a grounded hazard, H6 wraps a known Hunting grammar in a stewardship purpose, and W6 recombines known Woodcutting constraints at mastery. These compound patterns do not require standalone sites, species, mechanics, or interfaces. Several patterns may share a placement class and interface logic. One pattern may receive more than one authored instance only when habitat, civic purpose, or progression changes the decision it presents.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| W4 | Damaged-woodland recovery | Distinguish usable recovery from unsafe or ecologically needed material, clear selectively, route outputs | Tangible ordinary wood when serviceable; conditional bark; no salvage-resource category | Advanced assessment under irregular conditions | Storm-, flood-, or fire-affected managed woodland | Recovery brief and safety closure; not an invitation to strip damaged land | Keep/bank/trade or supply repair/fuel demand according to condition | Damage type, contamination, retained habitat, logistics |

No later node may expose bark as a separate target. Bark eligibility belongs to the selected source and action; if bark becomes the dominant reason to repeat an otherwise pointless cut, the content has failed this blueprint.

## Cross-cutting authored condition patterns

| ID | Condition pattern | What it changes | What it must not become |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Open / ordinary availability | Baseline access, ordinary stock, normal service, and dependable baseline trade for lawful transferable goods | Infinite supply or unlimited high-value purchasing |
| C2 | Guided / purpose-limited | Identifies the local reason and acceptable action for work | Permit-item grind or hidden governance simulation |
| C3 | Reserved / commissioned | Directs an opportunity or output toward an active household, civic, or stewardship need | Contract-management system or compulsory public work |
| C4 | Disrupted / recovery | Temporarily changes source, service, or stock availability and points to another valid response | Continuous price simulation or mandatory chore |
| C5 | Unsafe / closed | Prevents work when land, water, fire, contamination, or recovery makes it irresponsible | Arbitrary timer gate or unexplained node disappearance |

The condition vocabulary is shared across content. A normal authored instance needs its ordinary state and **at most one** meaningful altered state unless it is story-critical. No archetype must implement every condition. Content production should prefer visible cause, a clear alternate response, and bounded duration or story resolution.

## Progression coverage matrix

| Band | Hunting patterns carrying the band | Woodcutting patterns carrying the band | Supply/interface learning | Required change in player judgment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Novice | H1; introductory H2 | W1 | S1, basic S5/S6 purchase and storage | Recognize eligibility, ordinary custody, and safe recovery |
| Competent | H2; introductory H3 | W2 | S2 and market choice | Choose self-supply, conversion, or purchase for a clear purpose |
| Advanced | H3, H4; introductory H5 | W3, W4 | S3/S4 and disruption response | Prepare for habitat/source condition, preserve value, route output |
| Masterful | H5 | W5 | Integrated storage, repair, receiving, and several supply paths | Anticipate risk, competing claims, repair demand, and custody |
| Practical Mastery | H6 plus recombined earlier patterns | W6 plus recombined earlier patterns | S6 commission and refurbishment relationships | Solve a purpose-bound problem efficiently without requiring a new material tier |

Practical Mastery content must recombine known grammar under a meaningful purpose. It must not be a denser cluster of identical sources or a rare-color replacement.

### Knowledge and mastery payoff contract

Every Advanced, Masterful, or Practical Mastery instance must deliver at least one reusable nonnumeric payoff:

- earlier diagnosis of a valid opportunity;
- avoidance of invalid, wasteful, closed, or unsafe work;
- safer or cleaner recovery that preserves an approved output relationship;
- combination of source, processing, delivery, or repair needs into a more efficient journey;
- access to purpose-limited work because the player understands and satisfies its stewardship conditions.

These payoffs use existing outputs and relationships. They cannot introduce a mastery-only material, superior hide or wood grade, arbitrary credential item, or compulsory daily task.

## Placement requirements

Later node/content production assigns each opportunity to one of the eight approved broad placement classes:

1. managed working woodland;
2. plausible hunting habitat;
3. agricultural household zone;
4. settlement-edge service and dirty-work zone;
5. fuel-work zone;
6. market and receiving area;
7. repair and craft workshop cluster;
8. storage and civic-infrastructure support.

Every assignment must state why the relationship belongs there, what visible labor or environmental clue supports it, and which other class receives or consumes its output. Exact coordinates, travel time, routes, river links, and site counts remain **MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED**.

## Variation budget

An authored instance deserves separate content only when at least one of these changes the player decision:

- source or target behavior;
- habitat or source condition;
- legitimate cutting or hunting purpose;
- access, closure, or stewardship state;
- preparation or recovery problem;
- output eligibility or condition;
- destination and competing civic demand;
- logistics between source, processor, storage, and sink;
- evidence that lets an experienced player act more efficiently.

Visual difference, a new name, a larger number, or a different placement alone is insufficient.

## Production blueprint per later opportunity

Each later content record or design sheet must answer:

1. Which archetype and progression band does it serve?
2. What world condition makes the action legitimate?
3. What does the player read before acting?
4. Which approved verb sequence is used?
5. Which outputs are primary, conditional guaranteed, or relationship-only?
6. What can close or alter the opportunity, and how is that communicated?
7. Which processor, market, store, workshop, or civic sink receives its value?
8. What knowledge improves the player's preparation, route, recovery, or destination choice?
9. What existing pattern, asset, or interface can be reused?
10. What prevents the opportunity from becoming XP-only repetition?

If these questions cannot be answered without a new material, skill, or unsupported map fact, the candidate returns to design review rather than entering production.

Read, Prepare, Approach, Resolve, Recover and the Woodcutting work sequence are **authoring responsibilities**, not a requirement for a separate click, interface, or animation at every phase. Later interaction design should expose only the decisions that matter in that instance and compress the rest.

## Scope and redundancy audit

| Test | Result | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Long-form identity | **STRONG** | Hunting and Woodcutting each have six patterns whose decisions change across five bands. |
| Catalog restraint | **STRONG** | The blueprint commits to relationship patterns, not species, tree tiers, or one object per inventory state. |
| Ordinary-world credibility | **STRONG** | Six interface patterns keep households and workers productive without player intervention. |
| Source-to-sink coverage | **STRONG** | Every represented state reaches conversion, crafting, maintenance, trade, or storage through an identified family. |
| Solo-developer feasibility | **STRONG** | Twelve gathering archetypes reuse two compact action grammars; six interfaces reuse custody verbs and four condition states. |
| Map readiness | **PARTIAL BY DESIGN** | Placement classes and evidence requirements are defined; exact placement awaits map verification. |
| Balance readiness | **PARTIAL BY DESIGN** | Rates, quantities, levels, prices, and site counts are deliberately deferred. |
| Species and wood catalog | **DEFERRED WITHOUT BLOCKING** | Final names are unnecessary to prove the opportunity and economic logic. |

## Node-production gate

Proposal-stage node and content production may begin when the integrated pack passes its hostile audit. Production must instantiate the approved relationships; it may not invent new economic logic to compensate for a weak node idea.
