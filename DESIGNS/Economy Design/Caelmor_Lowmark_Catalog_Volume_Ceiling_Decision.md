# Caelmor Lowmark v1 Catalog-Volume Ceiling Decision

**Status:** Proposal for Creative Director approval

**Scope:** Player-facing Lowmark v1 item, material, equipment, tool, food, conversion, construction, maintenance, and repair identities

## 1. Recommendation

Use the following planning envelope:

| Catalog | Target range | Hard planning ceiling |
|---|---:|---:|
| Player-facing item identities | **40–55** | **64** |
| Player-facing recipe or service identities | **24–36** | **48** |

The counts are separate. A recipe does not require a new intermediate item, and an item does not require a dedicated single-use recipe.

The governing objective is:

> RuneScape-like relationship density, Caelmor-sized catalog.

The legacy 102-item catalog is not an authority, floor, target, or source of automatic entries. The recommended envelope is built from the current Lowmark foundation: ten represented equipment and wood states, edible and Cooking needs, four equipment paths, Smithing, Leatherworking, ordinary conversions, repair, commissions, tools where they materially change play, and ordinary trade.

## 2. Counting policy

### Count as an item identity

- a transferable inventory state;
- a distinct equipped object;
- a player-carried tool whose ownership changes access, method, preparation, maintenance, or choice;
- a raw or prepared food whose separate identity creates a real Cooking, storage, consumption, or trade decision.

### Do not count as an item identity

- workshop-only process states;
- heated, cut, fitted, drying, inspected, or partially assembled work;
- provenance, custody, quality, condition, or fitting records;
- an output relationship before its actual inventory representation is approved;
- NPC services, market categories, node types, or opportunity archetypes;
- cosmetic text variants with identical function and economy role.

### Count as a recipe or service identity

- a player-facing transformation with a distinct input, output, physical purpose, and progression role;
- a construction order that produces a distinct equipment identity;
- a repair or refurbishment operation whose damage class and material demand differ meaningfully;
- an ordinary-labor conversion that the player can commission, supply, purchase, or route through the economy.

### Do not count as a recipe or service identity

- every arrow in a workshop process;
- hidden preparation steps;
- a duplicate recipe distinguished only by level, color, quality adjective, or quantity;
- internal thread, panel, padding, blank, plate, strap, lining, or repair-kit assembly;
- a separate repair entry for every equipment object when one condition-driven operation covers the same physical work.

## 3. Why the range is sufficient

The target reserves room for the required foundations without authorizing a catalog in advance:

| Planning category | Expected envelope | Basis |
|---|---:|---|
| Approved material and supply states | **10 items** | Nine equipment-chain states plus ordinary wood |
| Food and Cooking representation | **8–12 items** | A bounded set of recoverable food identities and prepared outputs sufficient to create consumption and trade decisions |
| Major equipment path representation | **16–22 items** | Four paths across four major milestones, with limited headroom for physically necessary form or armor-piece representation |
| Player-carried tools | **2–5 items** | Only tools that change access, method, preparation, or maintenance; workshops cover ordinary fixed implements |
| Bounded functional headroom | **4–8 items** | Justified omissions discovered during Cooking, equipment-form, storage, or market integration |

These envelopes overlap in final planning and are not quotas to fill. Their upper ends total more than the 55-item target because final equipment form, food representation, and tool ownership must compete for the same budget. That competition is intentional.

The 64-item ceiling allows a bounded correction if final armor-piece representation or the minimum coherent Cooking loop requires more identities. It is not an invitation to add nine unused entries after reaching 55.

## 4. Required v1 catalog categories

### 4.1 Approved material foundation

The following ten states are **REQUIRED**:

1. Plain carbon steel stock.
2. Raw hide.
3. Vegetable-tanned leather.
4. Flax bundle.
5. Flax tow.
6. Linen textile.
7. Tannin-rich bark.
8. Wood charcoal.
9. Rendered tallow.
10. Ordinary wood.

No additional metal, leather, textile, bark, charcoal, tallow, or ordinary-wood grade is required for progression.

### 4.2 Food and Cooking

A **minimum coherent food layer is REQUIRED**, because edible Hunting output is the largest unresolved source-to-sink flow.

The later food pass must select the smallest set that can:

- receive the approved light, ordinary mixed, and full-recovery relationships without creating one item per animal or body part;
- provide meaningful Cooking transformations;
- distinguish at least a few consumption, preparation, storage, or trade purposes;
- absorb Hunting output through real use rather than an unlimited disposal buyer;
- remain useful at later progression without becoming a linear ladder of larger healing numbers.

This decision reserves **8–12 item identities** and a bounded portion of the recipe envelope for food. It does not name meats, dishes, cuts, ingredients, or quantities.

### 4.3 Equipment forms

Representation for the four approved paths is **REQUIRED**:

- Keeper's Blade;
- Crestbridge Guard;
- Bridgewarden Harness;
- Riverpath Leathers.

Each path needs enough finished equipment identity to express the four major milestones: dependable field equipment, declared working identity, proven specialist equipment, and aspirational commission.

The default planning assumption is **one meaningful finished identity per path per milestone**, or sixteen equipment identities. A path may use fewer when one object can express progression through an approved commission or refurbishment state without losing player clarity. It may use more only when final slot or form decisions create materially distinct equipped objects.

Exact weapon form, handedness, armor-piece breakdown, guard slot, and loadout compatibility remain unresolved. Their resolution must fit inside the shared catalog envelope.

### 4.4 Tools

Only player-carried tools that change play are **REQUIRED when demonstrated**. A tool earns an item identity when owning, choosing, preparing, maintaining, or bringing it changes access or method.

Workshop hammers, needles, knives, vats, looms, ovens, and similar ordinary implements may remain station or service context when the player makes no meaningful inventory choice about them. No tool ladder is required simply because a skill spans 99 levels.

### 4.5 Repair and commissions

Repair and commission operations are **REQUIRED systems** but do not justify separate material catalogs.

- Repair consumes represented steel, charcoal, leather, linen, tow, tallow, or ordinary wood according to actual damage.
- Raw hide, bark, and flax bundles remain upstream inputs through their approved conversions.
- Aspirational commissions use ordinary materials with better fitting, workmanship, integration, inspection, custody, and repair standards.
- Repair kits, commission tokens, masterwork substances, generic components, and per-item scrap states remain unnecessary.

## 5. Entry classification

### REQUIRED for v1

| Entry class | Reason |
|---|---|
| Ten approved material and ordinary-wood states | They already carry distinct trade, conversion, construction, repair, or supply choices |
| Bounded raw/recoverable food representation | Hunting needs actual Cooking and consumption destinations |
| Bounded prepared-food outputs | Cooking requires useful transformations and recurring demand |
| Finished equipment identities for the four paths and major milestones | Progression needs readable functional destinations and aspirational attachment |
| Physically distinct equipped pieces required by final armor/loadout rules | The player must be able to equip, compare, maintain, and repair the actual form |
| Distinct player-carried gathering or craft tools when ownership changes method | Access and preparation need an inventory identity only when the choice is real |
| Player-facing tanning, construction, Cooking, repair, and ordinary-conversion operations | These connect sources to sinks and support self-supply, trade, and commissions |

### JUSTIFIED but optional

| Entry class | Admission condition |
|---|---|
| Additional prepared-food role | It creates a distinct preparation, consumption, preservation, routing, or trade choice rather than a stronger-number replacement |
| Additional equipment form within an approved path | It creates a different combat or utility identity and does not duplicate a milestone lesson |
| Additional player-carried tool | It changes method, access, preparation, maintenance, or risk rather than adding a stat tier |
| Wool felt | An approved final form proves linen and tow cannot satisfy its physical damping, lining, or weather need |
| Seasoned hardwood equipment input | An approved weapon or guard form requires rigid nonmetal structure that steel cannot appropriately provide |
| Safe recovered steel using the existing stock identity | Inspection, loss, access, and a real reuse decision are established; no separate scrap tier is created |
| Limited ordinary trade good | The player handles it directly and it passes the five-part test; scenery and economic implication are insufficient |

### DEFER

| Entry class | Reason |
|---|---|
| Ranged equipment, ammunition, and Fletching catalog | S01 remains unresolved |
| Later-region materials and equipment | Lowmark is the only full v1 region |
| Exact species-specific Hunting drops | Final fauna and Cooking representation must prove separate uses first |
| Exact wood species or worked-lumber forms | Ordinary wood already carries the approved Lowmark relationships |
| Final tool set | Must follow actual action, access, and station interaction design |
| Quest-only evidence, records, keys, and temporary objects | Narrative implementation must justify them; they do not consume the economy catalog budget by default |
| Optional felt or hardwood | Their physical need remains unproven |

### REJECT as redundant or filler

- separate raw-meat identities for every eligible animal without different Cooking or trade roles;
- separate cuts, organs, horns, bones, sinew, trophies, or fat items without demonstrated sinks;
- tree-species logs, bark grades, fuelwood grades, lumber tiers, or decorative wood variants without distinct physical uses;
- iron-to-steel progression ladders, named steel grades, prestige alloys, or fantasy metals;
- hide species tiers, leather quality tiers, masterwork leather, or prestige tanning agents;
- charcoal grades, linen grades, tow grades, tallow grades, or superior versions of ordinary process goods;
- blanks, plates, thread, cut panels, padding packages, strap kits, lining kits, fitted subassemblies, and generic work-in-progress items;
- repair kits, maintenance tokens, scrap currencies, commission seals as consumables, and capstone ingredients;
- cooked-food ladders distinguished only by larger recovery values;
- disposable XP recipes, duplicate conversions, and vendor-trash outputs;
- items created solely to fill an unlock level or make a source table look varied.

## 6. Recipe and service envelope

The **24–36 target** supports:

- one leather-preparation relationship with later tuning variants expressed through process control rather than new recipes;
- the four equipment paths across major milestones;
- a bounded Cooking set;
- ordinary flax, charcoal, and tallow conversion interfaces;
- condition-driven hard and flexible repair/refurbishment operations;
- maintenance or commission variants only where their player-facing procedure differs materially.

The same recipe may accept context, fitting, or damage parameters without becoming several catalog identities. Aspirational commissions remain advanced variants of their construction family, though each distinct finished equipment result still needs a clear player-facing order.

The **48-recipe ceiling** exists for final Cooking and equipment-form resolution. It must not be consumed by exposing workshop steps or duplicating one recipe at successive levels.

## 7. Relationship-density audit

Depth already comes from relationships rather than catalog volume:

| Depth source | Current foundation |
|---|---|
| Multiple uses | Steel, leather, linen, tow, tallow, wood, and charcoal cross construction, repair, maintenance, ordinary work, or trade |
| Processing | Hide and bark become leather; flax becomes linen and tow; wood can feed charcoal; animal-processing flow can become tallow |
| Repair | The same represented materials recur according to damage class instead of becoming repair kits |
| Trade | Player gathering, ordinary suppliers, imports, processors, markets, storage, and workshops coexist |
| Routing | Hunting recovery and Woodcutting custody determine whether goods go to food, tanning, rendering, repair, fuel, market, or storage |
| Self-supply | Players may gather and process selected inputs without being forced to master every supplying skill |
| Commissions | Aspirational work increases fitting, workmanship, access, and provenance demands without unique substances |
| Method choice | HM1–HM8 and WM1–WM8 share a compact output vocabulary while differing in evidence, ecology, stewardship, risk, purpose, and efficiency |

Adding items is therefore not the primary way to add depth. A new item must open or clarify a relationship that the current identities cannot express.

## 8. Ordinary wood decision

Keep **one ordinary-wood inventory identity** for Lowmark v1.

Managed source, condition, custody, and destination create Woodcutting variety. They do not automatically create different log items. Ordinary wood can support household and civic repair, market sale, storage, and fuel-worker conversion while tannin-rich bark remains the conditional woodland co-output.

A second wood state requires an approved physical need that ordinary wood cannot represent, such as a final equipment form requiring prepared rigid structure with its own trade and repair behavior. Species flavor alone is insufficient.

## 9. Food and Cooking risk

Food is the strongest reason the catalog may approach the upper target rather than remain near forty items.

The food pass must avoid two failures:

1. **Overcompression:** one generic edible item erases ecology, Cooking progression, preparation, and trade choice.
2. **Species proliferation:** every Hunting expression creates raw meat, cuts, organs, fats, and several cooked variants.

The bounded 8–12 item reservation should be tested against HM1–HM8 throughput, ordinary food supply, Cooking progression, consumption rate, storage, and market demand. If it cannot produce meaningful choices, the first response is to revise representation and recipe relationships. It is not automatic permission to exceed 64 items.

## 10. Equipment and tool risk

Equipment is the largest fixed identity block. The four-path, four-milestone structure suggests sixteen finished identities before armor-piece splitting or optional forms.

Final equipment-form decisions should prefer:

- one readable object where one object carries the function;
- separate equipped pieces only when slot, use, repair, choice, or presentation materially differs;
- milestone progression through function and workmanship rather than parallel grade variants;
- no cosmetic recolor or provenance variation as a new economy item.

Tools create the second common bloat route. A different required level, better speed, or new material name is insufficient by itself. If a workshop or action can communicate tool context without a player inventory decision, no item is needed.

## 11. Five-part admission test

Every proposed item must pass all five questions:

1. **Purpose:** What player-facing physical, economic, combat, utility, preparation, or narrative function requires representation?
2. **Source:** How does it enter the Lowmark economy through gathering, conversion, trade, commission, or justified authored acquisition?
3. **Sink:** What consumes, equips, maintains, transforms, exchanges, or retires it?
4. **Progression role:** What does it teach, enable, specialize, connect, or preserve across progression?
5. **Non-redundancy:** Why can no approved item, parameter, condition, service, or workshop-only state express the same relationship equally well?

Failure on any question means **DEFER** or **REJECT**, not “add now and find a use later.”

Recipes and services use the same test, replacing source with required inputs and sink with output or restored function.

## 12. Conditions for exceeding the ceiling

Exceeding **64 items** or **48 recipes/services** requires all of the following:

1. a required v1 function remains unrepresented after consolidation;
2. the proposed entry passes the five-part admission test;
3. an existing item, parameterized recipe, condition state, service, or workshop-only step cannot express it;
4. source-to-sink throughput shows that it will not create a dead end or filler sink;
5. the addition does not resolve pressure by inventing grades, tiers, drops, or disposable recipes;
6. asset, interface, persistence, balance, localization, and QA burden remains acceptable for a solo developer;
7. the catalog is re-audited for entries that can be merged or removed;
8. the Creative Director explicitly approves the exception.

An expansion, later region, or resolved S01 decision should receive its own catalog envelope. It should not consume hidden Lowmark v1 headroom.

## 13. Main risks and controls

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Food catalog multiplies by species and cuts | Reserve a bounded functional food set and require distinct Cooking or consumption roles |
| Armor is split into many pieces before slot rules exist | Resolve equipped form and repair meaning before assigning item identities |
| Tool tiers imitate a material ladder | Require a method, access, preparation, or maintenance difference |
| Repair creates kits, scraps, and per-item recipes | Consume represented materials through damage-class operations |
| Ordinary wood becomes species and grade catalogs | Retain one broad state until a missing physical function is demonstrated |
| Milestones create parallel item variants | One meaningful identity per path per milestone is the default, not a minimum quota |
| Ordinary labor produces separate intermediate goods | Keep thread, padding, tanning liquor, blanks, panels, and work in progress internal |
| Legacy names return because content slots appear available | Apply the current five-part test; legacy presence grants no authority |
| Ceiling becomes a target to fill | Treat the lower range as success when all functions are covered |

## 14. Creative Director decision required

Approve or revise:

- **Target item range:** 40–55.
- **Hard item ceiling:** 64.
- **Target recipe/service range:** 24–36.
- **Hard recipe/service ceiling:** 48.
- **Exception rule:** all eight conditions in Section 12, including explicit Creative Director approval.

Approval closes the final structural catalog-volume decision. It does not authorize item creation, recipe balancing, schemas, JSON, runtime implementation, or use of the legacy catalog.
