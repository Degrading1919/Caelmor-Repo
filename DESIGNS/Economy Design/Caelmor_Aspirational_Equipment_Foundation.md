# Caelmor — Aspirational / BIS Equipment Foundation

**Status:** approved concept-direction proposal. The Creative Director approved Portfolio A and Acquisition A. Working names remain provisional. This document defines equipment purpose and progression identity only; it does not define materials, recipes, nodes, exact statistics, drop tables, schemas, or a level-by-level ladder.

## Authority and boundaries

This proposal uses the reconciled canon, approved Lore Foundation Lock, combat architecture, approved skill and XP progression, and equipment/economy direction.

- Lowmark is the sole full v1 region.
- V1 has no player spellcasting system or spellcasting equipment.
- S01 remains unresolved. Ranged aspirational equipment is deferred until the Creative Director explicitly resolves it.
- Combat remains deliberate, readable, deterministic, and server authoritative. Equipment may support attack, defend, mitigation, positioning, preparation, and equipment-bound ability identities without changing those architectural contracts.
- Aspirational equipment should be function-specific rather than one universally superior loadout.
- Practical mastery sits around level 80. Post-80 progression should favor specialist opportunity, identity, and commemoration rather than mandatory replacement tiers.

## Portfolio shape

The v1 aspirational portfolio contains four destinations:

1. **Keeper's Blade** — dependable melee generalist.
2. **Crestbridge Guard** — active-defense specialist.
3. **Bridgewarden Harness** — sustained heavy protection.
4. **Riverpath Leathers** — flexible light protection.

The portfolio is intentionally compact. The Blade and Guard define combat-action identity; the Harness and Leathers define protection identity. They may support compatible combinations later, but exact slots, allowed combinations, and loadout rules remain unresolved.

## 1. Keeper's Blade — provisional name

| Design field | Approved direction |
|---|---|
| Fantasy and identity | A masterwork Lowmark sidearm whose authority comes from dependable service, documented repair, and disciplined workmanship rather than supernatural origin or forgotten royal ownership. |
| Gameplay purpose | Provide the reliable melee reference point: a readable attack rhythm, consistent commitment, and a clean relationship between attacking, repositioning, and defending. |
| Why players aspire to it | It represents accumulated combat judgment, Smithing competence, responsible maintenance, and earned civic trust. Its appeal comes from being the weapon a knowledgeable player can depend on in varied encounters. |
| Combat or utility niche | General-purpose deliberate melee. It should remain useful across mixed enemy behaviors without erasing specialist weapons that may exist later. |
| Tradeoff or limitation | It does not own exceptional reach, concentrated offense, maximum mitigation, or a supernatural answer to enemy mechanics. Reliability is its specialization. |
| Lore and regional grounding | Lowmark crossing duty, militia service, repair-oriented smithing, market access to conventional metal, and cultural respect for work that remains useful under pressure. |
| Likely progression role | The practical-mastery destination for the balanced one-handed melee path. It should be earned late enough to summarize the player's learned habits without being withheld solely as a level-99 trophy. |
| What earlier equipment must teach | Attack commitment, recovery windows, target choice, basic maintenance, positional discipline, and when to interrupt offense with defense. |

## 2. Crestbridge Guard — provisional name

| Design field | Approved direction |
|---|---|
| Fantasy and identity | A defensive implement associated with holding a crossing and protecting the passage, workers, or people behind the bearer. Its identity is civic responsibility expressed through active defense. |
| Gameplay purpose | Make defending, bracing, and reading telegraphs a complete equipment identity rather than a passive armor check. |
| Why players aspire to it | It rewards composure and encounter knowledge. Mastery is visible in the player's choice of when to commit to defense and when to release it. |
| Combat or utility niche | Predictable high-pressure sequences, prolonged defense, and safe recovery from dangerous enemy patterns. It must remain fully worthwhile for solo play. |
| Tradeoff or limitation | Defensive commitment reduces offensive flexibility. It should not provide constant safety, guaranteed mitigation, or a universal answer to every damage source. |
| Lore and regional grounding | Crestbridge, crossing keepers, bridge defense, public passage obligations, and the Lowmark belief that protection is a duty performed in view of others. |
| Likely progression role | The practical-mastery destination for active defense. It can complement the Keeper's Blade direction if later slot and animation decisions permit, but that pairing is not yet locked. |
| What earlier equipment must teach | Defend timing, cancellation limits, mitigation as an outcome rather than a guarantee, readable telegraphs, recovery, and choosing which pressure to absorb. |

## 3. Bridgewarden Harness — provisional name

| Design field | Approved direction |
|---|---|
| Fantasy and identity | Heavy protective equipment whose status comes from repeated maintenance and public service. It should look repaired, fitted, and trusted rather than pristine or extravagantly enchanted. |
| Gameplay purpose | Support sustained melee participation and measured forgiveness for mistakes through direct protection and dependable mitigation behavior. |
| Why players aspire to it | It visibly represents Smithing mastery, preparation, and the ability to remain useful under pressure. Its attachment value grows from survival and repair history. |
| Combat or utility niche | Prolonged engagements, holding position, and encounters where the player accepts direct pressure rather than relying on constant repositioning. |
| Tradeoff or limitation | Its value is concentrated in direct protection. It should offer less environmental adaptability, distance control, and flexible encounter preparation than the light-protection path. |
| Lore and regional grounding | Lowmark bridge crews, local militias, imported conventional metal, repair smiths, war recovery, and the duty to keep crossings and communities functioning. |
| Likely progression role | The practical-mastery destination for heavy protection. It is a function-specific BIS choice rather than a mandatory upgrade over every lighter setup. |
| What earlier equipment must teach | Basic mitigation, armor upkeep, preparation for prolonged fights, accepting positional commitment, and distinguishing protection from invulnerability. |

## 4. Riverpath Leathers — provisional name

| Design field | Approved direction |
|---|---|
| Fantasy and identity | Light protective wear shaped by ferriers, hunters, field workers, and travelers who must remain capable across wet ground, paths, crossings, and changing conditions. |
| Gameplay purpose | Support positioning, expedition readiness, and flexible preparation while preserving the consequences of lower direct protection. |
| Why players aspire to it | It connects Hunting and Leatherworking mastery with practical knowledge of Lowmark's terrain and encounter conditions. Its strength becomes clearer as the player learns when direct mitigation is unnecessary. |
| Combat or utility niche | Mobile melee, exploration, variable encounters, and situations where avoiding poor commitments matters more than enduring repeated heavy impacts. |
| Tradeoff or limitation | Lower direct mitigation and less tolerance for repeated heavy hits. It must not become the fastest, safest, and most protective choice simultaneously. |
| Lore and regional grounding | Lowmark animal husbandry and hunting byproducts, bounded tanning practice, river travel, field labor, repair, and the cultural value of remaining useful through changing conditions. |
| Likely progression role | The practical-mastery destination for light protection. It stands beside the Bridgewarden Harness as an alternative identity rather than beneath it. |
| What earlier equipment must teach | Spacing, encounter preparation, hazard awareness, maintenance, repositioning, and accepting vulnerability in exchange for flexibility. |

## Approved acquisition model

All four destinations use an **earned commission plus player mastery** structure.

1. **Civic and narrative legitimacy:** participation in Lowmark's story and local obligations establishes why the commission is offered. This is not a random prestigious order or unexplained shop unlock.
2. **Demonstrated equipment understanding:** the player must have used the relevant earlier identity enough for the final concept to summarize learned habits rather than bypass them.
3. **Player contribution:** the player supplies meaningful craft mastery and preparation. The final equipment is neither a pure quest handout nor a single enemy drop.
4. **Skilled fitting and custody:** appropriate Lowmark workers or institutions participate in commissioning, fitting, witnessing, or recording the work. Exact actors and procedures remain later selections.
5. **No supernatural shortcut:** water practice, Riverwarden history, memory, resonance, and relic status do not replace physical craft or player mastery.

The four commissions may share an overall civic logic while retaining different craft and field requirements. They do not need four unrelated capstone quests.

## Portfolio constraints

- No concept receives its identity solely from having the highest numbers.
- No concept is acquired entirely through random drops.
- No concept requires another player.
- No concept requires spellcasting.
- No ranged concept enters v1 while S01 remains open.
- No legacy named resource is automatically required.
- No item is assigned a Founder, Riverwarden, Remnant, or Volkhari craft lineage without separate canon authority.
- Exact special attacks, mitigation rules, movement behavior, durability, repair costs, slots, and balance remain future design work.

- Additional melee weapon categories beyond the balanced Blade path.
- Exact equipment slots and compatible loadouts.
- Exact equipment-bound abilities or special attacks.
- Cap-99 commemorative recognition and post-80 specialist opportunities.
- Durability, repair, binding, trade, and replacement rules.

## Progression implications

- Earlier equipment must teach behavior before the aspirational piece rewards mastery of that behavior.
- Heavy and light protection must remain meaningful alternatives.
- Active defense must be learned through readable commitments rather than granted only at the endpoint.
- Practical mastery is the intended aspirational destination; level 99 remains an identity and completion landmark rather than a compulsory BIS replacement.
- Acquisition must connect combat use, crafting mastery, local trust, and preparation without requiring a full catalog expansion.
