# Caelmor — Lowmark Economy Placement Requirements

**Status:** proposal-stage world integration and placement requirements for Creative Director audit. This document connects approved gathering, ordinary supply, processing, trade, storage, and repair relationships to broad Lowmark placement classes before exact map work. It does not select coordinates, routes, travel times, site counts, final nodes, species, yields, prices, inventories, or implementation data, and it does not amend live lore.

## Authority and placement boundaries

Lowmark is an inhabited repair economy shaped by managed water, agricultural households, crossings, trade, war recovery, and practical stewardship. Its economy should appear as a chain of visible relationships rather than a row of isolated MMO stations.

This proposal preserves:

- Hunting as hunt-and-field-dress work on eligible game, with an edible relationship, conditional guaranteed raw hide, and optional fat flow to ordinary renderers;
- Woodcutting as stewardship-approved selective harvest, with ordinary wood primary, eligible bark as a guaranteed co-output, and suitable wood optionally entering ordinary charcoal supply;
- imported conventional steel stock;
- household flax production and ordinary textile conversion;
- husbandry, tannery, fuel-worker, and renderer labor outside the player skill list;
- Smithing and Leatherworking as the principal player equipment crafts;
- the nine approved equipment-chain inventory states;
- no local ore source invented for Lowmark, no Farming or Textile skill, no bark-only activity, and no player charcoal or tallow production skill;
- map uncertainty, unresolved exact governance, C03/C06/C07, and S01.

## Placement principles

1. **Place causes before interfaces.** Habitat, working woodland, farms, crossings, and repair demand justify where outputs and services appear.
2. **Separate incompatible work.** Clean domestic water, food handling, tanning waste, rendering, smoke, sparks, and bulk receiving have different placement needs.
3. **Show movement without fixing routes.** Covered stock, drying racks, carts, sheds, repair piles, and receiving yards can imply flows while exact roads and waterways remain unknown.
4. **Cluster by practical relationship, not item count.** One market or workshop interface may handle several approved states. There is no world object per inventory item.
5. **Keep ordinary people present.** NPC and household supply must remain visible enough that the player is a participant rather than the only producer in Lowmark.
6. **Make stewardship readable.** Markings, retained growth, closure signs, public repair staging, and separated waste explain why work is permitted or unavailable.
7. **Use disruption as authored variation.** Flood, fire risk, route delay, damaged storage, herd loss, repair demand, or contamination may alter supply. No persistent management simulation is required.
8. **Preserve exact geography.** Any dependence on a specific bank, crossing, road, settlement, elevation, wind direction, or travel distance is marked **MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED**.

## Broad placement classes

### P1 — Managed working woodland

Supports stewardship-approved selective harvest, ordinary forestry, repair stock, eligible bark recovery, and some fuel-worker feed.

- Must communicate active care: retained growth, selective cuts, renewal areas, protected banks or edges, recovery work, and temporary closures.
- Should include more than one work context over the full region: tended edge, renewal stand, bank intervention, damaged recovery, public reserve, or constrained extraction.
- Does not require a different woodland biome or species for every progression band.
- Public-repair demand should be visible in at least some woodland work briefs so cutting and downstream need feel connected.
- Bark has no separate placement. It inherits the location and conditions of eligible wood work.
- Fuel suitability should be expressed at delivery or ordinary-labor intake, not through a charcoal tree.

**MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED:** relationship between managed woodland, vulnerable banks, settlements, public works, and practical haul routes.

### P2 — Plausible hunting habitat

Supports hunt, approach, recovery, and stewardship rather than stationary creature harvesting.

- Habitat must make target behavior and field recovery believable: agricultural margins, wet margins, recovering land, cover transitions, or quieter pressure-sensitive ground may all serve once final ecology is approved.
- Eligible game need access to food, cover, water, and movement space appropriate to its future category.
- Hunting pressure, local custody, unsafe conditions, or ecological strain may close or redirect an opportunity.
- A field-dressing or recovery relationship may occur at the opportunity or at a nearby safe recovery context; exact interaction remains open.
- Raw hide cannot appear universally. Its eligibility comes from the animal and successful recovery.
- Suitable animal fat can route toward ordinary rendering without a mandatory fat inventory state.

**MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED:** habitat continuity, separation from dense settlement, water barriers, agricultural conflict, and plausible movement corridors.

### P3 — Agricultural household zone

Supports flax cultivation, husbandry, ordinary food relationships, household stores, and some first-stage sorting or drying.

- Flax should appear as a household crop relationship within the existing agricultural landscape, not a player Farming node.
- Husbandry should visibly support households and food first while legitimately routing hide and suitable fat into equipment-related supply.
- Household textile work can be distributed rather than concentrated in a single factory.
- Drying, fire safety, pest protection, clean water, flood exposure, and food-versus-fiber pressure provide believable constraints.
- The player may buy, sell, supply, or respond to shortage through an interface without running the household.

**MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED:** where drier plots, shared water, flood exposure, household yards, and settlement boundaries are plausible.

### P4 — Settlement-edge service and dirty-work zone

Supports tanning, rendering, and other heat-, odor-, waste-, or wash-intensive ordinary labor.

- Tanners need hide and bark intake, water for ordinary work, drying space, and controlled waste separation.
- Renderers need legitimate animal-processing intake, heat, cleanliness, fire safety, and stable storage.
- These workers may occupy the same broad settlement edge but should not be represented as one combined process station.
- Food handling, domestic wells, clean textile washing, and irrigation should be protected from dirty return flows.
- Visible drains, settling areas, covered vats or work sheds, drying frames, smoke, and separated stores can communicate process without turning every step into an interaction.
- Closures may follow contamination, fire, spoilage, flood, or loss of clean working space.

**MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED:** water direction, drainage, prevailing wind if represented, domestic-water separation, flood reach, and safe distance from dense housing.

### P5 — Fuel-work zone

Supports ordinary conversion of suitable wood flow into charcoal.

- Needs accepted wood intake, dry staging, controlled burn space, cooling, firebreaks, and covered output storage.
- Should be accessible to managed woodland and markets without occupying protected woodland or dense residential space.
- Can accept player-supplied or ordinary wood, but structural reserve and scarce public-repair stock are not automatically valid feed.
- Smoke, drought, active fire danger, wet feed, or civic fuel priority can interrupt production.
- Players interact through sale, delivery, purchase, or shortage response. They do not operate a new Charcoal-making skill.

**MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED:** safe fire separation, practical feed movement, smoke relationship to habitation, and trade access.

### P6 — Market and receiving area

Supports imported steel receipt, mixed household goods, ordinary leather, flax, linen, tow, bark, charcoal, tallow, and player buying or selling.

- Imported steel must visibly arrive through trade and inspection rather than appear at an ore source.
- Bulk, dirty, flammable, food-adjacent, and high-value goods may share market administration while using different storage or handling spaces.
- One receiving function can serve several materials. Separate stalls for all nine states are unnecessary.
- Route disruption, rejected condition, public requisition, storage damage, or delayed carriers can explain supply variation.
- The interface should expose provenance and current availability clearly enough to support choices without simulating wholesale logistics.

**MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED:** relationship to established crossings, docks, caravan access, market settlements, and uncertain regional trade routes.

### P7 — Repair and craft workshop cluster

Supports Smithing, Leatherworking, equipment construction, repair, refurbishment, commission integration, and accepted material intake.

- Smiths need steel, charcoal, safe heat, cooling water, and access to repair demand.
- Leatherworkers need raw hide or leather, bark where tanning is player-facing, linen, tow, tallow, clean drying or fitting space, and separation from dirty tannery waste where appropriate.
- Workshops may sit near markets or public repair demand while preserving fire, noise, and water safeguards.
- Formed steel, prepared hide, thread, cut panels, padding, fitted subassemblies, and inspected work remain internal process states, not separate stores or node families.
- Repair should consume represented materials directly where practical. A repair-shop object should not create generic kit inventory.
- Aspirational commission work uses better fitting, tolerances, inspection, custody, and repair planning at an appropriate workshop; it does not require a capstone-material site.

**MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED:** relationship between safe workshop heat, market access, public works, clean water, and settlement layout.

### P8 — Storage and civic-infrastructure support

Supports reserve stock, covered storage, public-repair staging, shortage response, and crafting-from-storage compatibility.

- Storage should exist where the economy needs continuity: household stores, market warehouses, workshop holdings, and bounded public-repair reserves.
- A player-facing storage access point can serve multiple crafts and supply flows. It need not reproduce every NPC warehouse.
- Civic staging can make current bridge, dike, ferry, mill, boat, or building demand visible without finalizing site counts or creating construction gameplay.
- Flood, fire, damp, pests, theft, custody dispute, or emergency use can disrupt access.
- Stored war remains, memorial objects, or evidentiary salvage are excluded from routine material supply.

**MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED:** flood-safe storage, relation to crossings and workshops, and which public works exist close enough to justify visible staging.

## Placement relationships, not fixed routes

The following relationships should inform later map work:

```text
plausible hunting habitat
→ safe recovery or household/market intake
→ food relationship + raw hide + suitable fat flow
→ Cooking design / tannery / renderer

managed working woodland
→ wood recovery and sorting
→ public repair / household or market use / eligible bark / accepted fuel feed
→ workshops and storage / tannery / fuel workers

agricultural households
→ flax bundles + husbandry flows
→ textile workers / hide intake / renderer intake
→ market, workshops, and storage

regional trade arrival
→ receiving and inspection
→ steel storage
→ smithing and refurbishment
```

Later map design should make at least portions of these flows visually inferable. It does not need to place every stage adjacent. Distance, separation, and transport are meaningful when grounded in safety and settlement logic rather than inserted as travel padding.

## Environmental storytelling requirements

### Woodland and public repair

- Retained growth and roots should be as visible as cut sources.
- Old and new repair work can show why certain wood is reserved.
- Work markings or closures should communicate intent before interaction.
- Bark recovery should appear as part of sorting eligible cuts, never as scars left on otherwise standing sources.
- Damaged-source work should leave evidence of flood, fire, rot, wind, or obstruction without creating a new salvage material.

### Household production

- Small cultivated plots, bundles drying under cover, household mending, and shared work spaces can show that flax and cloth come from labor rather than a vendor abstraction.
- Husbandry presence should explain hide and rendering supply without turning homes into equipment factories.
- Clean textile work and food activity should be visually separated from tanning and rendering waste.

### Dirty industry and fuel work

- Tanning, rendering, and charcoal work should visibly need water, heat, air, drying, drainage, and storage.
- Smoke, smell, stains, carts, covered vats, drying frames, firebreaks, and guarded cooling areas can explain placement.
- These signs are atmosphere and process evidence. They do not each require an interactive object.

### Trade, storage, and repair

- Imported steel should arrive covered, counted, inspected, and stored near onward workshop demand.
- Repair piles should contain ordinary maintained structures and equipment, not endlessly renewable war relics.
- Warehouses, public reserves, and workshop stores can display scarcity or disruption through empty bays, delayed loads, protected stacks, or redirected deliveries.
- Finished and patched work should show the full loop: materials remain valuable after initial crafting because Lowmark continually maintains what it has.

## Disruption and closure framework

Use a small authored vocabulary across families:

| State | World cause | Player consequence | Ordinary-economy response |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Open** | Normal responsible work and trade | Usual actions and interfaces available | Ordinary NPC supply continues |
| **Purpose-limited** | Public repair, household priority, or accepted-input restriction | Some sources or destinations are favored; one clear choice may appear | Supply is redirected rather than erased |
| **Recovery closure** | Ecological pressure, contamination, flood damage, or regeneration need | A gathering or processing opportunity pauses or changes | Imports, stored supply, or other ordinary producers partly compensate |
| **Unsafe closure** | Fire, collapse, active flood, spoilage, or severe process hazard | Interface unavailable until an authored condition changes | Alternate market supply may tighten but the economy does not halt permanently |
| **Trade disruption** | Delayed carriers, crossing trouble, or outside shortage | Imported or traded goods become less available | Repair, reuse, reserves, and local substitutes become more important where physically valid |

These are proposal states, not a requirement for a global simulation. A limited event, story state, world schedule, or static variation may express them later.

## Placement coherence checks

### Clean and dirty water

- Ordinary processing water does not become magic.
- Tannery and renderer waste must not return directly to domestic or irrigation water.
- Smithing cooling, textile washing, food handling, and household water need believable separation from contaminated flows.
- Exact upstream/downstream placement is **MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED**.

### Fire and smoke

- Smithing, charcoal work, rendering, dry flax, cloth stores, and wood stores create different fire risks.
- Dense clustering for player convenience cannot erase firebreaks and safe storage.
- Exact prevailing wind or fire-response geography is **MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED**.

### Flood and storage

- Lowmark's water landscape makes uncovered low storage implausible for vital stock.
- Some visible flood exposure can create disruption, but critical stores need raised, protected, or otherwise credible placement.
- Exact flood-safe ground is **MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED**.

### Trade arrival

- Steel dependency requires at least one credible receiving relationship with regional trade.
- This proposal does not choose whether the dominant movement is by road, bridge, ferry, barge, or a combination.
- All arrival modes and settlement assignments are **MAP VERIFICATION REQUIRED**.

## Provisional decisions and alternatives

### P-P01 — Distributed economy with a few legible hubs

**Provisional recommendation:** distribute sources and household labor across plausible landscapes while consolidating buying, storage, repair, and imported receiving into a few multifunctional interfaces.

- **Alternative:** one central production hub. It is convenient but makes households, habitat, stewardship, and waste geography feel decorative.
- **Alternative:** fully distributed specialist interfaces. It is realistic but creates excessive travel and content burden.

### P-P02 — Broad settlement-edge service zone with internal separation

**Provisional recommendation:** place tanning and rendering in a shared broad dirty-work class, with distinct process spaces; place charcoal in its own fire-safe class.

- **Alternative:** combine all three into one service station. This hides their different inputs, waste, fire, and cleanliness relationships.
- **Alternative:** isolate every processor far from settlements. This increases travel and weakens the visible ordinary economy without a map basis.

### P-P03 — Authored disruptions rather than systemic simulation

**Provisional recommendation:** use a small set of authored closure and shortage states that can change availability, dialogue, and visible stock.

- **Alternative:** no disruption. Simpler, but removes much of Lowmark's stewardship and repair identity.
- **Alternative:** continuous ecological and logistics simulation. It is costly, difficult to communicate, and unsuitable for current scope.

### P-P04 — Shared intake and exchange interfaces

**Provisional recommendation:** markets, stores, workshops, and ordinary processors may accept multiple related goods through role-based interfaces. Avoid one NPC or object per item.

- **Alternative:** material-specific vendors and stations. It is explicit but bloats settlements and makes nine states feel like nine disconnected systems.

## Map verification register

Later map review must verify, without assuming exact answers now:

1. Which existing settlement contexts plausibly receive regional steel trade.
2. Where managed working woodland can exist without contradicting bank protection, agriculture, or settlement density.
3. Which woodland contexts can support difficult extraction and public-repair reserve work.
4. Where credible hunting habitat and movement space remain around agriculture, wetlands, war-scarred land, and settlements.
5. Which ground is suitable for flax and household drying with manageable flood risk.
6. How clean household water and irrigation are protected from tannery and renderer waste.
7. Where charcoal work can maintain firebreaks, dry feed, smoke separation, and market access.
8. Where covered storage and public-repair staging can sit above or outside likely flood damage.
9. Whether workshop heat, cooling, market access, and public repair demand can coexist in proposed craft clusters.
10. Which broad movement relationships are by road, crossing, ferry, barge, or mixed transport.

Failure to resolve an exact point does not block architecture. It blocks only the affected placement or route from becoming final content.

## Placement readiness result

The approved sources, processors, trade flows, storage, and repair sinks can be placed through eight broad classes without selecting exact geography or building one station per material. The architecture preserves visible ordinary labor, meaningful player participation, Lowmark's repair identity, environmental constraints, and solo-developer scope.

Later node and interface production can assign concrete representations to these classes once the map, ecology, and final content budget are reviewed. Exact location, route, distance, count, species, and output tuning remain deferred.
