# Caelmor Part 6 — Cross-Region Material Relationships and Lock Handoff

**Status:** proposal-stage synthesis and final gap audit. It defines readiness boundaries and the work required before equipment/economy design. It does not create materials, items, recipes, nodes, schemas, or canon.

## 1. Cross-region material relationships

The Parts 2–4 foundation supports dependencies through broad geography, labor, scarcity, and recovery. Exact cargo lists, routes, volumes, and prices remain unselected.

| Regional pair | Supported relationship | Material/craft consequence that may later be derived | Current limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lowmark–Thornfell | Lowmark supplies food and river/road exchange; Thornfell supplies mountain labor, stone/metal/wood precedent, escorts, and bridge expertise. | Food security, pass maintenance, tools, weather gear, and repair can develop as reciprocal needs. | No exact road, duration, quota, exclusive source, or approved catalog. |
| Lowmark–Mire | Food, ferries, household settlement, care, herbs, memorial contact, and records move between river communities. | Preservation, care goods, route maintenance, memorial work, and household repair may share methods. | Hydrology, direct channels, cargos, and Mire production scale remain unverified. |
| Lowmark–Emberholt | Emberholt needs food, water-linked logistics, labor, and outside witnesses; Lowmark markets encounter Emberholt metal and glass precedent. | Industrial food demand, containers, repairs, contracts, and class access can motivate exchange. | No fixed pipeline, import volume, special-metal tier, or direct route is approved. |
| Thornfell–Mire | Pass/shelter expertise meets herbs, care, uncertain ground, and seasonal travel; existing sources name some herbal/resin exchange. | Weatherproofing, medicine, route markers, provisions, and mourning practice may develop through recurring contact. | Exact substance, processing, route, and volume cannot be inferred from a motif or one legacy list. |
| Thornfell–Emberholt | Stone/bridge/route labor meets forge, civic heat, and ridge access; both depend on safe mountain infrastructure. | Joint repair, industrial access, tools, protective work practice, and contested technical credit have strong support. | Do not infer shared alloy secrets, a Stonebound supply network, or a verified direct material route. |

## 2. Dependency rules for later design

1. A region may import what its ecology does not support, but the importer, carrier, route class, risk, and reason for dependence need approval.
2. A trade relationship does not prove an exclusive source, stable volume, quality tier, or price.
3. A finished craft in one dossier does not prove every input is local.
4. War salvage is finite, politically charged, and evidentiary; it is not automatically a renewable gathering loop.
5. Ritual material is not automatically magical material.
6. Resonant or Stonebound-adjacent remains cannot become ordinary production stock without contradicting C11 and postwar restraint.
7. Mire earth-memory threats and local impressions are hazards or evidence contexts, never assumed harvesting nodes.
8. Volkhari practice cannot be absorbed into Emberholt industry or treated as a demographic supply chain under open C06.
9. Lowmark farm-water practice cannot define a licensed tool class, public market, or player system until C09 is selected.
10. Map uncertainty forbids exact travel time, direct-road claims, and precise supply routes until verification.

## 3. Required derivation card

Before any future material, tool, weapon, armor piece, garment, ritual object, food, or relic component is proposed, its designer should complete this chain:

| Step | Required question |
|---|---|
| Desired function | What practical, protective, social, ceremonial, evidentiary, or status need already exists in the world? |
| User and custodian | Who needs it, who makes or maintains it, and who can refuse access? |
| Component/material | What physical qualities are required? Do not name a new substance merely to satisfy the qualities. |
| Processing | Which existing or approved labor turns the source into a usable component? What fuel, water, skill, waste, danger, and time does it require? |
| World source | Which geography, animal, plant, salvage context, trade relationship, or byproduct justifies the input? |
| Scarcity and stewardship | Is scarcity ecological, technical, political, seasonal, legal, finite, or imposed by care obligations? |
| Cultural meaning | Why does this region value, distrust, display, hide, repair, inherit, or discard it? |
| Historical consequence | Which war, recovery, migration, institution, or tradition changed its use? |
| Cross-region effect | Who depends on it elsewhere, and what evidence supports that movement? |
| Canon status | Which approved source supports every claim, and which open decision could change it? |

If the chain begins with a skill unlock or desired tier and works backward by inventing a resource, it fails the lore gate.

| Gate | Result | Finding |

Targeted second-pass work confirmed that adding more customs, mysteries, or named objects would not cure the failures. The remaining work is selection, source reconciliation, and grounded source/process development.

## 6. Lore Foundation Lock readiness

**Lore Foundation Lock readiness: NOT READY.**

The proposal foundation is historically and narratively deep, and all four regions have strong cultural identity. Lock is withheld because:

1. approved rulings and selected proposals are not yet reconciled into the canon corpus;
2. the proposal set has not received a selective canon approval pass;
3. major source and processing foundations remain missing across multiple regions;
4. exact geography cannot yet support precise material movement or placement.

The required READY statement is therefore not issued.

## 7. Material-culture readiness

**Material-culture readiness: NOT READY FOR EQUIPMENT/ECONOMY CATALOGS.**

The strongest future derivation areas are Lowmark food/river work, Thornfell stone-metal-wood-leather work, Mire memorial/root/reed/clay practice, and Emberholt forge/heat/glass/textile workplaces. Even these areas need selective canon approval and, in several cases, missing source or process foundations.

## 8. Recommended next handoff

### Immediate handoff: canon integration and source/process lore

1. Creative Director selects the recommended Part 2–5 clusters.
2. A reconciliation editor updates Timeline, War History, Factions, detailed dossiers, merged overview, character anchors, and status labels as one coordinated canon change.
3. Four bounded regional lore briefs establish only missing foundations: source ecology/geology, labor, stewardship, transformation, waste/risk, scarcity, and broad exchange. They should begin with existing cultural functions and may not name new resources or finished equipment.
4. A map verifier approves only the broad routes and source locations needed by selected foundations.
5. Part 6 is rerun against the reconciled canon and the new source/process foundations.

### Equipment/economy handoff after a READY decision

Once the rerun reaches READY and the Creative Director approves Lore Foundation Lock, equipment/economy designers should receive:

- the approved canon-selection ledger;
- reconciled regional source and labor foundations;
- the derivation card above;
- explicit forbidden inferences for magic, Stonebound, Mire manifestations, Volkhari knowledge, and war salvage;
- map-approved source and exchange bounds;
- a requirement to trace every proposal from function to source.

Until then, equipment/economy production should remain deferred.
