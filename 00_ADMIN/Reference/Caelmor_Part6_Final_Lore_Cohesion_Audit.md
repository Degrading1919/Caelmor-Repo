# Caelmor Part 6 — Final Lore Cohesion Audit

**Status:** proposal-stage audit for Creative Director review. This document does not amend canon, approve every proposal, design material or equipment catalogs, or declare Lore Foundation Lock.

## 1. Authority and decision gate

This audit uses Phase 1 canon, the Master Assistant Protocol, Stage A, Parts 2–5, and Creative Director rulings through C15. Where an old source conflicts with a Director ruling, the ruling controls the audit. The old source still requires a later approved correction.

No unresolved Creative Director decision prevents the **readiness test** from being run. The test can preserve C03, C06, C07, C09, and C16 as bounded unknowns. Those questions block particular later claims, not an honest assessment of whether the present foundation is sufficient.

The distinction matters:

- **Proposal cohesion:** whether Parts 2–5 form a consistent world model.
- **Canon cohesion:** whether the live Timeline, War History, Factions, dossiers, summaries, and character files express that model consistently.
- **Material-culture readiness:** whether geography, labor, processing, use, scarcity, stewardship, and exchange are strong enough to derive future materials and crafts without inventing their justification afterward.

## 3. Coherent foundations worth preserving

The following principles survive every audit and should govern later selection:

1. **History persists through work.** Bridges, sluices, passes, ferries, shelters, workshops, care records, memorials, and repairs carry history more reliably than exposition.
2. **Regional difference comes from obligation.** Lowmark manages water and shared subsistence; Thornfell manages access and collective survival; the Mire manages testimony, uncertain ground, and care; Emberholt manages useful heat, risk, and public accountability.
3. **The Great War remains within living memory.** Adults may have direct experience, while younger people inherit records, habits, injuries, missing relatives, and contested public stories.
4. **Magic has social and material limits.** It responds to element, practice, emotion, and place; it does not provide automatic truth or excuse missing labor.
5. **Institutions are neither monoliths nor scenery.** Their legitimacy comes from tasks people need done, while class, access, secrecy, and error create conflict.
6. **Cross-region connection is ordinary before it is mysterious.** Food, shelter, work, marriage, fosterage, care, trade, and repair explain most movement.
7. **A later revelation adds evidence.** It does not erase the local meaning or emotional resolution of an earlier story.

## 4. Major remaining cohesion failures

### 4.1 Selected truth is not integrated into canon

Director rulings and the coherent Parts 2–5 model remain separated from old canon files. A later designer can still encounter mutually exclusive dates, magical laws, identities, supernatural behavior, or site names. This is a governance failure even where the preferred answer is known.

### 4.2 Proposal volume exceeds approved canon

Parts 2–5 deliberately generated alternatives and candidate networks. Treating every row as true would overcrowd the world, overdetermine mysteries, and make institutions implausibly busy. The next canon pass must choose a representative set and preserve some entries as optional, belief, or rejected material.

### 4.3 Material transformation is underdescribed

The world often names a landscape, finished craft, or legacy resource without establishing the middle: stewardship, gathering, fuel, preparation, processing, waste, risk, ownership, seasonal limits, training, and exchange. This is the main material-culture readiness gap.

### 4.4 Precise geography remains unverified

Broad trade and migration are credible. Exact material flows are not. A later catalog cannot responsibly assign a direct route, node, travel time, or exclusive source until selected relationships are checked against map and hydrology.

## 5. Second-pass result

The targeted second pass tested chronology, regional identity, institutional function, magic rules, peoples/factions, character custody, mystery ownership, map limits, and material foundations. It found no new contradiction inside the Parts 2–5 proposal model. It did confirm two failures that cannot be solved by adding more disconnected lore entries:

- directed facts and selected proposals have not been reconciled into the live canon corpus;
- several regional material chains lack supported sources or processing traditions.

**Cohesion conclusion:** Caelmor has a deep, coherent **proposal foundation**, but does not yet have a fully coherent **canon foundation**. This audit therefore does not recommend Lore Foundation Lock at this time.
