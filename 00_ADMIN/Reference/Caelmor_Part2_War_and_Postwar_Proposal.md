# Caelmor Part 2 — Great War and the thirty-year inheritance

**Status:** proposal for Creative Director review, not canon. Read with `Caelmor_Part2_Historical_Backbone_Proposal.md` and `Caelmor_Part2_Historical_Gap_Audit.md` in this manifest-approved folder. Canon files are unchanged. The Great War lasts 5–10 years and ends about 30 years before the present by Director decision; the seven-year sequence below is one proposed implementation, not an approved calendar.

## The war's cause: failing duties, not elemental destiny

**Anchored:** Emberholt's firecraft experiments and denials, Thornfell's ridge warnings and defensive mobilization, Lowmark's reluctant escorts and supply role, and Mire Memorykeepers' warnings are all present in `LORE/Worldbuilding Docs/Caelmor_War_History.md`, IV–VII. That source's old dating, formal prewar Remnant, and Volkhari Titan are superseded by Director decisions recorded in Stage A C01–C04. Its blanket postwar-ban wording is now resolved by the Director's C12 ruling as an overbroad summary of agreed restraint on dangerous wartime practices, not a durable universal law. The region dossiers anchor the passes, grain routes, central Mire campaigns, and Glassed Hollow.

**Proposed causal reading:** The older custom of reciprocal passage could not answer a new question: who could inspect and restrict another region's dangerous practice? Emberholt's civic leaders had real reasons to protect trade and public firecraft, yet concealed experiments made assurance less credible. Thornfell could close a pass but harm the civilians it sheltered. Lowmark could feed all parties but could not keep food apolitical. Mire communities could testify to harm but lacked the power to make distant commanders listen. A wartime precursor inside Emberholt exploited these failures, sometimes worsening them, without being made the secret cause of every human decision.

The immediate spark is proposed as a failed inquiry into firecraft accidents and missing inspection records, followed by a border escort clash. Each side can honestly remember itself as defending an obligation. The exact inspection delegation, casualty names, and culpability are intentionally left open for a later source and map pass; no invented foreign kingdom or second war is required.

`~37–30 BP` is a working editorial placement. Event labels are proposed. Known landmarks anchor the events, but the dossier does **not** establish a single continuous road or exact travel time among Windscar, Crestbridge, the Mire, and Emberholt. Any specific movement between them below is a proposal requiring map verification.

The seven-year spine is intentionally causal: pressure on passes redirects people and supply; Lowmark's refusal to make water a weapon increases the importance of ordinary logistics; the Mire absorbs attrition; the precursor promises an escape from stalemate; Glassed Hollow removes that promise; exhaustion ends the campaign. If a future choice makes the war five, eight, or ten years long, expand or compress campaigning seasons inside the directed bound rather than add an unrelated war.

### Battle and civilian evidence rule

Each turning point needs at least two independent forms of evidence before it becomes an authored reveal: a physical scar (gorge, ash field, drowned camp, glass), an administrative trace (horn interval, toll, escort list, or sealed report), and/or a living duty (cairn tending, ferry practice, memorial naming). The proposed records above are **not canon artifacts**. Their value is that they let present people disagree about motive while the Director-set facts remain fixed.

### The wartime precursor, Glassed Hollow, and the formal Remnant

The wartime actors should be called an **unnamed precursor** in factual narration. **Proposed:** ember theorists, officers, archive handlers, and ritual engineers embedded in Emberholt's emergency effort gradually form a covert working circle. Their opportunity is wartime exceptional authority; their temptation is to treat Volkhari affinity and human emotion as things to control. They redirect supplies and records toward the Hollow. After its failure, survivors possess culpable evidence and a reason to keep it hidden. Only later do people, archives, and doctrine cohere into the **formal Remnant**, whose corrected Factions account seeks mass civic embercraft under covert control. This does not place later Ashfall, Deepbrand, Hearthveil, or Red-Lit Scribe hierarchies intact during the war. Exact name, founding date, personnel continuity, and relation to the Ashfall Authority remain open under C03.

Kaelith's escape and continued freedom, Thalan's present captivity, and the Titan's initiate identity are Director-set. A future public story may wrongly assign Volkhari guilt only if it is explicitly identified as a false account. Do not use a Stonebound as a witness to the ritual. Under the Director's C08 ruling, “spirit” describes living/emotional imprints interacting with elemental resonance; it is neither a fifth discipline nor a hidden separate cause of the Hollow.

## Five civilian event dossiers that connect the regions

These are proposed evidence chains for later writing, **not** quest scripts or approved biographies.

1. **Withheld warnings, late prewar.** Thornfell ridgeline reports, Lowmark transport concerns, and Mire testimony arrive separately before a failed inquiry. Existing present anchors Ressa Highcrag and Dorn Feldhorn could maintain horn knowledge; Aldric Fenmere retains old supply papers; Emberholt Archivist Marenya Silvertide represents a later archive intermediary. None is assigned authorship of an earlier record without evidence. The delayed re-reading: each region thought another ignored a warning, but their records reveal distinct questions were asked and answered incompletely. `LORE/Worldbuilding Docs/Caelmor_War_History.md`, V–VI; character anchor files for all four regions.
2. **Four routes under strain, middle war.** A period of interrupted passage forces food, letters, wounded people, and displaced households through Lowmark waterways, Thornfell signal routes, Mire safe ground, and Emberholt approaches. Maelin Rowe's canon wartime sealed cargo, Hadrik Stonewake's war scars, and Edris Hollowmere's tending of the wounded are direct anchors; present custodians of names and banners need not be veterans. The delayed re-reading: an event called “logistics” in a council account is remembered as abandonment in a household and rescue in an infirmary. Exact route geometry is open.
3. **Bannerless retreat, late war.** Exhausted civilians and forces enter the Mire with wounded and stripped standards. Edris and Bran Hollowstep can carry direct survivor memory; Calwen Reed-Binder, Mara Fenholt, Annelise Reed, and Maera Cairnwatch can carry present care and record practices only where their existing character text supports them. The delayed re-reading: cairn names, actual graves, and unmarked household members differ because bodies and families crossed regions. No literal ghost or disputed chapel identity is used. Mire and Lowmark character anchors; Mire dossier II–IV.
4. **The false forge collapse.** The Hollow, official report, Ashfall's restricted archive, and Volkhari testimony preserve incompatible accounts. Marenya can be a present keeper of altered material without being declared a wartime witness. A later convergence of place, report gaps, and survivor testimony changes the reading of an early civic safety story. It must never change the Director-set Titan, Kaelith, or Thalan facts. Timeline V; corrected Factions; Emberholt dossier II.
5. **Cessation without settlement.** Local forces stop fighting, but no shared reparations or complete loss count follows. Maelin's pre-cessation river work, Hadrik's scars, and Edris and Bran's survival keep a thirty-year memory socially immediate. Younger caretakers inherit work and silence, not implausible longevity. A new generation may learn that “the war ended” means armies ceased while debts, missing names, and truth claims did not. Stage A C01; region character anchors.

## Thirty years of recovery and managed memory

The intervals are proposed, relative to the war's end; they do not create a new calendar or specify regional statutes beyond the Director's C12 ruling.

| Interval after cessation | Interregional process | Institutions and living consequences |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **0–3 years: The Stopping** | Households count missing people, clear crossings, recover bodies, reopen mills and passes, and choose where displaced families can remain. Surviving authorities broadly agree that weaponization, amplification, uncontrolled ritual experiments, and dangerous magical research must stop. | Lowmark charters are argued over; Thornfell cairns include absent bodies; Mire caretakers gain authority by staying; Emberholt citizens demand safer public fire practice. No durable central authority can enforce a universal magic ban. |
| **3–9 years: Repair Bargains** | Grain, escort labor, marsh guidance, and Emberholt repair work become bargaining power. Agreements are local and incomplete; each region interprets the shared postwar restraint through its own institutions. | Fenmere stewardship, Thornfell rotation, Mire chapel/cooperative care, and Emberholt civic council service gain practical legitimacy. A current tariff argument can descend from an emergency obligation rather than simple greed. |
| **~9–18 years: Managed Memory** | Public explanations diverge. Emberholt's formal Remnant develops from precursor survivors while civic offices build a sincere safety culture that hidden actors can influence. Other regions preserve contrary ledgers, signals, burial testimony, and family accounts. | Red-Lit Scribe control and Ashfall secrecy grow inside functioning institutions; Emberholt citizens are not all conspirators. Exact Remnant founding year stays open. |
| **Later postwar to present: Unsettled Peace** | Dreamers first emerge **after** the war. Hearthveil-induced visions target influence within Emberholt; ordinary grief dreams remain common human experiences. | A dream can reopen a question but requires independent corroboration. Veterans, caretakers, and younger officials disagree over which past account deserves public trust. Exact Dreamer first year is open. |

**Directed C12 — postwar magic law:** The surviving authorities' broad agreement targeted the *wartime uses* of magic, especially weaponization, amplification, uncontrolled ritual experimentation, and dangerous research. Some later records compressed this into “magic was outlawed.” There was no lasting central power that could impose that phrase as a universal rule. Regional restrictions diverged. Emberholt's public civic embercraft and Lowmark workers' simple farming water spells therefore remain consistent with the historical agreement; neither practice implies approval of the war techniques. Thornfell's mostly natural acoustics and rare dormant magic, and the Mire's loss of active Memorywarden practice, should not be explained by a fictional continent-wide police regime. Exact local decrees, enforcement, and teaching norms remain Part 3 questions.

### Regional transitions as one shared recovery

**Lowmark** holds food and river access, so its noble and village legitimacy comes from provisioning and repair. House Fenmere's canon wartime supply compacts and retained ledgers make administration politically charged. A family can say the same granary fed people and prolonged fighting. Simple farming water spells remain part of ordinary work under the Director's ruling, while dangerous wartime practice is subject to the postwar restraint; exact Lowmark teaching and oversight remain open for Part 3. Lensemasters and Shadeweavers can preserve or misread different aspects of the same record without becoming a hidden master-government. `LORE/Characters/Caelmore_Characters_Region1_Lowmark_Anchors (Not Full List).txt`, Fenmere, Maelin, Corvin, Annelise; `LORE/Factions/Caelmor_Factions.md`, Lowmark sections.

**Thornfell** turns military routes back into shared infrastructure. The Assembly, clan tribunals, Ridge Wardens, cairn keepers, bridgewrights, and hornbearers already exist; this proposal gives their present disputes a recovery function: allocate escort work, repair passes, protect highsteads, and decide when a dangerous call may sound. Blood-Oath and Hearthguard views can each point to an actual cost borne during war. Most echoes remain natural; a few exceptional resonances remain possible. Thornfell dossier II–IV, VII–VIII; Factions Thornfell sections.

**The Mire** cannot wait for a victorious state to rebuild its ground. Chapel councils, cooperatives, Memorykeepers, Marshwardens, and healers gain standing by drainage, burial, food sharing, and displaced-family settlement. Their duties survive the Memorywardens' lost active craft. Mourning is one part of ordinary civic life, not the whole identity of every Mire family. Lowmark and Thornfell can exchange names and visits with Mire caretakers because wartime bodies and households crossed their boundaries. No particular chapel name or supernatural major-threat roster is chosen here. Mire dossier II–V, VII–VIII; Factions Mire government.

**Emberholt** responds to lower-city loss and fear with public schools, the Speaker's council, Emberguard safety work, guild repair, and civic embercraft. Many citizens sincerely want controlled fire to protect a city built around heat. The hidden Remnant uses the same education, recordkeeping, and selection channels to pursue mastery. The present Speaker is male by Director ruling; the office's founding sequence remains proposed. The Volkhari catastrophe is concealed, not erased from every possible record. Emberholt dossier II–IV, XVII; corrected Factions Remnant/Emberholt sections.

## Dreamers, induced visions, and ordinary grief

**Directed:** Dreamers first appear after the Great War and cannot be the first rediscoverers' cause. **Proposed evidence boundary:** a true Dreamer experiences a fragment that may describe a place, person, or unresolved event but may be misread; it does not confer comprehensive prophecy. Hearthveil-induced dreams are targeted attempts to guide loyalty, fear, or civic selection and can mimic revelation. Ordinary nightmares after loss need no supernatural cause. A recurring image gains historical weight only when independently checked against a record, witness, or place. This rule lets a once-dismissed dream acquire meaning after a Lowmark ledger or Mire account is found, and then gain *another* meaning when a Hearthveil pattern is exposed. Corrected Factions, Hearthveil and alignment sections; Stage A C02.

The exact origin, first date, reliability, and social recognition of true Dreamers remain proposed or open. No existing character is newly labeled a Dreamer. The proposal does not use Dreamers to authenticate Stonebound speech, a fifth element, or a complete war chronology.

## Resolved Part 2 decisions and remaining questions

- **C08, spirit — resolved:** The old term refers to living/emotional imprints and the interaction of memory, grief, intention, and identity with elemental resonance. It creates no fifth element or discipline. Mire earth-memory remains one regional expression; exact phenomena at disputed sites are separate Part 3 questions.
- **C11, Stonebound:** no battle, warning, or archive in this history relies on their agency or on the disputed Thornfell summit identity.
- **C12, magic law — resolved at historical level:** Authorities broadly agreed to stop dangerous wartime practice, but no durable center enforced a universal ban; regions interpreted restraint differently. Exact regional decrees and enforcement are later local-detail questions, not Part 2 blockers.
- **C03, Remnant:** precursor name, membership continuity, formal founding date, and Ashfall relationship remain unchosen.
- **C06, C14, C15:** no exact Volkhari survivor count, Mire chapel identity, or two-major-threat roster is asserted.
- **C16:** “Great War” is the working term. “Long War” in Phase 1 may be an epithet or stale wording, never evidence of a second conflict here.

The companion gap audit identifies the narrower approvals needed before any of these proposals become canon or drive Part 3 regional writing.
