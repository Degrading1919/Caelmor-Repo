**Status:** proposal for Creative Director review. This document changes no canon. `Caelmor_Part2_War_and_Postwar_Proposal.md` develops the war and its thirty-year inheritance; `Caelmor_Part2_Historical_Gap_Audit.md` records decision gates and Part 3 briefs. ## Reading key and source authority

- **Directed** means an explicit Creative Director ruling already recorded in Stage A. It governs this proposal despite stale wording in older files.
- **Anchored** means an existing source supports the fact, place, people, or institution cited.
- **Proposed** means a connective historical claim offered for approval. It must not be repeated as settled canon.
- **Open** means the proposal deliberately withholds a factual choice. Neither silence nor an in-world rumor resolves it.

The Director has directed a 5–10-year Great War ending about 30 years before the present; Dreamers arising only afterward; a wartime precursor forming the formal Remnant later; a precursor/Remnant initiate becoming the Fire Titan; Kaelith escaping and remaining free; Thalan as the sole *current* captive; Founders as teachers distinct from first rediscoverers; mostly natural Thornfell acoustics with rare dormant magic; Lowmark workers using simple farm water spells while the Last Field-Mage alone retains combat water practice; a male current Flameward Speaker; and two major Mire supernatural threats with minor anomalies possible. Detailed regional dossiers and corrected Factions govern over the merged overview except where a Director ruling overrides them. See `Caelmor_Stage_A_Contradiction_Register.md`, opening rule and C01–C15.

The old timeline's war and Dreamer dates, War History's Volkhari Titan, and any claim that the wartime organization already had the formal Remnant structure are superseded directions, **not** alternative historical truths. Whether any character believes an obsolete account must be written explicitly as belief in a future canon pass.

### Date migration rule

The Great War is one short conflict, not centuries of fighting. Text that calls its campaigns a “final century” or places the aftermath 150 years ago needs coordinated correction later, but this proposal does not silently move every ancient event by a fixed offset. The age of rediscovery can still be centuries old; its relationship to the new war date must be set by source reconciliation. Do not call the Great War's length a century simply to preserve old prose. `LORE/Timeline/Caelmor_Timeline.md`, III–VIII; Stage A C01–C03.

## The ancient world: continuity without lost empires

### Stonebound and Volkhari

**Anchored:** The Stonebound predate the first learned elemental traditions and mostly lie dormant. Their source-defined function concerns deep maintenance and balance beneath future Emberholt. Volkhari are a distinct fire-attuned people whose inherited discipline is not human embercraft. `LORE/Timeline/Caelmor_Timeline.md`, I–II; `LORE/Factions/Caelmor_Factions.md`, Volkhari and Stonebound sections.

**Proposed:** The oldest surviving evidence should be partial and practical: sealed pressure paths, blocked heat routes, ridge evacuation marks, and warnings preserved as habits after their explanation is lost. Volkhari families kept hazard knowledge because they lived with the volcanic ground; they did not rule a vanished continental state or found human fire magic. Later Emberholt expansion put civic workers and investigators above older works without granting them the right to claim Volkhari history as their own. This makes the Glassed Hollow seizure of Volkhari knowledge a violation of a long relationship, not the sudden discovery of an exotic power.

**Present consequence:** A city mark first read as an industrial safety symbol could later be recognized as evidence of erased Volkhari presence. An excavation restriction first explained as civic caution could reflect either genuine Stonebound disturbance or a Remnant interest in keeping witnesses out. **Open C11:** the Thornfell summit event's identity and Stonebound agency remain undecided. The proposal gives Stonebound no speech, prophecy, military action, or authority to certify history. The surviving Volkhari population remains open under C06.

### The three older regional lineages

**Riverwardens.** Canon places a small water-attuned enclave near Veilwater and credits it with teaching early human settlers flood timing and irrigation, before withdrawing from demands for power. `LORE/Regions/Caelmor_Region1_LowmarkVale.md`, VI–VII. **Proposed:** shared canal closures, flood markers, and an obligation to observe before altering flow became Lowmark custom even where their source was forgotten. A village could know the reliable procedure while rejecting the claim that it came from magic. The later farm spells authorized by the Director are a surviving small-scale practice; the Field-Mage's combat knowledge is a separate rare inheritance. The exact teaching and legal status of farm magic awaits the policy decision.

**Echohewn.** Canon describes dwarven-influenced highland builders, extraordinary acoustics, rare air-resonance, horn codes, and a Dwarven Founder story now conflated with a First Air Mage. `LORE/Regions/Caelmor_Region2_ThornfellMarches.md`, I, VII–VIII; Stage A C07, C10. **Proposed:** useful architecture and bridge procedures survived because ordinary builders kept applying them; the subtle magical edge became hard to recognize after context was lost. A horn tower can be excellent engineering, while a rare dormant event there remains real. This keeps natural sound and exceptional air practice distinct. The war's desperate use of resonance then damaged both the sites and trust in the people who knew their limits.

**Memorywardens.** Canon makes this a prewar order of earth-attuned caretakers; war conscription and overwhelming grief broke its practice. Modern Chapel-Tenders and Memorykeepers inherit forms and duties but not its active magic. `LORE/Regions/Caelmor_Region3_MireOfFallenBanners.md`, IV–V, VIII, XIII. **Proposed:** the order taught testimony before burial, limits on placing grief into ground, and the duty to keep names even when armies refused them. These methods were useful before war and became civic necessities afterward. A present memorial route can thus have an old practical reason without being a functional spell. **Open C08:** “spirit” in source vocabulary awaits the Director's ruling and does not yet establish a fifth discipline.

None of these lineages is proposed as a continent-spanning secret order. Their ages need not match the Stonebound's or each other's. Their living successors have different amounts of knowledge and authority.

## How four settled regions became interdependent

**Proposed sequence, anchored to existing geography and governments:**

1. **Settled routes.** Lowmark's river crossings and grain land, Thornfell's pass knowledge, Mire raised banks and channels, and Emberholt's foothold settlements made seasonal exchange useful before any shared political center existed. The exact map adjacency and travel durations remain unverified; no single road through all four is asserted.
2. **Local commons.** Lowmark lineages earned standing by keeping dikes, mills, and crossings usable. Thornfell clan speakers shared control of signals, bridges, and closures because a unilateral decision could strand another highstead. Mire cooperatives and care keepers gained legitimacy by maintaining safe ground and receiving the dead. Emberholt guild and civic authorities gained standing by keeping an inhabited volcanic city safe. These are proposed histories of existing institutions, not four new governments. `LORE/Factions/Caelmor_Factions.md`, regional governments; detailed dossiers, settlement and social-role sections.
3. **Repeated passage bargains.** In bad seasons each jurisdiction supplied some combination of food, escort, refuge, or repair while preserving local authority. This is a proposed recurring custom, **not** a formal continent-wide treaty, lost kingdom, or fifth government. The four record forms differ: Lowmark charters, Thornfell signal rosters, Mire testimony, and Emberholt guild accounts.
4. **Knowledge travels along the same routes.** Rediscoverers and then teachers carry warnings and demonstrations between communities, but geography and local obligations give each tradition its own language. Later political leaders mistake a shared ethic of restraint for proof that another region will accept the same emergency demands.
5. **The bargains narrow.** As fire experimentation, border scrutiny, and food demands grow, authorities argue over who can inspect, close, escort, requisition, and shelter. The wartime precursor exploits this distrust; it is not the sole cause of every dispute.

**Present consequence:** all four can say they once kept the others alive and all four have evidence of abandonment. This supports a later changed understanding: a Lowmark marginal note, Thornfell horn interval, Mire list of transferred civilians, and Emberholt safety memorandum may refer to one crisis from different duties, even though no single archive contains the whole story. Each suggested record is proposed, not a canon artifact.

### The older kings and scholars: a bounded proposal

Phase 1's world fantasy calls Caelmor “a fractured realm once guided by scholars and kings” (`00_ADMIN/Reference/Caelmor_Project_Instructions.txt`, World Fantasy Statement; `01_PRE-PRODUCTION/1.1 Core Vision/Caelmor_Phase1_1_Core_Vision.md`). No source establishes a named dynasty, its borders, or that one crown ever ruled all four regions. **Proposed:** before the present regional governments took their current forms, kingly courts occasionally confirmed crossings, land claims, and passage obligations whose daily enforcement remained local. Scholars and record keepers gave such promises continuity; Riverwardens, Echohewn, Memorywardens, and Volkhari were never reduced to royal branches. As crowns lost reach or legitimacy over generations, charters and local service outlasted the courts that endorsed them. The Great War then burned or scattered many surviving records without being made the sole cause of a millennia-old collapse.

This supplies a historical role for kings without inventing a lost empire: a seal could still support Lowmark's competing noble claims, while a Thornfell clan could reject the idea that a distant court ever controlled its pass. Mire communities might retain the *duty* recorded in an old confirmation but not a court's account of who performed it; Emberholt guild archives might cite royal wording selectively. The number of courts, their chronology, and whether any crown had jurisdiction outside Lowmark are **open**. A later Director-reviewed polity pass should set those facts before a named kingdom, dynasty, or map boundary is written into canon.

## Magic-history framework

### Rediscoverers, Founders, and Dreamers

**Directed:** First rediscoverers and Founders are separate people or roles; Dreamers appear after the war and are unrelated to rediscovery. `LORE/Factions/Caelmor_Factions.md`, Elemental Traditions and alignment summary; Stage A C02, C07.

**Proposed working definition:** “First Mage” is a retrospective description of an early local rediscoverer who demonstrated a repeatable relationship with one regional element. It is not a formal title. A Founder is a later, iconic teacher whose achievement was making methods portable, bounded, and teachable through cautions and apprenticeships. No Founder count, exact identity, lifespan, or teacher-student chain is selected. Lowmark's First Listener remains a contested Riverwarden honorific; Thornfell's Dwarven Founder account remains a conflation to correct, not proof that the first rediscoverer and teacher were one person. A rediscoverer's situational notes and a Founder's simplified teaching may disagree without requiring one to be a fraud.

### What magic history can and cannot prove

Material evidence, testimony, and observation should corroborate a claim. An Echohewn sound channel does not prove every horn call was magical. A Mire earth-memory imprint does not identify the dead person who caused it. Volkhari bodily affinity is not proof of the Titan's identity. A Dreamer can supply a detail worth checking, never an omniscient answer. This preserves the grounded tone and makes later re-readings earned through records and people. The Stage A research memo supplies the structural method; no RuneScape plot or surface content is imported.
