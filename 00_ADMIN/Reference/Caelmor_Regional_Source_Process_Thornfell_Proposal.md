# Caelmor Regional Source / Process Foundation — Thornfell Marches

**Status:** proposal-stage connective lore for Creative Director review. It establishes ordinary source and process foundations without approving resources, items, recipes, nodes, tiers, schemas, exact routes, or production volumes, and it does not amend live canon.

## Authority and limits

This proposal uses the reconciled Thornfell dossier, Timeline, War History, Factions, Parts 2–6, and the canon-reconciliation audit.

- Most wind, horn, echo, and pressure behavior is natural. Rare dormant resonance remains exceptional and dangerous when forced.
- Echochamber Summit may couple rare Echohewn resonance to an ancient Stonebound node. The response is passive and place-bound; it cannot speak, teach craft, identify a source, validate ownership, reveal history, or become a production method.
- Resonant or Stonebound-adjacent remains are evidence and hazard contexts, never ordinary stock.
- C03, C06, C07, exact Assembly procedure, exact routes, and exact source sites remain open.

## Regional proposition

Thornfell's source culture is organized around survival through exposed seasons and maintaining access without stripping the slopes that make access possible. Food stores, cloth, fuel, and containers are as politically important as stone or metal because a pass community can possess excellent craft knowledge and still be defeated by a closed route, a burned slope, or a failed winter store.

## Foundation 1 — food and preservation

| Required field | Proposed foundation |
|---|---|
| World source or import dependency | Local household and highstead food comes from already supported goats, herbs, and whatever limited upland gathering or cultivation later selection permits. Lowmark remains the broad staple-food dependency. No crop, herd scale, dish, or recipe is selected. |
| Who gathers or imports it | Herding households, seasonal gatherers, carriers, escorts, shelter hosts, and highstead storekeepers handle different stages. Lowmark producers and markets may supply staple stores without a fixed route or quota. |
| Who processes it | Households, shelter keepers, granary stewards, and communal cooks preserve and distribute food. Their authority follows responsibility for surviving closures rather than a new guild. |
| Broad processing method | Ordinary drying, smoking, salting, fermenting, cool storage, and careful rotation may all exist as broad methods. Later food design must choose actual ingredients and combinations. |
| Fuel and water needs | Hearth fuel must be divided among warmth, cooking, preservation, and craft. Water is used for washing, soaking, and cooking where locally available, with no water-magic implication. |
| Waste and risk | Spoilage, smoke, chimney fire, contamination, vermin, exposure during collection, herd loss, and missed deliveries threaten households. A closed pass may protect people while also causing hunger. |
| Stewardship and access | Highstead and shelter stewards maintain stores, publish or witness broad allocation decisions, and protect future planting stock and breeding herds. Exact Assembly authority is not fixed. |
| Scarcity reason | Short growing seasons, exposed weather, damaged slopes, winter isolation, labor loss, transport interruption, and competing fuel needs create scarcity. |
| Regional cultural meaning | A stocked shelter and an honestly kept store tally signify fulfilled obligation. Shared food after a rescue or closure carries memory of who was admitted, delayed, or overlooked. |
| Broad cross-region dependency | Lowmark supplies broad food and exchange capacity; Mire may contribute herbs or care-linked provisions; Emberholt demand can compete for transported food. No exact cargo or road is approved. |

## Foundation 2 — textiles and weather clothing

| Required field | Proposed foundation |
|---|---|
| World source or import dependency | Goats, wool precedent, hides, furs, harsh weather, and imported cloth provide a mixed base. Pasture and herd management remain bounded household or highstead work rather than an unlimited local source. |
| Who gathers or imports it | Herding households collect fleece or hides where ordinary practice supports it; hunters and carriers may supply bounded inputs; traders bring fiber or cloth from outside. |
| Who processes it | Households wash, prepare, spin, felt or weave, stitch, mend, and pass down cloth. Local makers finish weather protection, climbing and work needs, and ceremonial textiles without an approved product catalog. |
| Broad processing method | Shearing or collection, cleaning, drying, carding or equivalent preparation, spinning, weaving or felting, cutting, stitching, full repair, and careful retirement establish the process range. |
| Fuel and water needs | Washing and hide work require limited clean water; drying and finishing require hearth heat and protected space. Water use and dirty runoff matter sharply in small settlements. |
| Waste and risk | Overgrazing, animal loss, contaminated water, tannery waste, smoke, fire in dry stock, damp storage, and labor diverted from winter preparation are ordinary risks. |
| Stewardship and access | Highstead households and shared pasture customs can limit use where slope cover or herd survival is threatened. Makers may refuse unsafe or dishonestly sourced hides. Exact legal procedure remains open. |
| Scarcity reason | Herd health, pasture limits, seasonal shearing, difficult washing and drying, route closures, and the time required for durable repair constrain cloth. |
| Regional cultural meaning | Layered, repaired clothing shows preparedness and the history of mutual help. Visible stitches or clan colors may carry identity, but pattern alone cannot prove kinship or provenance. |
| Broad cross-region dependency | Lowmark may supply plant fiber or cloth; Mire and Emberholt household links may carry repair methods or finished cloth. No borrowed custom is canon without a carrier and chronology. |

## Foundation 3 — dyes and oils

| Required field | Proposed foundation |
|---|---|
| World source or import dependency | Small amounts of colorant come from ordinary plant, wood, and mineral-earth byproducts collected during existing herbal, woodland, and stone work. Animal byproducts support limited grease or rendered-fat use; liquid oils and colors not available from those ordinary streams are imported. No named source, color catalog, or exclusive origin is approved. |
| Who gathers or imports it | Households, herbal gatherers, woodland workers, herding households, stone workers, carriers, and traders supply small-scale processors. No exclusive gatherer or source is created. |
| Who processes it | Household workers, banner keepers, leather workers, healers, and ritual caretakers can steep, render, press, mix, store, or apply ordinary preparations in the broad sense. |
| Broad processing method | Sorting, drying, crushing, steeping, pressing or rendering, straining, warming, and careful storage define possible work without choosing a plant, fat, pigment, formula, or product. |
| Fuel and water needs | Modest hearth heat and water support preparation; lamp or finishing oils compete with food, leather, medicine, and fuel uses once an input is selected. |
| Waste and risk | Smoke, staining, spoiled or rancid stores, flammability, contaminated water, skin irritation, and overcollection of useful plants or resin are the main risks. |
| Stewardship and access | Households and cairn or banner custodians control ceremonial use; highstead access rules can prevent collection from unsafe or depleted slopes. No color is made magically authoritative. |
| Scarcity reason | Seasonal growth, labor, competing uses, import disruption, storage loss, and stewardship limits create scarcity without a rare magical pigment. |
| Regional cultural meaning | Color and oil can mark care, warning, mourning, welcome, or maintained equipment. Meaning comes from agreed custom and custody, never from supernatural authentication. |
| Broad cross-region dependency | Mire herbal and resin precedent and Lowmark agricultural or market exchange offer broad possible inputs. Emberholt may provide finished color work. Exact substances and routes remain open. |

## Foundation 4 — ceramics and glass

| Required field | Proposed foundation |
|---|---|
| World source or import dependency | Thornfell does not currently justify local clay extraction, pottery kilns, or glassmaking. Durable fired vessels and glass should therefore be treated mainly as imports and carefully maintained goods. Local stoneworking can meet some container and surface needs without pretending to be ceramic production. |
| Who gathers or imports it | Traders, carriers, and shelter or highstead buyers receive fired goods from broader exchange. Local stoneworkers and repairers maintain non-fired local alternatives and fit imported components. |
| Who processes it | In Thornfell, the ordinary work is inspection, repair, safe storage, fitting, and reuse of sound shards only where appropriate. Primary potters and glassworkers remain outside the region unless separately approved later. |
| Broad processing method | Stone carving and fitting are local precedents. Cleaning, binding or mounting, and protected storage support imported fired goods. No local firing, glazing, glass batch, or kiln process is asserted. |
| Fuel and water needs | Repair and cleaning use modest water and heat. Avoiding a local kiln prevents unsupported competition for already scarce fuel. Any later firing proposal must separately justify clay, fuel, smoke, ash, and fire safety. |
| Waste and risk | Breakage in transit, thermal shock, sharp shards, fraudulent repair, loss during closure, and the high replacement burden make careful custody necessary. Resonant fragments are not used as repair stock. |
| Stewardship and access | Shelter keepers and households prioritize intact containers for water, food, care, and records. Imported decorative use can signify status, but no class rule is fixed. |
| Scarcity reason | Fragile transport, pass closures, replacement delay, and competing household uses make fired goods scarce even when ordinary elsewhere. |
| Regional cultural meaning | A complete vessel in a remote shelter signals preparation and trust. Repaired vessels can mark continuity, while imported glass may indicate connection rather than local mastery. |
| Broad cross-region dependency | Lowmark and Emberholt provide broad pottery or glass exchange possibilities after their own sources are selected. No direct supply line or exclusive origin is approved. |

## Foundation 5 — fuel and resource stewardship

| Required field | Proposed foundation |
|---|---|
| World source or import dependency | Mountain woodland, ordinary brush or wood waste, and existing resin or fuel precedent can support bounded household and craft heat; imported fuel may cover shortages. No deposit, species, charcoal grade, or fuel catalog is selected. |
| Who gathers or imports it | Highstead work parties, cutters, bridge and shelter crews, carriers, and traders gather or receive fuel under local access decisions. Smiths, cooks, textile workers, and households compete for it. |
| Who processes it | Cutters and yard workers dry and size wood; fuel burners prepare ordinary charcoal at controlled sites; storekeepers allocate dry fuel through winter and repair seasons. No charcoal grade or production scale is selected. |
| Broad processing method | Selective cutting, collection of safe waste, seasoning, sheltered storage, controlled burning, ash removal, and replenishment planning establish the chain. Stone and metal work retain their existing ordinary quarry, sorting, smithing, and repair foundations. |
| Fuel and water needs | Fuel is itself the constrained input; water and cleared space are necessary for fire control. Smithing, cooking, drying, warmth, and any future firing cannot all claim priority at once. |
| Waste and risk | Slope erosion, lost root cover, landslide, wildfire, smoke, unstable cuts, unsafe pits, ash, and winter shortfall make extraction a public survival issue. |
| Stewardship and access | Wardens, bridgewrights, highstead or clan work bodies, and makers may close unsafe slopes, reserve structural timber, protect cover, and require repair labor. This does not settle Assembly procedure or ownership law. |
| Scarcity reason | Seasonal access, ecological limits, postwar slope damage, seasoning time, labor, fire risk, and competing uses constrain supply. Rare resonance is not a scarcity mechanism for ordinary resources. |
| Regional cultural meaning | Restraint proves competence: a slope left uncut or a fire kept small may be more honorable than visible extraction. Resource custody grows from keeping passes, shelters, and neighbors alive. |
| Broad cross-region dependency | Lowmark food and market access, Mire herbs or resins, and Emberholt metal or technical exchange form broad reciprocal ties. None creates a Stonebound network or exact route. |

## What this brief deliberately leaves open

- exact foods, crops, herds, fibers, hides, dyes, oils, clays, glass inputs, woods, fuels, ores, stone, and finished goods;
- pasture law, Assembly procedure, exact source sites, roads, durations, volumes, prices, quotas, grades, and exclusive suppliers;
- local pottery or glassmaking unless a later source and fuel case supports it;
- C03, C06, C07, and all Founder or Volkhari craft links;
- all equipment, recipe, gathering, progression, and schema work.

## Foundation result

Thornfell now has proposal-level foundations for food resilience, weather textiles, bounded colorant and oil work, imported fired-goods dependence, and fuel stewardship. These fit its established stone, metal, wood, leather, and repair identity without turning resonance into industry. This is sufficient for a later readiness rerun, not a catalog or live-canon amendment.
