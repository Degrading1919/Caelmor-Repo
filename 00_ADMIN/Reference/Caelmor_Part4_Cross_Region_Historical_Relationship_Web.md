## Historical shape

Caelmor's four regions were connected **before** the Great War by ordinary dependencies: Lowmark's crossings and food, Thornfell's pass and bridge work, the Mire's knowledge of difficult ground and care, and Emberholt's civic and guild demand. The Great War (5–10 years, ending about thirty years ago) strained those connections rather than creating them from nothing. Some ties became coercive, some became refuge, and some became useful precisely because two communities did not agree about politics. The first postwar years made local caretakers and repair offices powerful; the later decades left adult witnesses beside children of the displaced and people who know the war only through a household duty.

The following are not six bilateral mini histories. A Lowmark load can appear in a Thornfell departure dispute and a Mire care account without one continent-wide relief office. An Emberholt civic repairer can be sincerely thanked in one place and politically mistrusted in another. A displaced person may belong to the region that raised them and to the region whose records still list them as missing. The connection is a chain of people and custody, not a shared symbol or automatic Remnant plot.

## Triangular and four-region consequences without a single hidden organization

1. **One person, several accurate records.** A Thornfell cairn can mark the last known moment, a Mire care entry a later survival, and a Lowmark departure copy an earlier identity. A present family may need all three before correcting a name. Each institution preserves its own duty; none is a universal master ledger. This is a proposed evidence pattern, not proof that one named person traveled that exact route.
2. **A repair credited twice and felt three ways.** A Lowmark crossing may become usable through local keepers and Thornfell workers while an Emberholt civic/guild account later credits its own contribution. The disagreement can concern labor, payment, and public gratitude. A visible repair sequence plus independently kept work accounts, not a matching mason's mark, is required to choose a case. This is a proposal that gives three regions distinct stakes without alleging theft.
3. **Food, shelter, and names after the armistice.** Part 2's Repair Bargains let one region supply food, another passage, and a third temporary care. Emberholt's city can also send workers or receive migrants. There is no continental relief command: a present household may inherit a moral obligation that its neighbor considers already discharged. Selecting a particular four-region event would require four independent custody points and map verification; Part 4 does not assert one.
4. **The supernatural cannot close the file.** C11's Summit coupling, C14's one chapel, C15's two major earth-memory threats, and postwar dreams can create danger, memory, or a reason to ask. They cannot name a culprit, validate a genealogy, identify a war order, or replace a living witness. The later understanding comes from people choosing how to hold and correct evidence.

## Editorial ownership and limits

The first region encountered should be able to resolve a present local need: open or close a route safely, correct a current tariff, recognize a family, care for a returnee, or amend a memorial with honest uncertainty. A second region may later reframe why that need existed. This protects the Phase 1 Lowmark-only v1 boundary; no row promises four playable regions, a specific quest count, or a finished narrative arc.

Every selected row still needs a claim card with (1) source status, (2) source-supported or proposed experiencer, (3) independently dated evidence at each end, (4) the specific disagreement, (5) what remains unproved, (6) the owner of a present resolution, and (7) a route check before exact scene placement. No exact ages, travel times, road order, Founders' roster, Volkhari population, Lowmark farm-water code, or precursor founding date are inferred here. Repeated phrases, stitches, marks, and meals remain leads only. The Remnant is not the cause of a civilian omission without direct case evidence.
