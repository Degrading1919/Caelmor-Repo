# Caelmor artistic decision methodology

**Use** — Direction, original concept development, regional visual languages, asset diagnosis and production handoff. This working method supports the Creative Director under the Master Assistant Protocol. Every resulting new visual detail is a working art proposal unless its authority is documented. Visual analysis can be useful at any stage. The evidence required to accept the work rises with the claim.

## 1. Set the brief in player terms

State the player, task, approach and viewing distance, the emotion to support, and what must be clear or remain discoverable. Choose a testable sentence such as “On the road approach, the crossing reads as the public route before the player notices the watch stair.” Record the actual evidence available for camera, movement, interaction, combat space and scene state. Treat assumptions as a shot brief until verified. Separate the approved fact, proposal or constraint that grounds each critical claim from an artist's working judgment. For a review, first identify the artifact revision and the view or region of the image under discussion.

Write down the **invariants** before looking at reference aesthetics. For architecture these can include a passable opening, working sequence, protected exit, support and water/heat path. For a character they can include movement range and readable weapon tell. For a prop they can include the silhouette and reach target. If the player-facing objective is ambiguous, compare plausible reads and proceed with a reversible assumption. For an approved state change, define the interface shared by every state, such as route, interaction point and standing space. Compare the smallest readable change in dressing, finish or light with a geometric change where a new function must be visible. Document a fixed footprint only where it serves this project's needs.

## 2. Derive forms from causes

Use the chain **site and social need → action or making process → spatial or physical arrangement → construction → what the player sees**. Ask what supports it, how someone uses it, what gets wet or hot, who maintains it, and which choices expose a meaningful difference between communities. Then specify visible consequences. Example proposal for a Lowmark wet-work shelter: raise the dry tool rack above splash height, keep the wet receiving edge low and give the public approach a dry side. These three cues explain where work happens and where the player goes.

For buildings, sketch public, service and work paths in plan, and structure, drainage, venting, headroom and camera occlusion in section. Locate the people and materials in each step. For landscape, reason about flow, slope, visibility, habitat and route sequence. For objects and characters, reason about grip, contact, leverage, range of motion, storage, repair and use. For culturally meaningful forms, identify a transmission mechanism such as shared craft knowledge or inherited construction, and specify the local adaptation. Use condition to show a physical or social event that would leave the particular trace. A patched sill has a different cause and consequence from expensive stone sorting or a better carpentry joint.

## 3. Choose a concept by comparing how it works

Develop two to four genuinely different organizing ideas for new design work. Vary a route, section, construction family, handling sequence or visual hierarchy. Compare at a common camera and scale when images are available. Disqualify options that violate binding canon, critical interactions, clarity or build constraints. Among the remaining options choose by the strongest intended read and smallest credible scope, and name the meaningful tradeoff. Use one bounded correction for a well-diagnosed defect. Put unresolved observations behind the cheapest discriminating check. When a gate is under-evidenced, give a conditional choice and state what would change it.

Separate the visual cues that carry identity from the parts that can change. If a route or encounter needs a new layout, test whether the material family, proportion, rhythm or focal contrast survives the change. Reassess any cue that impairs play. Record which cue was retained, what moved and what made the old arrangement fail.

Order design by scale.

- **Approach** — Distinguish principal mass or silhouette, route void and a single dominant value or shape cue. The asset must be identifiable against the actual background, in motion, and at the relevant camera extremes.
- **Use** — Group repeated bays, thresholds, joints, grips or pose landmarks into a legible rhythm. Break rhythm at a functional focal point. Use surrounding quiet values to keep the path, interaction or readable enemy tell distinct.
- **Discovery** — Place a small, local trace where the action or history would have left it. Separate what a player sees immediately from what they can infer on inspection. Preserve ordinary ongoing use alongside historical damage. Give clues redundancy through position, shape, motion, sound or value when color or small lettering alone would fail.

Set color through **value grouping first**, then substrate hue, lighting and controlled accents. Compare neutral material to diagnose form, material under common light to diagnose finish, and scene lighting to diagnose presentation. Use hue and chroma to differentiate related places, layers and activities where they aid the read. Keep substrate, construction, craft quality, age, repairs and lighting conceptually separate. Each can have its own material or geometry control if supported and justified. The richest asset may be well maintained or neglected. High age can coexist with fresh repairs.

## 4. Define the family and its controlled variation

For a visual language, specify the invariant functional program, the region's proposed construction and material logic, its range of local variation, and a counterexample that would be interchangeable with another region. Make a grammar by writing **rule, cause, visible cue, permissible range and rejection condition**. For a settlement or family, compare a small number of actual uses or social contexts side by side. Give each row a local activity, silhouette and assembly, shared versus local materials, the value grouping in scene light, and a related tool, textile or motif if it follows from that activity. Check that the shared language is still visible across categories. Use regional facts as inputs and describe inferred art rules as proposals. One place may contain imported craft, exceptional sites or different status levels without weakening the underlying language.

Vary independently where the task and tooling allow.

| Driver | First lever | Evidence of meaningful change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Function | Mass hierarchy, voids, working adjacencies and circulation | In a clay comparison viewers identify the job and point to the feature that explains it |
| Site and regional practice | Foundation, support, section, enclosure and locally credible material assemblies | Matched function reads differently across sites without relying on named palettes |
| Capacity or status | Working area, privacy, approach and material preparation | A viewer can explain who uses it and how capacity or access changed at equivalent condition |
| Growth, seed or local exposure | Site-constrained changes in orientation, bay depth, span or roof relationship | Several controlled variants differ in meaningful form, while shared function and clearances survive |
| Age and maintenance | Contact wear, exposure, localized repair and replacement | Each alteration has a plausible cause and support, rather than evenly applied visual noise |

For a concept family, compare at least two instances when practical. Change one driver at a time in diagnostic comparisons. Treat dimensions, budgets and render settings as observed requirements or proposed test values with a rationale. Reuse existing assets, sockets, modules or surfaces only where interfaces and artistic roles agree. Allocate unique work to a focal or functional dependency that cannot be served by the base family. For each repeated piece, state the exact proposed count, join or attachment, allowed transforms and exposed edge variants. Test one assembled cluster where seams and repetition are most visible. Keep access clear and recalibrate scale-dependent cues when pieces are varied. Prefer one working Lowmark example and one discriminating variant before catalog expansion.

## 5. Translate the chosen concept into a buildable instruction

Use the smallest artifact that answers the next question.

1. **Location and silhouette** — Plan or diagram with approach, scale proxy, intended view and one quiet fallback route or angle when applicable. Use blockout or draw over a real viewport to find overlap before detail.
2. **Look development** — Neutral form, shared-light material view, then in-context value study. Make color, weather or history decisions visible at the intended distance. Compare the shortest viable correction against its baseline.
3. **Production sheet** — Include relevant views or plan/section, scale, repeated pieces versus unique pieces, seams and joins, controlled states, materials, invariants and what can move. Annotate what the build may simplify based on visibility, use and interaction. A detail earns bespoke work if it is needed to identify an action, carry a story beat at its actual viewing distance, or resolve a real join. Where relevant include the visible and reverse faces, player scale, standing space, interaction target or default animation dependency. Check that a work object reads statically when animation is absent. Reference known inputs and interfaces. For a generator, map the change to the existing semantic plan, mesh role, material control, or scene dressing layer where supported. Record a fixed seed or input pair and protected-edit/export implications only when relevant.
4. **Handoff** — Give the responsible role the mission, source of truth, exact affected element, prioritized change, protected invariants, cheapest check and evidence required for acceptance. Example “Move the receiving shelf onto the dry side of the opening. Keep the clear passage and runoff edge. Compare the same approach and reverse views in clay and verify the loaded route in the native mesh.” Include an expected artifact or an explicitly missing dependency.

Keep the production review focused on one uncertainty per evidence pass. Capture only the affected configurations plus a comparison control. A beautiful angle can illustrate intent. Acceptance needs evidence at the operating angle or in the relevant host. Use a working state, route, handoff or rig before spending on a presentation image.

## 6. Diagnose and verify

Report only the highest-impact issues that the evidence supports. Each issue should name **where and what is observed → probable mechanism → player or build consequence → concrete correction → falsification check**. Include what already works so the implementer can protect it. Distinguish image observation, source description, likely cause, and directly verified behavior. For example, a streak at a roof joint might be paint, lighting or an intersecting post. A neutral close view and a section through that join discriminate before any palette change.

Check hard gates first and record each as **Pass**, **Revise** or **Evidence needed**, only for the current stage.

- **Authority and originality** — Can the critical decisions be traced to current project authority or explicitly labeled proposals? Would the design make sense with the external reference removed? If recognizable reference silhouette, composition and motifs still coincide as an ensemble, retain the abstract design problem and derive a different physical arrangement and visual read from Caelmor.
- **Player use and composition** — Can a viewer identify the job, path, interaction or silhouette at the intended camera and state? Can a player execute it with the current traversal, rig or interaction contract? Test both when claiming both.
- **Construction and visual language** — Does section or object assembly explain how loads, people, weather or use flow? Are distinct regions, functions and status legible in controlled neutral and material comparisons as relevant?
- **Production** — Is there a bounded reusable deliverable with named owner and verification? Are there measurements for any stated compatibility, performance or integration claims?

Describe the evidence level precisely. A source/plan describes intent. A clay or material view demonstrates what it shows from that camera. A native scene can verify mesh and authoring behavior. A checked export demonstrates the interchange path exercised. A Unity test proves only its tested runtime claims. A human playtest or Creative Director review is distinct from model-based interpretation and technical test success. End with a clear disposition for this stage and the next dependency-ready check.

## Caelmor regional starting points

These are starting propositions for investigation. Confirm region, settlement, season, activity and project scope from current sources on every consequential design. Lowmark is the v1 delivery focus. The other three examples are continuity tools for studies or authorized work.

| Region | Canon anchor | Reversible visual direction to test | Counterexample |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lowmark Vale | Fields, fisheries, river trade, shared water works, practical rebuilding and quiet remembrance | Allow river approaches, raised dry stock and low work edges to generate legible profiles. Give thresholds and repair patches the clearest evidence of flood adaptation. Let ordinary trade and labor carry the scene. | A generic cottage with river-colored trim and a door opening onto the wet handling lane |
| Thornfell Marches | Wind, steep ridges, narrow passes, cairns and bridgewrights | Let windward shelter and stepped bearing drive section. Cut deep, protected thresholds and put lookouts where the route opens. Reserve ceremonial stone cadence for documented contexts. | A scaled-up Lowmark roof in cool colors or a ceremonial gable on every work building |
| Mire of Fallen Banners | Saturated ground, war traces, elevated Sorrowmere paths, flood-resistant longhouses, memory keeping | Let the dry walking datum, braced support, contact with water and landing sequence generate forms. Separate maintained living structures from partial, quiet wartime remnants. | A grounded stone house tinted moss green, or indiscriminate ruin dressing around a working landing |
| Emberholt Heights | Volcanic stone, proud craft, industrial heat and controlled practical emberwork | Show heat and work moving through sheltered hearths, vented upper volumes and stone bearing masses. Use iron where it visibly does a job. Separate everyday warmth from rare dangerous spectacle. | A timber settlement painted black, a modern slab block with dark windows, or universal emissive surfaces |

## Boundaries for production assistance

The Creative Director retains material direction and canon acceptance. Route narrative and regional intent to the World & Quest Designer, interaction and player readability to the Gameplay Designer, production schemas and JSON to the Schema & Content Engineer, and C# or runtime validation to the Systems Engineer. For offline art authoring, give the implementation brief to the Creative Director or their authorized operator. Preserve the official five-role structure. Work within the assigned mission and current file governance. Escalate foundational direction/scope, binding contract changes, equal-authority contradictions or genuinely inaccessible dependencies after retrieval. Record routine in-bounds tradeoffs at the milestone. The methodology itself grants no write, publishing or approval authority.
