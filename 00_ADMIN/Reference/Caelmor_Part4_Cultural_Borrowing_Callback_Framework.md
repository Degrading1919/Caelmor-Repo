# Caelmor Part 4 — cultural borrowing and delayed-understanding framework

**Status:** additive proposal for Creative Director review, not canon, quest scripts, or a production commitment. This document uses the current Part 2/3 proposals and detailed dossiers as sources; Creative Director rulings through C15 control disputed facts. It describes how a custom or clue may travel between people without treating every resemblance as one hidden tradition.

## The burden of a shared sign

A mark, phrase, meal, memorial form, or repair habit first has a **local use**. Similarity elsewhere is a lead. A borrowing claim needs a plausible carrier (a household, apprentice, caretaker, worker, letter, or institution), a chronology in which the transfer could occur, and a second source that can corroborate the relationship. Different meanings can survive in both places. The older meaning is not automatically the “true” one; an adopted custom may become locally important for reasons its originator never intended.

The evidence ladder for any callback is:

1. **Observation:** what someone can see or hear without knowing its history.
2. **Local explanation:** what neighbors use it for now, including honest disagreement.
3. **Possible carrier:** a named household, occupation, record exchange, migration, or documented visit. An anonymous motif alone stops here.
4. **Independent check:** a dated or placed record, living testimony checked against another custodian, or a physical repair sequence that can distinguish copying from coincidence.
5. **Re-reading:** the later evidence changes a present relationship or obligation. It does not have to solve every old uncertainty.
6. **Owner:** the community that must act on the new understanding, even if another region supplies evidence.

If steps 3–4 cannot be established, preserve parallel invention, a general human response to grief/work, or an unresolved resemblance as valid outcomes. No Stonebound response, Dreamer vision, earth-memory manifestation, or Remnant rumor can replace a custody chain. C11 Stonebound responses do not report history; C15's General and Sorrowback are not dead eyewitnesses. A genuine Remnant operation needs its own case evidence rather than a convenient explanation for every gap.

## Callback pacing without full quest authoring

The first encounter should present a useful local fact: a route closure, hospitable cup, unfinished name, disputed wage, repaired bridge, or memorial attribution. An initial local outcome should stand on its own. A later encounter elsewhere may supply one missing piece and change whom the player understands to have borne a cost. A final return belongs to the region or household that must act; it may correct a record, reopen a duty, acknowledge an overlooked person, or preserve uncertainty honestly. The return is an option for later story planning, not a scripted quest or a promise that all candidates appear in v1.

Phase 1 keeps v1 in Lowmark with one main arc. A Lowmark mark or family account can thus be meaningful locally before later-region material exists. A future callback must not retroactively make the original local resolution false or every ordinary kindness a secret clue.

## Cultural differences that should survive contact

Lowmark tends to turn a repeated practice into workable stewardship or a written claim; Thornfell asks which household or crew stood the risk; the Mire asks whether a name or grief has been handled without false certainty; Emberholt may frame an act as public safety, civic pride, household devotion, or guild competence. These are tendencies in the detailed dossiers and Part 3 proposals, not universal personality rules. Migrants can resist, combine, or reject them. Borrowing is most interesting when a person keeps the act but changes its purpose, and a later record makes that change legible without proving one region owned the other.

**Part 4 boundary:** no universal shared code, hidden four-region care society, fifth element, all-seeing ancient witness, or Remnant authorship of every omission is proposed. No material catalog, item, resource, recipe, or quest script is implied by these cultural practices.
