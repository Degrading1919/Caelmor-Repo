# Caelmor Part 4 — shared events and disputed-history ledger

**Status:** proposal-status work for Creative Director review. This document changes no canonical source. It takes Phase 1 canon, the Stage A register, Part 2's historical backbone, and Part 3's regional proposals as its authority order. `Anchored` identifies an existing source or a Director ruling; `Proposed reconstruction` is a usable connective reading awaiting review. No row creates an exact date, named road, travel time, historical meeting, or new canon record.

## Operating boundaries

The Great War was a 5–10-year conflict ending about thirty years before the present. Its seven-year Part 2 spine is editorial scaffolding, not a calendar. The wartime precursor later became the formal Remnant, but C03 leaves its name, precise transition, and personnel continuity open. Dreamers arise after the war and require independent corroboration. C11's Stonebound response is passive and place-bound; it cannot testify to an event. The Chapel of the Sinking Star / Sunken Chapel / Sunken Church is one site, and the General Who Wouldn't Fall and the Sorrowback are the Mire's two major earth-memory threats; neither is historical testimony.

The ledger treats a disagreement as meaningful when two parties preserve different duties, not when one has a decorative motif. A later reveal needs two independent channels: for example, a local physical trace plus a record, or two differently situated human accounts. A matching phrase, mark, dream, or supernatural event alone never completes a chain.

## Evidence classes

| Class | What it can establish | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Anchored regional condition** | A region's known role: Lowmark provisioning and crossings; Thornfell pass, signal, and bridge work; Mire care and memorial work; Emberholt civic firecraft and the Glassed Hollow aftermath. | The identity, route, motive, or fate of a particular person. |
| **Administrative trace** | That a person, load, repair, toll, closure, or claim entered one institution's work. | That the trace was complete, honest, or correctly understood. |
| **Household / worker account** | What a person saw, carried, was told, or needed at one moment. | The larger command decision or all events beyond that person's reach. |
| **Place and material trace** | That disruption, repair, burial, collapse, or later reuse occurred at a site. | Who caused it or why, without a second source. |
| **Resonance, memory, or dream** | A present experience that may prompt inquiry. | Historical fact, identity, prophecy, guilt, or an account of the dead. |

## Event ledger

### 1. Prewar passage obligations — Lowmark ↔ Thornfell

**Canonical anchor:** Lowmark's grain and river/crossing economy and Thornfell's pass, bridge, and escort work are established regional conditions. Part 2 proposes that repeated passage bargains preceded the war; it does not establish a single treaty or route.

**Proposed reconstruction:** A recurring exchange of staple loads, repair labor, and closure warnings made both regions dependent on the other's timing. A Lowmark clerk could count an obligation met when a load left a mill; a Thornfell household could count it unmet until an escort brought it through a closure.

| Required story-web field | Ledger entry |
| --- | --- |
| Original local event / relationship | Seasonal passage and repair obligations become disputed when war closures interrupt them. |
| Who experienced or recorded it | Mill, ferry, toll, bridge, horn, and household custodians; no existing named person is assigned authorship. |
| Other-region account | Lowmark preserves departure and liability; Thornfell preserves arrival, shelter, and whether warning was usable. |
| Where accounts differ | Completion, delay, and the duty owed to stranded civilians. |
| Independent evidence | A freight or toll notation paired with a bridge repair layer, horn roster, or household receipt. |
| Reveal / resolution owner | Thornfell owns passage and family recognition; Lowmark owns the administrative claim. |
| Present consequence | A current tariff, repair-credit, or safety-closure dispute can be resolved without proving wartime intent. |

### 2. The relief-column failure — Lowmark ↔ Mire

**Canonical anchor:** Lowmark supplied war movement; the Mire became a central site of battlefield care, displaced people, and uncounted dead. Part 2 proposes the Mire Relief Column and its incomplete record.

**Proposed reconstruction:** A Lowmark carrier group reached a Mire refuge or infirmary under a relief designation that did not record every dependent household. Its later Lowmark paper may read as a completed delivery, while Mire testimony preserves the people left awaiting care after an escort or conditions changed.

| Required story-web field | Ledger entry |
| --- | --- |
| Original local event / relationship | A civilian relief transfer under wartime strain. |
| Who experienced or recorded it | Carriers and receiving caretakers; a surviving household record may preserve the wait rather than the official transfer. |
| Other-region account | Lowmark sees a rationed load and completed duty; Mire sees care triage, names, and uncertainty. |
| Where accounts differ | Whether a missing person was abandoned, redirected, or simply never recorded by the same office. |
| Independent evidence | Lowmark freight/household paper plus Mire testimony or burial/care notation, checked against a family object or living kin account. |
| Reveal / resolution owner | Mire owns the name and memorial decision; Lowmark owns correction of a relief privilege or false completion claim. |
| Present consequence | A present household may inherit an exemption born of real hardship even if its current holder no longer needs it. |

### 3. Stores, heat, and refusal — Lowmark ↔ Emberholt

**Canonical anchor:** Emberholt depends on Lowmark grain and trade; Lowmark remembers pressure to turn water and provisioning toward war. The Part 2 Ashen Flats and ration argument are proposed readings of established war damage and supply roles.

**Proposed reconstruction:** Emergency Emberholt orders changed ordinary Lowmark stores into priority supply. Some were needed for civilian heat, kitchens, and repair; some may have served wartime demands. The phrase authorizing priority may survive after the circumstance that made it defensible.

| Required story-web field | Ledger entry |
| --- | --- |
| Original local event / relationship | A supply obligation joins food-producing households to an overheated city under emergency demand. |
| Who experienced or recorded it | Granary, barge, guild, and household workers; not a preassigned Remnant operation. |
| Other-region account | Lowmark remembers stores leaving and risk shifting onto households; Emberholt remembers shortages and civic safety needs. |
| Where accounts differ | Whether priority meant mutual relief, coercion, military provisioning, or several of these across separate loads. |
| Independent evidence | A Lowmark ledger margin plus an Emberholt guild, ward, or household demand record; physical field/bank evidence can date disruption but not motive. |
| Reveal / resolution owner | Lowmark owns present allocation or contract remedy; Emberholt owns public credit for any civilian repair work. |
| Present consequence | Current buyers confront an inherited emergency clause without treating all Emberholt workers as heirs to the precursor. |

### 4. Shatterfall's separated households — Thornfell ↔ Mire

**Proposed reconstruction:** A pass closure separated households from the party expected to receive them in the Mire. Thornfell's first account names people lost at the closure; Mire's later account may name a person only as an unnamed patient, guide, or burial duty.

### 5. The inspection warning that did not join up — Thornfell ↔ Emberholt

**Canonical anchor:** Firecraft experiment and missing-inspection tensions precede the war; Thornfell ridge warnings and Emberholt civic/industrial interests are established. The failed inquiry is Part 2's proposed immediate spark. C11 permits a passive Summit response only under disturbance and never as evidence.

**Proposed reconstruction:** Separate warnings about fire risk, route safety, and escort conditions reached different offices. One side later calls the other silent; the surviving papers may show partial answers to questions that were never actually the same.

| Required story-web field | Ledger entry |
| --- | --- |
| Original local event / relationship | Prewar scrutiny of hazardous fire practice collides with pass and escort caution. |
| Who experienced or recorded it | Ridge signal/route workers and Emberholt inspectors, guild officers, or archive custodians. |
| Other-region account | Thornfell remembers ignored danger and pressure for access; Emberholt remembers an inquiry that lacked workable evidence or threatened civic continuity. |
| Where accounts differ | What warning was sent, who had authority to act, and whether delay was negligence, self-protection, or confusion. |
| Independent evidence | A Thornfell signal/closure record plus an Emberholt inquiry, work order, or inspection gap, with a physical site condition where available. |
| Reveal / resolution owner | Thornfell owns the closure decision; Emberholt owns archival correction and civic accountability. |
| Present consequence | Current technical cooperation is possible without asking either side to accept the other's wartime story whole. |

The Echochamber Summit may produce a passive physical or resonant response if current disturbance warrants it. It cannot authenticate the warning, identify responsible people, or supply a lost account.

### 6. Glassed Hollow's civilian afterlife — Mire ↔ Emberholt

**Canonical anchor:** Glassed Hollow, the precursor initiate becoming the Fire Titan, Kaelith's freedom, Thalan's current captivity, and the false "forge collapse" public account are governed by the Director rulings and Part 2 proposal framing. Mire communities preserve war harm through care and testimony.

**Proposed reconstruction:** Emberholt public loss records include ordinary workers and households harmed by the catastrophe or its cover story. Mire accounts preserve evacuees, convalescents, and people whose origins were omitted. Neither archive alone can establish a full casualty list.

| Who experienced or recorded it | Emberholt civic/guild record keepers and Mire caretakers, refugees, or families. |

This row does not set the C06 Volkhari population, C03 precursor membership, or any new captive history.

### 7. The Stopping and borrowed repair — all regions

**Canonical anchor:** The war ends without a durable continent-wide victor or universal magical ban. Authorities broadly reject weaponization, amplification, uncontrolled ritual experimentation, and dangerous research; regional practice diverges. The Stopping and Repair Bargains are Part 2 proposals.

**Proposed reconstruction:** In the first postwar years, households and local institutions exchanged whatever they could safely offer: Lowmark food and crossings, Thornfell escort and repair knowledge, Mire care and names, Emberholt labor and heat safety. Similar repair methods and memorial habits can result from direct work contact, later adoption, or parallel need; similarity does not prove one origin.

| Required story-web field | Ledger entry |
| --- | --- |
| Original local event / relationship | Local recovery bargains keep people fed, sheltered, named, and moving after collapse. |
| Who experienced or recorded it | Workers, returning households, local office records, chapel/cooperative care, and guild accounts. |
| Other-region account | Each region preserves the service it offered and the cost another region failed to see. |
| Where accounts differ | Credit, repayment, and whether a borrowed custom was aid, obligation, or later adaptation. |
| Independent evidence | A repair layer or household practice plus two records from differently situated regions; map placement must precede any route claim. |
| Reveal / resolution owner | The region where the present claim, custom, or public credit is contested owns its resolution; other regions corroborate only their own duties. |
| Present consequence | Civilian cooperation survives beside political resentment and does not depend on the Remnant or supernatural forces. |

### 8. Managed memory and the missing verse — postwar, cross-regional

**Canonical anchor:** The formal Remnant develops after the wartime precursor; local recovery institutions and differing archives persist. Dreamers emerge only after the war. Part 3 permits different household memorial customs and cautions against a universal shared code.

**Proposed reconstruction:** A work or memorial verse, repair phrase, or hospitality practice appears in more than one region after the war. It may be a documented borrowing through displaced families or workers, a separately adapted response to grief, or a false resemblance. No shared continental ritual is assumed.

| Required story-web field | Ledger entry |
| --- | --- |
| Original local event / relationship | A postwar household or work practice changes after people from different regions share recovery labor. |
| Who experienced or recorded it | Families, workers, school/community keepers, and local memorial or guild custodians. |
| Other-region account | Another region recognizes a partial phrase or use but attaches a different local meaning. |
| Where accounts differ | Origin, authorship, and whether the practice commemorates loss, safety, welcome, or all three. |
| Independent evidence | A dated local copy or securely placed inscription plus a separate family/work record that establishes actual contact. Dreams and motifs remain prompts only. |
| Reveal / resolution owner | The household or local institution using the practice owns whether and how it changes; no region may overwrite the other's meaning. |
| Present consequence | Cultural borrowing becomes visible as lived recovery rather than an ancient secret system. |

### 9. The orchard-bed arrangement — later postwar, Lowmark ↔ Mire

**Canonical anchor:** Lowmark receives war-displaced people and has orchard, council, and record cultures; Mire communities retain care, testimony, and household obligations after the war. Part 3 proposes that some displaced households never returned and that a present claim can preserve uncertainty rather than a tidy death record.

**Proposed reconstruction:** A Mire household temporarily places an adult or child kin-member with a Lowmark orchard or river household while its own home situation remains unsettled. The arrangement becomes long-term through work, care, and consent rather than a formal regional resettlement scheme. Lowmark's local record can treat the person as a worker or dependent; Mire kin may remember the arrangement as a promise to return.

| Who experienced or recorded it | The two households, a local council/land or work record, and kin who carried the original request. |

| Present consequence | Descendants can negotiate belonging, inheritance, or memorial language without treating one side's vocabulary as a fraudulent version of the other. |

### 10. The cooled-work crew — later postwar, Thornfell ↔ Emberholt

**Canonical anchor:** Thornfell retains bridge, masonry, signal, and closure work; Emberholt depends on civic repair and heat safety. Part 2's Repair Bargains are proposed local exchanges, while C12 allows regional public practice without a universal magic prohibition.

**Proposed reconstruction:** After the war, an Emberholt repair body seeks practical highland stone or load-bearing advice for a hazardous civic worksite. Thornfell workers provide a bounded method or labor under a local work agreement. The contact is ordinary technical cooperation; it neither proves a fixed route nor makes all later shared techniques a common tradition.

### 11. The return-care register — later postwar, Mire ↔ Emberholt

**Canonical anchor:** Mire care institutions carry war aftermath; Emberholt has civic fire safety, guild, household, and lower-ward needs. Part 3 treats convalescence, returnees, testimony, and ordinary civic care as viable relationships between these regions.

**Proposed reconstruction:** An Emberholt worker or household member recovers in Mire care after an ordinary postwar accident, illness, or displacement-related strain, then returns or sends support back through personal contacts. A Mire register keeps the care duty; an Emberholt household or work record preserves the absence and return. The incident is not a Glassed Hollow clue and has no supernatural cause.

| Other-region account | Mire remembers care, uncertainty, and what recovery required; Emberholt remembers lost wages, altered household work, and a return. |

## Continuity and deferral rules

- Do not assign a direct Great War memory, exact age, marriage, fosterage, adoption, or travel itinerary to an existing anchor until the character-continuity ledger and map audit support it.
- A future story may use a named existing character as a current custodian only where their source biography supports that role. The ledger deliberately names roles rather than inventing wartime meetings.
- C03, C06, C07, C09, and C16 remain open. This document neither supplies a precursor name/founding date, Volkhari population, Founder roster, Lowmark farm-water governance, nor a final interpretation of "Long War." 
- A Chapel Shade, Banner Wraith, Earthen Echo, the General Who Wouldn't Fall, the Sorrowback, a Dreamer, or the Summit phenomenon can prompt a human investigation. None may identify the historical event, settle an account, or function as a speaking witness.
- Map labels and broad regional placement do not prove road order, channel route, crossings, or travel duration. Each selected event needs a later map/route plausibility review before canonization or content placement.

## Review result

This ledger gives every regional pair a distinct civilian history with local and cross-regional evidence owners. Its war rows retain independent human causes: administrative failure, triage, fear, damaged records, political protection, and coercion can each explain a discrepancy. The Remnant is reserved for evidence of its own precursor/formal operations. The next pass should test individual candidate records and people against the character ledger and the map audit, then decide which proposed chains deserve canon development.
