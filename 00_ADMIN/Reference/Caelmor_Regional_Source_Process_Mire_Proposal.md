# Caelmor Regional Source / Process Foundation — Mire of Fallen Banners

**Status:** proposal-stage connective lore for Creative Director review. It establishes ordinary source, labor, process, risk, stewardship, scarcity, and dependency without approving resources, items, recipes, nodes, tiers, schemas, exact routes, or production volumes, and it does not amend live canon.

## Authority and limits

This proposal uses the reconciled Mire dossier, Timeline, War History, Factions, Parts 2–6, and the canon-reconciliation audit.

- The Great War lasted 5–10 years and ended about thirty years ago. Concentrated battlefield trauma, rather than extended warfare across centuries, explains the Mire's present war-memory burden.
- The Chapel of the Sinking Star, Sunken Chapel, and Sunken Church are one place. Rowan Mirewake is the only known living guide who reliably knows its safe approach.
- The General Who Wouldn't Fall and the Sorrowback are exactly two major supernatural threats, and neither is undead. Minor impressions are not souls, witnesses, workers, sources, or harvesting nodes.
- This brief does not resolve C03, C06, C07, hearing or appeal authority, exact routes, or hydrology.

## Regional proposition

The Mire's material culture rests on wet-season household resilience, route care, cautious use of reeds and disturbed ground, repair, and deliberate import dependence. Its communities do not need a hidden local industry for every need. Imported metal, cloth, leather, durable timber, and some food make sense because waterlogged ground, unsafe remains, scattered settlement, and memorial duties make intensive extraction costly or unacceptable.

## Foundation 1 — everyday food and preservation

| Required field | Proposed foundation |
|---|---|
| World source or import dependency | Root crops, herbs, seasonal gathering, peat work, and broad hunting or fishing needs already support mixed household subsistence. Imported Lowmark grain or other staples provide resilience. No ingredient list, crop cycle, herd, catch, or recipe is selected. |
| Who gathers or imports it | Work-group households, Marshfall gatherers and peat workers, Rootrest herbalists, ferriers, carriers, and visiting traders hold separate parts of the chain. Chapel-Tenders can organize communal or mourning meals without controlling all food. |
| Who processes it | Households, hosts, care sites, ferriers' stations, and Chapel-Tenders sort, dry, smoke, salt, ferment, cook, or store ordinary food in broad terms. Later design must select actual foods and methods. |
| Broad processing method | Seasonal gathering, cleaning, trimming, drying, smoking, salting, fermenting, elevated storage, rotation, and shared cooking establish a practical range without defining recipes. |
| Fuel and water needs | Peat is an existing fuel context for domestic-scale preservation and heat. Water is abundant but not automatically clean; raised storage, drainage, and safe washing matter more than volume. |
| Waste and risk | Damp, rot, mold, smoke, spoiled stores, contaminated water, vermin, unstable routes, sudden isolation, and disturbed ground create loss. No supernatural manifestation identifies safe food or honest custody. |
| Stewardship and access | Households share seasonal work; ferriers can refuse unsafe passage; wardens and local guides close dangerous ground; care and memorial obligations influence distribution. Exact hearing authority remains open. |
| Scarcity reason | Wet seasons, unsafe access, labor shortages, spoilage, dry-storage limits, route closure, and delayed imports produce scarcity. Hunger can result from transport failure without implying barren land. |
| Regional cultural meaning | Preserving enough to receive a stranded traveler or feed a mourning table expresses care. Waste and hoarding carry moral weight in communities that remember displaced and wounded people. |
| Broad cross-region dependency | Lowmark is the clearest broad staple and household exchange partner. Thornfell and Emberholt ties may carry travelers, preserved provisions, wages, or care goods, without exact cargos or paths. |

## Foundation 2 — textiles and clothing

| Required field | Proposed foundation |
|---|---|
| World source or import dependency | Reeds support bounded weaving, tying, screens, writing strips, and basic construction. Most durable garment cloth remains imported or recovered through lawful household custody. Recovered banner cloth is finite memorial evidence, not ordinary textile stock. |
| Who gathers or imports it | Reed cutters, work-group households, ferriers, carriers, traders, and returnees bring local fiber, cloth, or garments into use. Banner custodians retain separate authority over recovered war cloth. |
| Who processes it | Households and repair workers dry and prepare reeds, bind and weave utility forms, mend garments, patch wet-weather clothing, and retire damaged cloth. Specialized cloth production may remain outside the region. |
| Broad processing method | Cutting, sorting, drying, splitting, twisting or weaving reeds, laundering, drying, patching, stitching, waterproofing only in a broad later-selected sense, and repeated repair form the foundation. |
| Fuel and water needs | Drying requires raised, ventilated space and modest heat. Washing needs clean water and careful drainage; persistent damp increases fuel and labor costs. |
| Waste and risk | Rot, mold, insects, contaminated wash water, fire in dry reed stores, weak recovered cloth, and false claims over banners or belongings are ordinary hazards. |
| Stewardship and access | Reed work follows local access and route-care needs; memorial cloth remains under custodial rules; households decide repair and inheritance. No office is granted a universal textile authority. |
| Scarcity reason | Drying weather, sound storage, labor, imported cloth arrivals, route access, and the protected status of war cloth constrain supply. |
| Regional cultural meaning | Patching and careful drying express refusal to discard what still serves. Memorial textiles preserve names, uncertainty, and care; they do not assign allegiance or speak for the dead. |
| Broad cross-region dependency | Lowmark and Emberholt can broadly supply cloth or garment work; Thornfell may exchange weather-repair knowledge. No region is fixed as an exclusive source. |

## Foundation 3 — hides and leather

| Required field | Proposed foundation |
|---|---|
| World source or import dependency | The Mire has no approved local hide or tanning chain. Ordinary leather is therefore an explicit import dependency unless a later pass selects husbandry, lawful hunting, tanning inputs, labor, water controls, and waste sites. |
| Who gathers or imports it | Outside animal keepers, hunters, processors, carriers, and traders provide finished or partly finished leather. Mire households, ferriers, wardens, and repair workers receive it. |
| Who processes it | Local work is limited to cleaning, drying, conditioning with later-approved ordinary means, patching, stitching, and replacement of worn parts. No local tannery, hide harvest, or specialist monopoly is asserted. |
| Broad processing method | Inspection, cleaning, drying, softening or dressing in broad terms, patching, stitching, and careful storage support maintenance only. Primary curing and tanning remain outside the region. |
| Fuel and water needs | Maintenance needs small amounts of clean water and dry warmth. Persistent damp makes drying space more important and shortens the life of neglected leather. |
| Waste and risk | Mold, rot, contaminated water, failed straps or coverings, and fraudulent sale of poorly cured imports threaten users. The absence of a local tannery avoids inventing industrial effluent. |
| Stewardship and access | Households and public-service workers prioritize maintenance; importers remain answerable for condition and provenance through ordinary local trust or later-selected procedure. Exact appeal authority remains open. |
| Scarcity reason | Import interruption, damp damage, limited dry storage, repair skill, and replacement delay make leather valuable. Scarcity does not justify harvesting supernatural creatures or disturbed war ground. |
| Regional cultural meaning | Maintained leather shows respect for scarce outside labor and for the route or care duty it enables. Visible repair is ordinary competence, not stigma. |
| Broad cross-region dependency | Thornfell and Lowmark offer broad hide and leather foundations; Emberholt can provide finished work. No direct supply line, quality hierarchy, or exclusive origin is chosen. |

## Foundation 4 — timber and reed craft

| Required field | Proposed foundation |
|---|---|
| World source or import dependency | Reeds, roots, twisted or reclaimed small wood, and limited safe local cutting support markers, screens, bindings, moorings, and minor repairs. Durable structural timber for raised buildings, ferries, bridges, or major works remains largely imported. No local forest or species is approved. |
| Who gathers or imports it | Reed cutters, route crews, ferriers, Rootcrafters, households, carriers, and traders gather, reclaim, or import stock. Wardens and guides identify ground that should not be disturbed. |
| Who processes it | Household work groups, marker setters, ferriers, route crews, and Rootcrafters sort, dry, split, twist, bind, shape, join, patch, and replace ordinary components. |
| Broad processing method | Seasonal cutting, drying above damp ground, splitting, bundling, weaving, pegging or joining in broad terms, seasoning imported timber, repair, and safe reclamation establish the chain. |
| Fuel and water needs | Drying and preservation need airflow, raised storage, and modest peat heat. Water enables movement but also causes rot; it is an environmental cost rather than free industrial power. |
| Waste and risk | Overcut banks, lost root stability, rot, fire in dry reeds, failed moorings, waterlogged timber, disturbed remains, and route obstruction threaten communities. |
| Stewardship and access | Ferriers, Marshwardens, route crews, guides, and households can refuse cutting or recovery that threatens passage, memorial ground, or stable banks. This is practical custody, not settled constitutional law. |
| Scarcity reason | Sound dry structural stock, storage, seasonal access, decay, route closures, and competing repairs create scarcity. Local reed abundance does not replace structural timber. |
| Regional cultural meaning | A maintained marker or landing is a promise to the next traveler. Materials used around memorial ground are chosen for care and reversibility, not spectacle. |
| Broad cross-region dependency | Lowmark's river and woodland foundation and Thornfell's structural and bridge knowledge can support imported timber or repair exchange. Exact hydrology and route geometry remain open. |

## Foundation 5 — metal tools and limited local work

| Required field | Proposed foundation |
|---|---|
| World source or import dependency | The Mire has no approved ore or primary smithing chain. Metal tools and ordinary stock are imported; sound civilian pieces are maintained and reused. Battlefield metal remains finite evidence, hazard, or memorial material rather than routine feedstock. |
| Who gathers or imports it | Traders, carriers, ferriers, households, route crews, wardens, and care institutions receive imported tools or stock from broad Lowmark, Thornfell, or Emberholt exchange. |
| Who processes it | Local tool keepers perform cleaning, rust control, haft replacement, sharpening, fitting, and minor cold repair where ordinary skill permits. Work requiring a forge returns to an outside or later-approved visiting smith. |
| Broad processing method | Inspection, cleaning, drying, oiling only if a later ordinary preparation is selected, sharpening, fitting, handle replacement, and documented return for substantial repair define the chain. No smelting, alloying, or local forge is asserted. |
| Fuel and water needs | Maintenance uses little heat and carefully controlled clean water. Avoiding primary smithing reflects absent ore, dry fuel, stable works, and waste handling rather than lack of intelligence or skill. |
| Waste and risk | Rust, failed edges, contaminated water, sharp fragments, counterfeit provenance, and accidental disturbance of war remains are the main risks. Resonance or earth-memory is a reason to stop work. |
| Stewardship and access | Tool owners, work groups, wardens, Memorykeepers, and memorial custodians distinguish ordinary property from dangerous or evidentiary remains. No manifestation can make that decision for them. |
| Scarcity reason | Import delay, wet corrosion, limited repair access, finite stock, and restrictions on battlefield recovery keep metal tools scarce and carefully maintained. |
| Regional cultural meaning | A maintained tool represents continued work without treating the battlefield as a mine. Refusing useful-looking war metal can be an act of witness and practical safety. |
| Broad cross-region dependency | Lowmark provides markets and repair exchange; Thornfell and Emberholt provide broad smithing precedent. The Mire may provide care, route knowledge, testimony, and labor in reciprocal relationships. |

## Import dependence as a regional strength and constraint

The Mire can remain materially coherent while importing much of its grain, garment cloth, leather, structural timber, and metal work. That dependence creates ordinary stories of delayed ferries, shared storage, repaired goods, household debt, fair dealing, and political neglect. It does not require one merchant monopoly or exact road.

Local strengths are seasonal knowledge, reed work, peat use at domestic scale, preservation under damp conditions, route maintenance, repair, and custody. These make Mire communities active participants in exchange rather than passive consumers.

## Supernatural and memorial exclusions

1. Neither major threat is a source, worker, witness, or repeatable production opportunity.
2. Earthen Echoes, Banner Wraiths, Chapel Shades, and other minor impressions cannot identify provenance or ownership.
3. Recovered banners, weapons, wagons, and armor remain finite, politically charged, and evidentiary.
4. The single chapel's shifting approach does not establish a supply route or private extraction right.
5. The world can remember without the dead walking or authorizing material use.

## What this brief deliberately leaves open

- exact foods, catches, crops, fibers, cloth, hides, leather treatments, woods, fuels, tools, metals, and finished goods;
- local husbandry, a tannery, primary smithing, industrial kilns, or managed forestry unless separately justified;
- exact sources, routes, hydrology, volumes, prices, quotas, office borders, and hearing or appeal rules;
- C03, C06, C07, and all supernatural extraction;
- all equipment, recipe, gathering, progression, and schema work.

## Foundation result

The Mire now has proposal-level foundations for food resilience, reed and clothing work, imported leather, bounded timber craft, imported metal tools, maintenance, and realistic external dependence. The chains preserve memorial custody and the non-undead rules. This is sufficient for a later readiness rerun, not a catalog or live-canon amendment.
