# Caelmor Part 5 — Lowmark v1 main-arc architecture

**Status:** proposal-stage narrative architecture for Creative Director review. This is one Lowmark-only arc, not a quest script, dialogue draft, quest JSON, reward plan, map placement, or canon rewrite. Phase 1's three-act water-magic arc and 10–14-step envelope govern the structure. The detailed Lowmark dossier supplies five canonical framework anchors; this proposal combines selected functions from them into **one** arc rather than treating all five as launch main stories. Director rulings through C15 supersede stale source language.

## Arc thesis and controlled author truth

Lowmark has survived because people keep water, food, crossings, and memory moving without demanding that one person control them all. The main crisis begins when several ordinary failures—deferred bank work, altered closure markers, political pressure on grain movement, and damaged postwar waterworks—interact with genuine but bounded water resonance. Places saturated by living grief and intention hold disturbed imprints in their flow. These effects are dangerous and meaningful, but they do not speak, identify the dead, expose a culprit, or provide a perfect record.

**Proposed author truth for Director selection:** a present coalition of local profiteers and frightened officials has reopened or bypassed old emergency channels to keep trade moving and conceal unfair allocations. They did not create Lowmark's deeper water wounds. Their changes concentrate existing resonance and debris at Stillwater Fen, making Wraith-like manifestations and trance events worse. The Last Field-Mage understands combat-scale water practice and enough Riverwarden restraint to help stop escalation, but cannot solve civic injustice or restore the past. The Riverwardens withdrew generations earlier because rulers repeatedly tried to turn care for water into command over people. Their current survival is revealed late as a guarded community, not a source of player power.

This truth is a proposal rather than canon. It can be revised without losing the architecture so long as the crisis retains three causes: **a current human decision**, **older damaged infrastructure**, and **bounded resonance shaped by living/emotional imprint**. Making the Remnant, a speaking river, or one evil mage the sole cause would break the arc.

## Three acts and twelve architectural movements

The word “movement” marks a narrative function within Phase 1's 10–14-step envelope. It does not prescribe a quest task, combat encounter, dialogue sequence, reward, or exact POI route.

### Act I — Arrival, work, and a river problem people can name

#### 1. A crossing that cannot simply stay open

The player arrives amid a practical disruption: a Brookhollow or Crestbridge movement of food and households is delayed after unsafe water behavior and a damaged bank marker. Maelin Rowe's judgment and a Crestbridge keeper's paperwork disagree about what “open” means. Participation teaches that Lowmark authority is split among people who do necessary work. The immediate movement ends with people and stores made safe, not an anomaly explained.

**Initial belief:** Maelin is obstructive, a clerk is heartless, or an ordinary river hazard is being dramatized. **Evidence:** present water height, an altered marker, crew accounts, and a record of pressure to move the load. **Truth withheld:** why several sites are worsening together.

#### 2. The cost of moving grain

At Crestbridge, Aldric Fenmere's current authority, Corvin Tath's duplicate tallies, village claims, and keeper enforcement reveal that a delayed load changes who goes without. The player helps establish a transparent present account. An inconsistency indicates that someone has used emergency language or bypassed a closure, but the missing/burned historical record prevents a convenient full answer.

**Arc function:** teaches political pressure through allocation rather than exposition. **Local result:** a current shipment or household claim receives an accountable decision.

#### 3. Three sites, three plausible explanations

The player observes related symptoms at a worked river site, an Ashen Flats or orchard edge, and a fen approach: wrong-flow eddies, sound or reflection disturbance, animals avoiding water, or people losing time in grief-heavy places. Wraithwatchers and workers offer different explanations. Physical checks show some ordinary causes; one bounded anomaly survives them.

**Arc function:** establishes the evidence rule. Magic is real, while investigation still requires people, records, and places.

#### 4. A harm someone is using

The player identifies a present abuse: changed markers, a reopened emergency channel, manipulated warnings, or staged fear that benefits a local trading or land interest. The responsible group remains role-based until the Director selects its social placement. Stopping the immediate abuse protects people but does not calm every water wound.

**Act I turn:** a human wrong and a supernatural condition coexist. Exposing one does not make the other fictional.

### Act II — Old duties, incomplete records, and restraint

#### 5. The guide-line under working land

Joric Fen, Annelise Reed, or local workers help interpret an uncovered wartime guide-line or treatment boundary. Its public reading conflicts with a household or memorial account. The site shows that the present channel pattern passes through land altered for emergency care and supply. No imprint identifies a patient or soldier.

**Arc function:** the war becomes a lived land-use inheritance. **Local result:** work and remembrance are protected through a bounded decision rather than freezing the entire area.

#### 6. Tovin's pattern, ordinary before magical

Tovin Pell's sketches record a repair sequence or water behavior others dismissed as anxiety. Workers first fix the demonstrable structural hazard. When the same sequence appears near another disturbed site, Tovin becomes a credible observer without becoming a mage, chosen witness, or main-plot savant.

**Changed belief:** what looked like personal unreliability is careful attention; what looks like one supernatural pattern partly reflects ordinary postwar alterations.

#### 7. The argument about practice

Farm workers, a Flow-Mender, a Lensemaster or theorist, and local authorities disagree over whether small water practices are relevant, safe, permitted, or merely skilled labor. This movement must work under all open C09 models: household transmission, commons stewardship, or charter oversight. A bounded farm intervention prevents a local failure but cannot reverse the wider crisis.

**C12 correction:** postwar authorities opposed weaponization, amplification, uncontrolled ritual experimentation, and dangerous research. Lowmark's actual local rules and fear are fragmented; “magic was outlawed” is an oversimplified later account, not the legal premise of v1.

#### 8. The Field-Mage as a limit, not an answer

Evidence of precise restraint and a quiet intervention brings the player into contact with the Last Field-Mage. The Field-Mage knows combat-scale water practice, recognizes dangerous concentration, and can demonstrate why force would spread or deepen the problem. They do not know every name embedded in the crisis, cannot settle the grain records, and refuse to turn the player into a weapon.

**Test of worth:** the player has listened to workers, corrected a present harm, and distinguished observation from interpretation. Access is earned through judgment, not by collecting magical objects or defeating enough manifestations.

#### 9. The older channel and the reason for withdrawal

The player reaches evidence of Riverwarden work—possibly a bounded outer place or mediated meeting before any deeper enclave access. It shows an older system designed to distribute pressure and let communities tend water together. Later authorities narrowed or repurposed parts of it. Riverwarden history explains a duty, not a lost superweapon. The First Listener tradition is presented as later teaching memory; it does not resolve C07's Founder roster or identify the first rediscoverer.

**Act II turn:** the current emergency can be stabilized only by combining ordinary repair, civic cooperation, memorial restraint, and limited expert water practice.

### Act III — Stillwater, accountable repair, and a complete Lowmark ending

#### 10. A public plan under unequal cost

At Crestbridge or a multi-settlement hearing, institutions must choose who closes routes, moves food, tends memorial ground, and accepts delay while the Fen is stabilized. Evidence exposes the present profiteering or concealment selected in movement 4. The plan gives named roles to existing institutions without creating a new permanent regional government. Side-story completion can alter trust or testimony but is not required.

**Arc function:** converts gathered knowledge into an accountable collective decision. The Field-Mage advises on risk and has no authority to rule Lowmark.

#### 11. Stillwater's vigil

At Stillwater Fen, the crisis peaks through dangerous currents, trance-like grief responses, frightened people, and Wraith-like manifestations. The player protects people, applies learned environmental distinctions, and enables workers and the Field-Mage to redistribute pressure. The resolution may emphasize stricter closure, carefully tended remembrance spaces, or a bounded combination; no option “kills grief,” preserves perfect memories, or releases literal souls.

**Evidence rule:** magical effects reveal where pressure gathers and how intervention changes it. Human records and testimony remain the only basis for names, blame, and civic claims.

#### 12. The river remains, and Lowmark chooses who tends it

The v1 ending restores safe movement at the immediate crisis sites, ends the current exploitation, establishes a reviewable local stewardship arrangement, and gives affected households a truthful account of what is known and unknown. The Last Field-Mage remains a rare practitioner rather than a trainer or ruler. Riverwarden contact, if selected, ends in mutual limits: Lowmark receives guidance or recognition, while the enclave does not resume control of the region.

The player is recognized for attention, work, and judgment. Phase 1's “symbolic magical artifact” cannot be specified here because this task forbids reward/item design; the narrative function is instead a non-mechanical sign of trust or acknowledged responsibility, subject to a later approved design pass.

**Complete local ending:** crossings reopen under accountable rules; grain and household needs are addressed; Stillwater is safer; the present human wrong is answered; Lowmark has a way to tend future water wounds. **Future questions:** who carried particular outside records, what other regions remember about shared relief and repair, the formal Remnant's later archive shaping, and the deeper history of elemental rediscovery. None is needed to understand or value the v1 ending.

## Recurring-character use within the main arc

- **Maelin Rowe:** introduces safety judgment and may return when public pressure threatens a crossing. She knows her own routes and work; she does not know the cause of every anomaly or an invented interregional journey.
- **Corvin Tath:** makes incomplete records materially consequential. He knows current flow and duplicate tallies; the burned ledger remains unknowable unless another proven copy exists.
- **Aldric Fenmere:** embodies useful authority with inherited blind spots. He can open archives and convene action but has no assigned wartime service or total knowledge.
- **Joric Fen and Annelise Reed:** keep land and remembrance human. Neither becomes an exposition device or receives supernatural confirmation.
- **Tovin Pell:** changes from dismissed observer to trusted worker. He neither becomes a mage nor supplies the final answer.
- **Lessa Mirepath:** sets the boundary between route knowledge and mystical projection. Her fen decisions remain practical and do not imply Mire kinship.
- **Last Field-Mage:** appears after the player has demonstrated evidence discipline. Their payoff is a cooperative, bounded intervention and a continuing refusal of power fantasy.

No one character needs to appear in every movement. The main arc should share narrative space with role-based workers, households, keepers, Wraithwatchers, and Flow-Menders so Lowmark feels inhabited rather than arranged around a small cast.

## Scope and completion controls

- One main arc, twelve architectural movements, three Phase 1 acts.
- Lowmark is the only full v1 region; every movement and local ending occurs there.
- Future-region evidence is optional and cannot be necessary for the resolution.
- Combat or danger may protect people and enable repair; it cannot extract historical truth.
- No player spellcasting progression, magic school, gear, item, resource, recipe, or reward is designed.
- No quest script, dialogue, state machine, JSON, exact route, POI order, or implementation requirement is specified.
- C03, C06, C07, C09, and C16 remain narrower future decisions. C09 affects the presentation of movement 7, not whether the arc works.

**Proposed v1 result:** the player understands Lowmark as a society of distributed practical authority, discovers that magic is real but constrained by responsibility and evidence, helps stop one present exploitation and one dangerous water crisis, and leaves the Vale with meaningful trust rather than new combat power. That is a complete Lowmark story even if no callback ever ships.
