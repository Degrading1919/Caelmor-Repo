# World architectural languages — proposal for the generator

**Purpose:** Push the existing four-region gallery toward unmistakably different structural silhouettes and construction logic. This is an art-direction and tool-design proposal, not a canon change, historical claim about Caelmor, asset specification, or approved material catalog.

## Authority and provenance

Phase 1 establishes an ancient, weathered, grounded world whose regions are culturally rooted, readable through space, and handcrafted; meaningful landmarks and environmental storytelling should carry place identity ([Phase 1.1, §§1, 3, 8](../../../01_PRE-PRODUCTION/1.1%20Core%20Vision/Caelmor_Phase1_1_Core_Vision.md); [Phase 1.2, Pillar C and implications](../../../01_PRE-PRODUCTION/1.2%20Pillars%20%26%20Tenets/Caelmor_Phase1_2_Pillars_and_Tenets.md)). The four region dossiers anchor places, work, terrain, and settlement forms cited below. The generator's existing [regional grammar](REGIONAL_GRAMMAR.md) already labels many material, roof, and palette choices as reversible visual interpretation.

These distinctions matter for status: the live dossiers are the regional lore source, but their resource lists explicitly remain legacy concepts, not an approved material or craft catalog (the regional dossiers, “Unique Resources”). The cross-region source/process synthesis is explicitly proposal-stage and does not approve materials, exact routes, or catalogs ([Source / Process Dependency Proposal, status and “Readiness consequence”](../../../00_ADMIN/Reference/Caelmor_Cross_Region_Source_Process_Dependency_Proposal.md)). The historical gap audit says material-culture readiness was out of scope and does not claim Lore Foundation Lock ([Part 2 Historical Gap Audit, §§“Material culture as history” and “Why the second pass stopped here”](../../../00_ADMIN/Reference/Caelmor_Part2_Historical_Gap_Audit.md)). Therefore, “stone,” “timber,” “iron,” and similar words below describe visual construction roles or plausible conventional materials, not new world-resource commitments.

The following are **proposed art inferences**, except Emberholt's angular, reinforced towers and elevated metalwalks, which implement the user's explicit art direction. No rule makes a supernatural effect a building material, construction process, or source of historical knowledge. Do not imply that hidden ancient practices are common modern civic knowledge.

## Regional grammar

### Lowmark Vale — low, broad, expandable working buildings

Canon anchors: fertile plains, river trade, mills, grain supply, communal rebuilding, warehouses and drying racks, a fortified bridge keep, and modest workshops ([Lowmark dossier, §§I–V](../../../LORE/Regions/Caelmor_Region1_LowmarkVale.md)). Ordinary household and community water practice is acknowledged; Riverwarden architecture is hidden and must not become the default Lowmark streetscape ([§§III, VI–VII]).

1. **Keep everyday massing close to the ground and horizontally legible.** Rural homes and sheds read as compact cores with low work wings; market buildings widen along a street or river edge. Reserve vertical emphasis for a mill, tower, bridge gate, or civic landmark, not the ordinary house.
2. **Show a working frame and practical openings.** Use a structural bay rhythm that can accept a porch, lean-to, loading door, drying loft, or later repair. Let storehouses and barns have broad access and deep eaves; dwellings stay more closed and domestic. Timber-frame with infill is a visual inference, not a locked regional building method.
3. **Make the farm/trade cluster part of the silhouette.** Pair dwelling, store, work shed, yard, drying racks, and river access in readable small compounds; larger Brookhollow/Crestbridge groups can tighten around market lanes, docks, or the crossing. Avoid an isolated “fantasy cottage” as the sole rural type.
4. **Treat the bridge and Crest Hall as exceptional infrastructure.** The bridge is a long horizontal stone span and Crest Hall a compact, defended administrative mass overlooking approach routes; their stone weight should contrast with lighter settlement fabric. Dwarven reinforcement is dossier canon for the bridge, not a warrant to make every Lowmark building masonry.

**Function / wealth / age / context:** Wealth should buy more bays, a larger yard, durable repair, and better-finished public fronts before it buys height. Age should accumulate attached sheds, replaced infill, patched roof planes, and reused structural members in legible phases. Brookhollow and Crestbridge densify around exchange and defense; Ashenstead shows rebuilt plots and memorial gathering space; Greyharrow can carry a modestly fortified core; rural holdings stay dispersed. [Historic England on timber framing](https://historicengland.org.uk/images-books/publications/eehb-insulating-timber-framed-walls/heag071-insultating-timber-framed-walls/) notes frame, infill, local material, status, and multi-period adaptation as useful observable variables; its [farmstead guidance](https://historicengland.org.uk/advice/caring-for-heritage/rural-heritage/farm-buildings/) supports reading farm buildings with settlement and landscape, not as isolated façades.

### Thornfell Marches — stepped, braced, cliff-attached construction

Canon anchors: steep ridges, narrow passes, watchposts, rope bridges, hewn-stone works, carved cliff stairs, fluted wind-channel faces, cliffside Stonewake terraces, ridge-shelf longhouse clusters, and a pass outpost with palisade and reinforced gates ([Thornfell dossier, §§I–III, V–VII](../../../LORE/Regions/Caelmor_Region2_ThornfellMarches.md)). Echohewn halls use engineered channels, but most acoustics are architectural and rare resonance only belongs at specified sites (§§I, VII–VIII).

1. **Attach buildings to contour, rather than placing freestanding boxes on a flat base.** Use split levels, retaining walls, stepped footprints, stair landings, and terraces that follow ridge shelves. Let the terrain itself interrupt and support massing.
2. **Bias toward compact, narrow, sheltered volumes.** Deep wall returns, small recessed openings, low cross-gables or simple roof masses, and a protected entry read as weather response. Keep big openings on sheltered courtyards; exposed elevations stay closed and strongly framed.
3. **Expose structural work where it carries real loads.** Retaining buttresses, lintels, cross-bracing, stair supports, and bridges should visibly meet the ground or cliff. Rope/chain bridges and cliff ladders are connective infrastructure with anchor towers and landings, not decorative trim.
4. **Make civic and defensive landmarks read by position.** Watchposts and ridge forts occupy skyline shoulders; Stonewake's assembly hall sits within the carved terrace system; Highstead longhouses form shared clusters on protected shelves; Passwatch uses a gate/palisade choke point.

**Function / wealth / age / context:** Wealth buys more terrace frontage, a dry sheltered court, and carefully cut stonework, not a larger unsupported cantilever. Age and exposure should show repaired retaining faces, roof replacement, weather-cut edges, and bracing added after slips. Defensive function thickens walls and narrows gates; communal Highstead housing repeats attached bays; Stonewake stacks civic, market, and stoneworker terraces. Use rubble or local stone as an inference, not a specified quarry or supply. Historic Environment Scotland documents one rural Highland example with rubble stone, narrow plan, recessed openings, and local material; use it as structural reference only, not a design to reproduce: [Chamberbane listing](https://portal.historicenvironment.scot/apex/f?p=1505:300:::NO:RP,300:P300_PAGE_ID,P300_PLACE_ID:102449,LB6004).

### Mire of Fallen Banners — raised floors, light links, wet-ground repair

Canon anchors: low bog, peat, reeds, sinking wartime structures, homes on stilts or raised banks, elevated Sorrowmere walkways, and flood-resistant longhouses ([Mire dossier, §§I–III, VI–VIII](../../../LORE/Regions/Caelmor_Region3_MireOfFallenBanners.md)). Modern Memorykeepers and Chapel-Tenders are nonmagical; architecture should not imply they understand Memorywarden techniques (§§III–V).

1. **Make the occupied floor line visibly higher than the ground.** Use stilt posts, raised banks, or mixed raised foundations with open or serviceable space below; keep the wet ground, changing waterline, and occasional flood debris readable. Height varies by site rather than becoming uniform stilts everywhere.
2. **Connect settlement with walkways and landings.** Paths bridge wet gaps between houses, work areas, and public places; use steps, ramps, short gangways, or mooring edges where channels meet the settlement. Buildings should face these routes and water approaches.
3. **Build slender, modular upper volumes.** Narrow longhouse plans, light wall panels, visible post-and-beam frames, and replaceable roof/wall portions suggest repair under scarcity. Store heavy goods above ground or on raised platforms; avoid massive ground-bearing masonry as the default.
4. **Let accumulated repair and memory shape public places.** Memorial gardens, banner lines, lantern paths, and communal meeting decks organize larger settlements. Older or sunken wartime structures belong as partially submerged ruins, not as the ordinary current house foundation.

**Function / wealth / age / context:** Wealth should appear in steadier, more numerous supports, a wider deck, safer connections, and better-maintained joinery; scarcity appears in reused boards, added braces, small annexes, and uneven path repairs. Stilt height and support pattern respond to local water depth and soil, while houses on raised banks remain valid where terrain allows. Sorrowmere can have the densest linked walkways and public platforms; Marshfall sits at a wetland/forest edge; Rootrest clusters around surviving roots; Griefwatch uses a defensive perch. The [UNESCO tentative-list account of Colombian stilt settlements](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5842/) describes driven wooden piles, raised floors/walls, platforms, docks, and settlement access by water; [SEPA's elevated-building guidance](https://www.sepa.org.uk/environment/land/planning/guidance-and-advice-notes/position-statement-elevated-buildings-in-areas-of-flood-risk/) explains the functional logic of leaving the ground below open to flood flow. These are design principles, not cultural analogues to copy.

### Emberholt Heights — monumental angular heatworks and civic industry

Canon anchors: volcanic stone terraces, a city of forges, heat vents, emberwork, industrial districts, Ash Terraces, Emberforges, glassed and sealed subterranean works, obsidian-black gates, and a civic training regime ([Emberholt dossier, §§I–VII](../../../LORE/Regions/Caelmor_Region4_EmberholtHeights.md)). The user's authorized art direction adds angular tower silhouettes, heavy reinforcement, elevated metalwalks, a less sinister industrial city, and brutalist-inspired massing. Treat “brutalist-inspired” as a visual cue, not a literal historical style or canon period.

1. **Compose districts as large interlocking solids on volcanic terraces.** Use angular stepped platforms, retaining masses, deep cuts, and strong vertical/horizontal blocks. Public plazas and production courts are planned voids in the mass, not leftover gaps.
2. **Give the skyline a functional industrial hierarchy.** Towers, stacks, vent shafts, lift-like frames, and furnace halls rise above lower workshops and civic blocks. Each tall element should connect to a legible heat, material, access, or civic function; avoid a row of decorative spires.
3. **Exaggerate structure at high-load and heat-facing points.** Deep piers, buttresses, braced frames, protected openings, thick base walls, and reinforced bridges make the mass feel engineered for heat and industrial use. Elevated metalwalks link work levels and towers, with visible supports and landing points.
4. **Separate civic face, production floor, and restricted depth through massing.** Ash Terraces and gates can be broad, legible, and ceremonial; forge yards expose process; sealed or hazardous below-grade works become discontinuities, blocked routes, altered masonry, and repair scars. This supports the story without making the entire city sinister.

**Function / wealth / age / context:** Wealth and civic importance increase span, terrace scale, access control, and finish quality; ordinary homes and shops should still belong to the city through repeated masonry modules and service lanes. Industrial buildings grow by attached bays, platforms, flues, and bridges; older structures can be adapted around newer work, while sealed disaster zones show a distinct interruption and restricted access. Emberhold emphasizes monumental civic massing; Emberforges cluster towers, stacks, furnace halls, yards, and elevated circulation; Cinderweave should be denser and more mixed-use; Ashspire is the wealthy ward described in dossier §VII.4. Use ordinary masonry and metal as visual languages, not claims that ember magic supplies material. UNESCO's [Völklingen Ironworks record](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/687/) describes blast-furnace frames, platforms, coal tower, conveyors, gas plant, and process equipment dominating a townscape; Historic England's [steel industry overview](https://historicengland.org.uk/research/results/reports/7802/TheSteelIndustryinEnglandAnHistoricalOverview) describes large utilitarian forms, few openings, functional ventilation, substantial foundations/platforms, and process-led layout. These support industrial hierarchy and structure; they do not dictate Caelmor's architecture.

## Shared variation rules

### Foundation implemented in 0.4

These are original authoring interpretations, rather than structural or climate
simulation. Lowmark uses broad halls with open shed-roof working ranges and
0.28m infill walls. Thornfell uses stepped foundations, 0.50m masonry, recessed
glazing, bearing stepped stone gables and weather copings. The stepped gable
treatment is an art interpretation, not a locked regional fact. Mire uses 0.18m
board construction on braced piers, a piecewise curved reed roof with curved ribs,
and supported links, cargo decks, gate landings and broad boat slipways. Emberholt
uses 0.70m masonry, recessed glazing, draining terrace roofs, parapets, scuppers,
forge ranges, hollow shafts and ironwalks connecting actual upper doors.

Each occupied mass has its own weather envelope and blind attachment faces.
Folded aprons join lower roofs to taller party walls. Lowmark mill wheels and
raceways, warehouse gantries, sistered repair posts, and the other functional
parts are generated from original mesh primitives. No hidden ancient techniques,
new resources, modern machines or narrative facts are implied by this geometry.

The 0.5 [household refinement](ARTISTIC_REFINEMENT.md) supersedes the stepped
gable treatment for ordinary Thornfell homes and work premises with calm
continuous roof verges. Towers are justified by public/defensive use rather
than wealth or a settlement label alone. Household investment now buys usable
work/store/private-room arrangements across existing domestic families; the Mire
adds sheltered working decks and screened private links; Emberholt uses narrow
room bodies, lower work ranges, enclosed domestic openings, real hearth flues,
forged sheet roofing and ground-bearing corbels/ties. These are additional
reversible art interpretations, not new canon or historical construction claims.

- **Make function change the silhouette.** A barn needs clear span and loading access; a home needs protected openings; a watchpost needs sightlines; a market needs doors onto a public route; a forge needs process volume, ventilation, and material movement. Apply regional grammar on top of that function.
- **Make wealth change capacity and workmanship.** Add useful bays, safer spans, better supports, a court, or more durable repairs before adding arbitrary ornament. Wealth is a generator control, not a canonical social taxonomy.
- **Make age a sequence of construction events.** Start from a coherent structure, then add a wing, replace infill or roof portions, reinforce a weak span, patch a walkway, or abandon part of a site. Tie visible wear to water, wind, heat, load, maintenance, and use. Keep old/new fabric legible.
- **Make settlement context change relationships and access.** Density, terrace position, route connection, waterfront edge, defensive choke, or work yard should alter plan and silhouette. A context is not merely a material palette.
- **Generate original geometry from rules.** Build supports, wall planes, terraces, spans, frames, towers, and connectors parametrically. Do not use prefab buildings, external models, copied landmark forms, or image-sourced geometry. Use heritage sources for load paths, settlement behavior, and process hierarchy only.
- **Keep distinctive silhouette decisions structural.** A region should remain recognizable as flat color or clay massing: Lowmark horizontal and clustered; Thornfell stepped and anchored; Mire elevated and linked; Emberholt angular, monumental, and process-tall.

## Reading list (primary heritage / official technical sources)

- Historic England, [Timber-framed Wall Construction](https://historicengland.org.uk/images-books/publications/eehb-insulating-timber-framed-walls/heag071-insultating-timber-framed-walls/): frame/infill relationships, regional variation, repair, and adaptation.
- Historic England, [Farm Buildings and Traditional Farmsteads](https://historicengland.org.uk/advice/caring-for-heritage/rural-heritage/farm-buildings/): farm-building character and relationship with landscape and settlement.
- Historic Environment Scotland, [Chamberbane cruck-framed cottage listing](https://portal.historicenvironment.scot/apex/f?p=1505:300:::NO:RP,300:P300_PAGE_ID,P300_PLACE_ID:102449,LB6004): Highland rural massing and openings.
- UNESCO World Heritage Centre, [Cultural Landscape of the Vernacular Stilt Housing of Cienaga Grande de Santa Marta and of Medio Atrato](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5842/): raised-floor and water-connected settlement logic.
- Scottish Environment Protection Agency, [Elevated buildings in areas of flood risk](https://www.sepa.org.uk/environment/land/planning/guidance-and-advice-notes/position-statement-elevated-buildings-in-areas-of-flood-risk/): open ground beneath elevated buildings and flood flow.
- UNESCO World Heritage Centre, [Völklingen Ironworks](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/687/): industrial process hierarchy, platforms, frames, and town-scale silhouette.
- Historic England, [The Steel Industry in England: An Historical Overview](https://historicengland.org.uk/research/results/reports/7802/TheSteelIndustryinEnglandAnHistoricalOverview): utilitarian massing, ventilation, platforms, and process-led site layout.
